Judges on the ballot

Arkansas holds its nonpartisan judicial elections in March. On the November 3, 2026 ballot the only judge contests are three circuit judge runoffs. No Supreme Court or Court of Appeals seat is on the ballot.

Checked on November 4, 2026. Arkansas holds its judicial elections in March, and judges are nonpartisan. The only judicial contests on the Nov. 3 ballot are runoffs between the top two March finishers, where no one won a majority. Our sources are an Arkansas Times roundup and our Arkansas guide race pages, which cite the Secretary of State. We could not open a Secretary of State runoff list, so the three runoffs below are unreviewed against it.

Supreme Court and Court of Appeals

No Supreme Court or Court of Appeals seat is on the Nov. 3 ballot. Both were decided in March.

  • Supreme Court, Position 3
    Nicholas Bronni source: Arkansas Times (unreviewed)    Won the March 3 election over John Adams, 55% to 45%. No runoff.
  • Court of Appeals, District 3, Position 1
    Sarah Capp source: Arkansas Times (unreviewed)    Won the March 3 election over Brandon Carter with nearly 63%. No runoff.

County, district and local judges

What this covers. The three circuit judge runoffs on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot, as reported by the Arkansas Times after the March 3 election and as listed in our Arkansas guide. Pulaski, Benton and Washington Counties are covered only through these seats: Washington County is in the 4th District, Division 2 runoff. No circuit judge race in Pulaski or Benton County needed a runoff, per the same roundup. Not covered: district (city) court judges, county judges, and justices of the peace. We found no official list of Nov. 3 races for those, so we make no claim about them. Check your county sample ballot.

  • Circuit Judge, District 2, Division 4 (at large)
    Curtis Hitt and Andrew Nadzam (nonpartisan) source: Arkansas Times (unreviewed) source: KAIT (unreviewed)    Runoff. Hitt had 39% and Nadzam 32% in March. Incumbent Doug Brimhall did not advance.

    Wendy Howerton and Kristin Pawlik (nonpartisan) source: Arkansas Times (unreviewed)    Runoff. Howerton had 39% and Pawlik 38% in March. This district covers Washington and Madison Counties.

    Kara Byars and Scott Davidson (nonpartisan) source: Arkansas Times (unreviewed) source: KAIT (unreviewed)    Runoff. Byars had 48% and Davidson 30% in March.
