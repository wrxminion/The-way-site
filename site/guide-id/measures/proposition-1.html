In plain words

Idaho law now bans abortion except to prevent the pregnant woman's death and, in the first trimester, in rape or incest cases reported to police. Prop 1 would create a right to abortion until viability, and after that when the woman's life or health is at serious risk. It also writes a right to privacy for decisions about birth control, fertility treatment and pregnancy care into state law. (voteidaho.gov (Idaho Secretary of State) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Creates a right to abortion before fetal viability; after viability no general right "except in cases of 'medical emergency'" (pamphlet long title, read as a summary). source: voteidaho.gov (Idaho Secretary of State)
  • Adds a right to privacy for reproductive decisions and liability protections for health care providers (pamphlet short title, read as a summary). source: voteidaho.gov (Idaho Secretary of State)

Who it affects most: Pregnant people in Idaho, their doctors and other health care workers.

Cost to the state: The pamphlet fiscal impact statement (read as a summary; the agency is named there as the "Idaho Division of Fiscal Management"): the state estimates the initiative would raise state spending between $3,100 and $7,800 a year, mostly Medicaid costs for complications from chemical abortions. The agency says it "cannot in good faith conclude" the measure would have no fiscal impact. The backers' funding statement says it creates no new financial obligation for the state.

What your vote means

YES

Abortion becomes a legal right in Idaho until viability, and after that in medical emergencies.

NO

Idaho's current ban stays: abortion only to prevent death, or for reported rape or incest in the first trimester.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Put on the ballot by citizen petition. The Secretary of State said on July 14, 2026 that it qualified, with 75,478 signatures and enough signatures in 20 of 35 legislative districts (18 needed). source: idahonews.com

Why supporters want it

  • Idahoans United for Women & Families (pamphlet argument, read as a summary): the measure would create exceptions to the current ban for medical emergencies, "ensuring that sensitive, urgent decisions in medical emergencies are made by patients and their doctors." They also say it creates no public funding for abortion and does not change parental consent laws. source: voteidaho.gov (Idaho Secretary of State)

Why opponents object

  • Idaho Family Policy Center, Idaho Chooses Life, Right to Life Idaho, Too Extreme for Idaho and the Roman Catholic Diocese of Boise (pamphlet argument, read as a summary): the measure is "a backdoor attempt to legalize late-term abortions, long past the point in pregnancy." They also say it changes the viability definition, lets non-physicians provide abortions and overrides parental consent. source: voteidaho.gov (Idaho Secretary of State)

Who backs each side

  • For: Idahoans United for Women and Families raised about $4.5 million as of Oct. 9, 2026. Top donors include Government That Works PAC ($900,000) and Michael R. Bloomberg ($250,000). source: idahonews.com
  • Against: Too Extreme for Idaho raised almost $2.6 million as of Oct. 9, 2026. Top donors include Angela and James Harrison ($1,000,000) and Dorothy Rebholtz ($300,000). source: idahonews.com
  • The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints announced a $1 million donation to the Too Extreme for Idaho PAC in October 2026, after the totals above. source: deseret.com
