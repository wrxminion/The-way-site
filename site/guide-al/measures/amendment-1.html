Amendment · every Alabama voter

Amendment 1: Filling an empty lieutenant governor's officeSpecial election to fill an empty lieutenant governor's office; lawmakers set some salaries

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Act 2026-341, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as Senate Bill 271 (Act 2026-341) ↗. Passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Fair Ballot Commission statements (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Today, if the lieutenant governor dies, resigns, becomes governor or is removed, the office stays empty until the next regular election. A special election is held only if both the governor's and lieutenant governor's offices are empty. This amendment would call a special election to fill the lieutenant governor's office. It would also let lawmakers set the pay of the lieutenant governor and legislative officers (for example, the Speaker and the Senate leader), which the constitution sets today, and require a legislative officer to approve any travel paid for with legislative money. (sos.alabama.gov ↗)

Biggest change

  • Requires a special election to fill the lieutenant governor's office if it becomes empty early in a term. source: 1819news.com
  • Lets the Legislature set the salary of the lieutenant governor and legislative officers by joint resolution. source: sos.alabama.gov
  • Requires a legislative officer to approve any spending of legislative money on travel. source: sos.alabama.gov

Who it affects most: Voters statewide, who would choose a replacement lieutenant governor instead of leaving the office empty; and the lieutenant governor and legislative leaders, whose pay lawmakers could change.

Cost to the state: The Fair Ballot Commission says there are no costs or taxes tied to Amendment 1. The cost of a special election, if one is ever needed, is not estimated.

What your vote means

YES

If the lieutenant governor's office becomes empty early in a term, voters elect a replacement, and lawmakers can set the pay of the lieutenant governor and their own leaders.

NO

An empty lieutenant governor's office stays empty until the next regular election, and those salaries stay set in the constitution.

Our one-line summary. Alabama does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Sen. Sam Givhan (R-Huntsville) sponsored it. Givhan says the idea has been discussed for years and is not tied to any current candidate. source: 1819news.com
  • When the lieutenant governor's office is empty, the Senate president pro tem (the leader senators elect) picks up its duties. source: 1819news.com

Why supporters want it

  • Sen. Sam Givhan, the sponsor: "The pro-tem's got enough duties as it is without having to also do the duties of lieutenant governor." On pay for the Speaker and pro tem: "They essentially have a full-time job, and you know, they should be paid more." source: 1819news.com

Why opponents object

  • No public statement found.

Who backs each side

  • For: sponsor Sen. Sam Givhan and the Legislature, which placed it on the ballot. Against: no group found. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: 1819news.com

Proposing an amendment to the Constitution of Alabama of 2022, to provide for the election of a Lieutenant Governor if the Office of Lieutenant Governor becomes vacant during the first two years of a term, to authorize the Legislature to set the salary of the Lieutenant Governor and officers of the Legislature, and to require any expenditure of legislative funds for travel to be approved by a legislative officer.

Proposed by Act 2026-341.
