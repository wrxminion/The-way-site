North Carolina voter guide/Constitutional Amendments/
Amendment · every North Carolina voter

Photo ID amendmentPhoto ID for every voter, including by mail

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Session Law 2024-58, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as Senate Bill 921 (Session Law 2024-58) ↗. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: State Board of Elections referendum list (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Since 2018 the state constitution has required photo ID for people who vote in person. A 2024 state law already asks mail-in voters to send a copy of their ID too. This amendment writes that into the constitution, so it would take another statewide vote to undo it. How you vote would not change right away. (spectrumlocalnews.com ↗)

Biggest change

  • Makes the photo ID requirement for mail-in voting part of the state constitution instead of only a state law, so lawmakers could no longer remove it on their own. source: spectrumlocalnews.com

Who it affects most: People who vote by mail, including many older, disabled, military and overseas voters.

Cost to the state: No cost estimate was published with the amendment.

What your vote means

YES

Photo ID for mail-in voting becomes part of the state constitution, so only another statewide vote could remove it.

NO

Photo ID for mail-in voting stays a state law that lawmakers could change; in-person photo ID stays in the constitution.

Our one-line summary. North Carolina does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Voters added photo ID for in-person voting to the constitution in 2018. A 2024 state law extended it to mail-in ballots. source: spectrumlocalnews.com

Why supporters want it

  • Andy Jackson of the John Locke Foundation, a conservative think tank, says it would make the ID rule harder to reverse if the other party takes control of the General Assembly, and that voters should see no change in how they vote. source: spectrumlocalnews.com
  • Republican supporters call ID checks "common sense security." source: axios.com

Why opponents object

  • Brooks Fuller of Common Cause NC: "I think we should be skeptical of any additional hurdle that's placed on voters." He says Black and young voters are less likely to have a qualifying ID. source: spectrumlocalnews.com
  • Disability Rights North Carolina and other critics say it can burden military, student, rural and older mail-in voters, and that it protects the ID rule but not the exceptions. source: axios.com

Who backs each side

  • For: the Republican leaders of the General Assembly who placed it on the ballot. Against: Common Cause NC and Disability Rights North Carolina. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: axios.com
