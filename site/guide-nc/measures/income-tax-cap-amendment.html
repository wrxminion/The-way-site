North Carolina voter guide/Constitutional Amendments/
Amendment · every North Carolina voter

Income tax cap amendmentCap the state income tax at 3.5%

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Senate Bill 1080, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as Senate Bill 1080 (2026) ↗. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: State Board of Elections referendum list (PDF) ↗

In plain words

The state constitution sets the highest income tax rate North Carolina is allowed to charge. Today that ceiling is 7%. This amendment lowers it to 3.5% starting in 2027. It doesn't lower anyone's taxes right away; it stops future lawmakers from raising the rate above 3.5% without another statewide vote. (axios.com ↗)

Example

Illustration only: on $60,000 of taxable income, the most the state could ever charge drops from $4,200 a year (7%) to $2,100 (3.5%). In 2026 the actual tax at 3.99% is $2,394. (axios.com ↗)

Who it affects most: Every income tax payer if lawmakers later want to raise rates, higher earners most, and the schools and services the state budget pays for, since more than half of it comes from income taxes.

Cost to the state: No official estimate. It does not cut current taxes; it limits future increases.

What your vote means

YES

The highest income tax rate the state may ever charge drops from 7% to 3.5% starting in 2027.

NO

The ceiling stays at 7%, so future lawmakers could raise the rate above 3.5% without a statewide vote.

Our one-line summary. North Carolina does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • North Carolina has cut its income tax rate for over a decade; the rate is 3.99% in 2026 and set to fall to 3.49% in 2027. The corporate rate is scheduled to drop to 0% by 2030. source: axios.com

Why supporters want it

  • Rep. Dean Arp (R-Union): "At its core, it's people deciding what they want to do with their own money." source: ncnewsline.com
  • Rep. Keith Kidwell (R-Beaufort): "We tax people into oblivion." source: ncnewsline.com
  • Supporters call the cap "a safeguard to protect all taxpayers" from future tax increases. source: axios.com

Why opponents object

  • Gov. Josh Stein (D) calls it "a millionaire protection cap." source: axios.com
  • Rep. Phil Rubin (D-Wake): "This amendment will put less money in working people's pockets." He warns of cuts to schools, child care, roads and public safety. source: ncnewsline.com
  • Rep. Pricey Harrison (D-Guilford): "This is absolutely unnecessary and seems to be politically driven." She notes more than half the state budget comes from income taxes. source: ncnewsline.com

Who backs each side

  • For: the Republican majorities in the General Assembly. Against: Gov. Josh Stein and Democratic lawmakers. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: ncnewsline.com
