North Carolina voter guide/Constitutional Amendments/
Amendment · every North Carolina voter

Property tax limit amendmentLimit how fast local property taxes can grow

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Session Law 2026-5, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as House Bill 1089 (Session Law 2026-5) ↗. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: State Board of Elections referendum list (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Counties and cities pay for schools, police, fire and roads largely with property tax. This amendment tells the state legislature to pass a law limiting how much the total amount of property tax a local government collects (its "levy") can go up. The amendment itself sets no number. The limit, and any exceptions, would come later in a separate law. (axios.com ↗)

Biggest change

  • The amendment does not set the limit. The actual cap, and any exceptions, would be written later by the General Assembly. source: ncnewsline.com

Who it affects most: Homeowners and businesses in fast-growing counties, and the county schools, sheriffs, fire departments and other services that property taxes pay for.

Cost to the state: Unknown. It depends on the limit lawmakers later choose.

What your vote means

YES

The legislature must pass a law limiting how fast local property tax collections can grow; the size of the limit is decided later.

NO

No such limit goes into the constitution; counties and cities keep setting their own property tax rates.

Our one-line summary. North Carolina does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Supporters say 22 states already limit local property tax growth. source: ncnewsline.com

Why supporters want it

  • Rep. Brian Echevarria (R-Cabarrus), a primary sponsor: "No one gets priced out of the home they own. They get taxed out of the home they own." On other states with limits: "The sky did not fall." source: axios.com
  • Rep. Carla Cunningham (unaffiliated, Mecklenburg), who cast a deciding vote, cited worries about older homeowners being pushed out as neighborhoods change. source: ncnewsline.com

Why opponents object

  • Rep. Brandon Lofton (D-Mecklenburg): "How many teachers are we willing to risk losing?" source: ncnewsline.com
  • House Democratic Leader Robert Reives (D-Chatham) says lawmakers can already give relief: "This amendment doesn't change anything about your property tax." source: ncnewsline.com
  • Charlotte-Mecklenburg Schools leaders warned of a "detrimental impact" on children, since the county adds to teacher pay with property tax money. source: axios.com

Who backs each side

  • For: the Republican majorities in the General Assembly. Against: Democratic lawmakers and some local school leaders. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: ncnewsline.com
