Judges on the ballot

North Carolina votes for one N.C. Supreme Court associate justice and three N.C. Court of Appeals judges. These are partisan races: each candidate appears with a party label. Eight candidates are on the ballot, four from each party.

The details below come from the State Board of Elections' official judicial voter guide. The guide says the candidate statements were provided by the candidates and do not reflect the Board's opinions. Seven of the eight candidates currently serve as judges. Sarah Stevens is the exception; the guide quotes her as saying she has not been on the bench before.

N.C. Supreme Court, Associate Justice Seat 1

  • Anita Earls (Democrat) source: NC State Board of Elections guideSenior Associate Justice on the court since 2018, according to the guide. Earlier she founded and led the Southern Coalition for Social Justice and was a deputy assistant attorney general at the U.S. Department of Justice. Full profile.
  • Sarah Stevens (Republican) source: NC State Board of Elections guidePrivate practice attorney for 40 years and a state legislator for 17.5 years, including 14 years as House Judiciary chair, according to the guide. She states, "I have not been on the bench before." Full profile.

N.C. Court of Appeals, Seat 1

  • Michael C. Byrne (Republican) source: NC State Board of Elections guideA judge with the N.C. Office of Administrative Hearings with six years on the bench, according to the guide. His statement lists endorsements from police and public employee organizations. Full profile.
  • John S. Arrowood (Democrat) source: NC State Board of Elections guideCourt of Appeals judge. Earlier he served at that court as a law clerk, staff attorney and staff director, and practiced civil and commercial litigation for more than 25 years, according to the guide. Full profile.

N.C. Court of Appeals, Seat 2

  • George Cooper Bell (Republican) source: NC State Board of Elections guideSuperior Court judge, elected in 2018. The guide says he has presided over 100 felony jury trials. Full profile.
  • Tobias (Toby) Hampson (Democrat) source: NC State Board of Elections guideCourt of Appeals judge since 2019. Earlier he spent about 16 years as an appellate attorney and was a Court of Appeals law clerk, according to the guide.

N.C. Court of Appeals, Seat 3

  • Craig Collins (Republican) source: NC State Board of Elections guideSuperior Court judge. The guide lists nine years each as an assistant district attorney, private attorney and district court judge. Full profile.
  • Christine Marie Walczyk (Democrat) source: NC State Board of Elections guideDistrict Court judge since 2007, according to the guide. She was managing member of a law firm from 1995 to 2007. Her statement lists endorsements. Full profile.

County, district and local judges

Statewide check: the Supreme Court and Court of Appeals candidates and parties listed above match the State Board of Elections candidate file as of November 4, 2026.

Coverage, as of November 4, 2026. This section lists every North Carolina District Court and Superior Court seat that has at least one candidate in the State Board of Elections candidate file for the November 3, 2026 election (164 seats in 39 judicial districts). Nominees of contested March 3 primaries were taken from the Board's March 3 results file, choosing the candidate with the most votes. We did not check for a later primary runoff. Mecklenburg (District 26), Wake (District 10) and Guilford (District 24) come first, then the other districts by number. A seat missing here had no candidate in the file. The file does not say who is the sitting judge, so we do not mark incumbents. Not covered: clerks of superior court, district attorneys, magistrates (appointed), and city courts. Judges here run with party labels.

Most of these seats have one candidate, who is unopposed in November and still appears on the ballot. 20 seats have two nominees.
