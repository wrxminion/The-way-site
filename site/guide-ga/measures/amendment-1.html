Amendment · every Georgia voter

Amendment 1: Bigger farm and timber land tax breakDouble the farm and timber land that can get a lower property tax value, from 2,000 to 4,000 acres

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): House Resolution 32 (2025), placed on the ballot by the Legislature as House Resolution 32 (Ga. L. 2025, p. 1081), with House Bill 90 (2025) ↗. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Secretary of State's 2026 constitutional amendments booklet (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Georgia has a program called conservation use. If you agree to keep land as farm, timber or wildlife land for at least 10 years, the county taxes it on what it earns as farm or timber land, not on what it would sell for. Today one owner can put at most 2,000 acres in the program. This amendment raises that to 4,000 acres. A state law passed with it (House Bill 90) would start on Jan. 1, 2027, if voters say yes. (news.ballotpedia.org ↗)

Example

Illustration only (made-up numbers): say farmland would sell for $3,000 an acre but is worth $1,000 an acre as farmland, and the local tax rate is 25 mills ($25 for every $1,000 of taxed value). For 2,000 more acres, the owner would pay about $60,000 a year in property tax without the program and about $20,000 with it, saving about $40,000. That money would come from the local tax base that pays for county services and schools. (news.ballotpedia.org ↗)

Biggest change

  • Doubles the conservation use acreage limit for one owner from 2,000 to 4,000 acres. It would be the first increase to the limit since the program was created. source: news.ballotpedia.org
  • The state law that goes with it, House Bill 90, takes effect Jan. 1, 2027, only if voters approve the amendment. source: capitol-beat.org

Who it affects most: Owners of more than 2,000 acres of farm or timber land, and the counties and school districts that tax that land.

Cost to the state: Unknown. No state cost estimate was found. Local governments and schools could collect less property tax on the land that joins the program.

What your vote means

YES

One owner can put up to 4,000 acres of farm or timber land in the lower property tax program, up from 2,000.

NO

The limit stays at 2,000 acres per owner.

Our one-line summary. Georgia does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Voters created conservation use in a 1990 constitutional amendment, with 62% voting yes. The program began in 1991. source: news.ballotpedia.org
  • A state Senate study committee on farmland held hearings and recommended growing the program. Georgia has lost about 2.6 million acres of farmland to homes and businesses over the past 50 years. source: capitol-beat.org
  • The General Assembly passed it in 2025: 168-5 in the House (March 6) and 50-1 in the Senate (March 18). Rep. Chuck Efstration (R) sponsored the resolution; Sen. Sam Watson (R-Moultrie) carried it in the Senate. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Why supporters want it

  • Sen. Sam Watson (R-Moultrie): "It's a tool to allow family farms to continue to grow and expand." source: moultrieobserver.com
  • Watson: "Agriculture has changed. Our family farms are getting much larger today." source: capitol-beat.org
  • Supporters say it will help keep family farms in business. The ballot question says it is "for the purpose of protecting family farmland." source: ajc.com

Why opponents object

  • Katherine Moore, president of the Georgia Conservancy, told the study committee that counties and local governments may push back because land taken off the tax rolls shrinks their property tax base. source: moultrieobserver.com
  • Some worry it could cut property tax money for local governments and schools, or give a tax break to large corporate farms that don't need one. (No one was named.) source: ajc.com

Who backs each side

  • For: the General Assembly, which passed it with large majorities in both parties; Sen. Sam Watson and Rep. Chuck Efstration. Against: no organized campaign found. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: news.ballotpedia.org
