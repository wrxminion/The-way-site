Judges on the ballot

We did not find any state judge or justice on Georgia's November 3, 2026 ballot. Georgia's nonpartisan judicial elections were held with the May 19, 2026 primary.

Checked November 4, 2026. Georgia holds nonpartisan judicial elections with the May primary, not in November. Cobb County's official May 19, 2026 nonpartisan sample ballot lists the Supreme Court, Court of Appeals, Superior Court and State Court races. We found no judicial office on the November 3 ballot. The Secretary of State calendar returned an access error from our build, so we could not read it directly. Check your sample ballot.

What we found

  • May election. The Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported on the May 2026 Georgia Supreme Court races and the Democratic-backed challenges to sitting justices. source: ajc.com (unreviewed)
  • Official calendar. The Secretary of State publishes the 2026 election calendar. source: GA Secretary of State (unopened)

County, district and local judges

Coverage: we looked for superior, state, probate and magistrate judge races on the November 3, 2026 ballot in Fulton, Gwinnett, Cobb and DeKalb counties and found none. The official source we could open is Cobb County's consolidated nonpartisan sample ballot for May 19, 2026, which lists the Cobb Judicial Circuit Superior Court and State Court seats there. We could not open the Fulton, Gwinnett or DeKalb ballots directly, and we could not open the Secretary of State's list of offices on the 2026 ballot. This section does not list the May winners county by county, and it does not cover municipal courts, whose judges are generally appointed by city governments (not checked city by city).

  • Superior and State Court judges, all counties source: Cobb County sample ballotDecided in the nonpartisan election on May 19, 2026 (runoffs, if needed, in June). Not on the November 3 ballot.
  • Probate judges: ballot question only source: ballotready.org (unreviewed)BallotReady lists a statewide question on the 2026 general election ballot on whether all probate court judges must be elected in nonpartisan elections. It is a question, not a judge race. We have not checked the official wording, and the amendment number differs between sources.
