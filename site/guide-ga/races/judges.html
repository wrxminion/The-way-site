Judges on the ballot

We did not find any state judge or justice on Georgia's November 3, 2026 ballot. Georgia's nonpartisan judicial elections were held with the May 19, 2026 primary.

Checked November 4, 2026. We opened the Georgia Secretary of State unofficial results page for the November 3, 2026 General and Special Elections (last updated 11/04/26 2:19 AM) and searched its contest list for the words Court, Judge, Justice and Probate. The search found no judge or court contest. The same page lists three Proposed Constitutional Amendments. One of them is the probate judge question described below. Georgia holds nonpartisan judicial elections with the May primary, not in November. The Secretary of State calendar and 2026 amendment booklet returned access errors from our build, so we did not read them. Check your sample ballot. source: GA SOS unofficial results

County, district and local judges

Coverage, as of November 4, 2026. Confirmed: the statewide results page for November 3 shows no state or local judge contest under a search for Court, Judge, Justice or Probate. Earlier we also found none on the Cobb County sample ballots. Not confirmed: the Fulton, Gwinnett, DeKalb and Chatham county ballots and candidate lists were not opened directly, so we cannot say that a county-only judge race (for example a special election to fill a vacancy) is absent from those four counties. The results page may not list every county-only contest. The Secretary of State's 2026 offices list and election calendar were not readable. This section does not list the May winners county by county, and it does not cover municipal courts (not checked city by city).

  • Superior and State Court judges, all counties source: Cobb County sample ballotDecided in the nonpartisan election on May 19, 2026 (runoffs, if needed, in June). Not on the November 3 ballot.
  • Probate judges: ballot question only source: Ballotpedia, probate amendment source: Atlanta News First source: ballotready.org (unreviewed)A statewide constitutional amendment on whether all probate court judges must be elected in nonpartisan elections (without party labels). A yes vote requires nonpartisan elections. A no vote keeps probate judges elected with party labels. It is a question, not a judge race. Ballotpedia and Atlanta News First call it Amendment 2. BallotReady calls it Amendment 1. We did not read the official wording, because the Secretary of State booklet was blocked, so the number and wording are not independently confirmed. Ballotpedia reports it as approved, from unofficial results; certified results come later. On the Secretary of State results page, Proposed Constitutional Amendment 2 shows 68.36 percent yes, but we could not tie that number to this question from an official source.
