Judges on the ballot

We did not find any statewide judge or justice on West Virginia's November 3, 2026 ballot. The Supreme Court of Appeals and Intermediate Court of Appeals races were decided in the nonpartisan election on May 12, 2026. The Secretary of State's 2026 General candidate list shows three local judicial contests for unexpired terms: two circuit court divisions and one family court division.

The Secretary of State's 2026 Judicial Candidate Guide says all West Virginia judicial races are nonpartisan and held in a single race, generally in May of election years. The May 12 date is from our West Virginia guide and we did not find an official page that states the day. One exception may put a circuit judge on the November ballot; see the last section.

How West Virginia elects judges: the guide says justices of the Supreme Court of Appeals, Intermediate Court of Appeals judges, circuit judges, family court judges and magistrates are elected by popular vote. Since 2016 all judicial races are nonpartisan and decided in one race, generally in May. Mental hygiene commissioners, juvenile referees, special commissioners and special masters are appointed. These are contested elections, not retention votes.

Possible November runoff: the guide says that since the 2024 judicial elections, if no candidate in a circuit court division gets more than 30 percent of the votes, a runoff is held at the next general election between the top two. The state candidate list shows no circuit division labeled as a runoff, but we did not read the certified May 12 results, so we cannot say none occurred.

Not covered: municipal judges, which the guide says are not covered by the judicial code, and any special election for a vacancy.
