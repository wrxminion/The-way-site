Judges on the ballot

We did not find any statewide judge or justice on Vermont's November 3, 2026 ballot. The governor appoints state judges from a nominating board list and the Senate confirms them. County probate judges and assistant judges are elected.

Statewide: judges of the Supreme Court and Superior Court are appointed, so none is on the ballot. Probate judges and assistant judges are elected by county. The Vermont Secretary of State's General Election Candidates page says the offices to be elected in 2026 include probate judge and assistant judges. All 14 counties are listed below.

What we found

  • Nominating board. The Judicial Nominating Board sends the governor a list of qualified candidates for Supreme Court justices, superior judges and magistrates. source: Vermont Judiciary
  • Recent confirmations. The Vermont Courts site reports the Senate confirmed Christina Nolan and Michael Drescher to the Vermont Supreme Court. source: Vermont Courts
  • Retention. Ballotpedia says appointed judges are retained by a vote of the General Assembly after each six-year term, not by voters. We did not confirm this against the statute. source: ballotpedia.org (unreviewed)

County, district and local judges

What this covers. Every probate judge and assistant judge candidate in all 14 Vermont counties, taken from the Vermont Secretary of State's 2026 General Election Qualified Candidates spreadsheet, which we downloaded on November 4, 2026. We list counties from largest to smallest. A party shown as two names (for example Democratic and Republican) means the person is the nominee of both parties. Each county elects one probate judge and two assistant judges, so for assistant judge you may vote for up to two. What it does not cover. The spreadsheet is the state's list of candidates who qualified; write-in candidates and any later withdrawals are not shown. We did not check the ballots your town prints. Superior court judges, who sit in these counties, are appointed and are not listed.
