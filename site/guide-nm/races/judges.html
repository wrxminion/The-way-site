Judges on the ballot

In New Mexico, judges first win a seat in a partisan election; later they face a yes or no retention vote. Retention votes for the judges on your ballot are listed here, from the Secretary of State's candidate list. The official list does not say which court the three statewide retention judges sit on; they are the two Supreme Court justices and one Court of Appeals judge named by the evaluation commission. District, Metropolitan Court, magistrate and municipal judge races are on each county's page.

How retention works here

The state Judicial Performance Evaluation Commission (JPEC) reviews each judge before a retention vote and gives voters a recommendation. The commission advises; voters decide. For 2026 it covered 43 judges and, per the Albuquerque Journal, recommended keeping 41; for the other two it made no recommendation because they have not served long enough. The Albuquerque Journal reports that under state law a judge must get at least 57 percent voter approval to stay on the bench. Search every judge's evaluation on the source: JPEC site.

Parties are as the list shows them (no party means "no party/declined to select"). These are partisan elections, and where one name follows a seat that candidate is the only one listed. Retention questions do not appear on this list, so the retention judges above are separate. Not covered: municipal judges, and any race the list does not show.
