Judges on the ballot

Texans vote in partisan races for 10 statewide judgeships: four Texas Supreme Court seats (civil cases), three Court of Criminal Appeals seats (criminal cases) and three seats on the 15th Court of Appeals, a statewide court created in 2024. Each candidate appears with a party label. District, county and regional appeals judges for every Texas county are listed in the section after the statewide courts and on a second page.

Checked on November 4, 2026 against the Texas Secretary of State's statewide sample ballot (posted August 28, 2026) and the Dallas County election results data, which both list all 21 statewide judicial candidates. The five names that lacked profiles (Triana, Ash, Yeary, Zimmerer, Meyer) match those sources. Sources marked "(unreviewed)" link to news or reference pages and were not independently reviewed.

County, district and local judges

What this covers. Every county in Texas (all 254), from one official source: the Texas Secretary of State ballot certification for the Nov. 3, 2026 general election, dated August 28, 2026 (a PDF listing the candidates the state and county parties certified for each county's ballot). We read it on Nov. 4, 2026. It is covered here as follows. Courts of appeals 1 through 14 (and the statewide 15th, above) are listed below. Detailed county sections on this page: Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar, Travis, Collin, Hidalgo, El Paso, Denton, Fort Bend, Montgomery, Williamson, Cameron, Nueces, Brazoria, Bell, Galveston, Lubbock, Webb, Jefferson, McLennan, Smith, Brazos, Hays, Johnson, Ellis, Midland, Guadalupe, Ector, Comal, Kaufman, Taylor, Randall. All other counties (221) are on the second page, county by county. Each county lists, where the certification shows them, district courts, the county judge, county courts at law and county criminal courts, probate courts and justices of the peace. Races are partisan; names appear with the party label on the certification. Limits. The certification is from August 28. Candidates who withdrew, were replaced or ran as write-ins afterward are not reflected, and your county sample ballot is the final word. We did not check it against each county's own sample ballot or results site, except that the earlier county results lists for Dallas, Tarrant and Travis (read Nov. 4) and the Harris and Bexar news guides had no name that is missing from the certification, apart from one Harris district seat noted below. City and municipal judges, constables and other non-judicial offices are not covered. Names are shown as certified, with capital letters normalized. We did not research any candidate.
