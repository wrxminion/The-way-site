Judges on the ballot

Texans vote in partisan races for 10 statewide judgeships: four Texas Supreme Court seats (civil cases), three Court of Criminal Appeals seats (criminal cases) and three seats on the 15th Court of Appeals, a statewide court created in 2024. Each candidate appears with a party label. District, county and regional appeals judges are listed in the section after the statewide courts.

Checked on November 4, 2026 against the Texas Secretary of State's statewide sample ballot (posted August 28, 2026) and the Dallas County election results data, which both list all 21 statewide judicial candidates. The five names that lacked profiles (Triana, Ash, Yeary, Zimmerer, Meyer) match those sources. Sources marked "(unreviewed)" link to news or reference pages and were not independently reviewed.

County, district and local judges

What this covers. Judge races in the five largest counties by population: Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Bexar and Travis. Texas has 254 counties and 14 regional courts of appeals, so most of the state is not covered. Sources, all read on Nov. 4, 2026: Harris County Elections sample ballot and a Click2Houston guide; the Dallas, Tarrant and Travis county election results sites (they list the candidates for each race); and a San Antonio Report voter guide for Bexar. Races are partisan. Dallas's results list shows only contested races, so Dallas seats with no opponent are missing there. Where a county source shows one name, we say so. Harris courts of appeals seats come from a sample ballot PDF that is hard to read by machine, so only four seats are listed. Courts of appeals 6 through 13, and all other counties, are not covered. We did not research any candidate.
