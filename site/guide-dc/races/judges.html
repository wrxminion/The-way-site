Judges on the ballot

We did not find any District of Columbia judge on the November 3, 2026 ballot. D.C. judges are not elected by voters. The President appoints them with Senate confirmation.

The Way does not yet have a full District of Columbia voter guide. This page covers judges only. Sources marked "(unreviewed)" were not independently reviewed. We did not check Advisory Neighborhood Commission or other local races.

What we found

  • Who chooses. The D.C. Judicial Nomination Commission recommends a panel of qualified people to the President for vacancies on the D.C. Superior Court and the D.C. Court of Appeals. The President nominates one person and the U.S. Senate confirms. source: DC Judicial Nomination Commission source: ballotpedia.org (unreviewed)
  • Terms. Associate judges serve 15-year terms and must retire at age 74, according to Ballotpedia. source: ballotpedia.org (unreviewed)
  • Vacancies. The Commission announced Superior Court vacancies in September 2025, including one from the retirement of Judge Craig Iscoe effective January 13, 2026. We did not confirm whether those seats have been filled. source: DC Judicial Nomination Commission

County, district and local judges

Coverage, as of November 4, 2026. We looked for any elected judge in the District of Columbia and found none. Judges of the D.C. Superior Court and the D.C. Court of Appeals are nominated by the President and confirmed by the U.S. Senate, so no judge appears on the November 3 ballot. There are no separate county, district or municipal courts in D.C. to cover. We did not review each ward's ballot.

  • D.C. Superior Court and Court of Appeals
    Appointed, not elected source: D.C. Courts source: D.C. Judicial Nomination Commission    The Judicial Nomination Commission recommends names to the President, who nominates. The Senate confirms. Associate judges serve 15-year terms.
  • Advisory Neighborhood Commissions
    Elected neighborhood boards, not judicial source: D.C. ANC Handbook    ANCs are advisory bodies of residents elected from neighborhoods. They advise the government on policy and have no court role. We did not review which ANC seats are on the ballot.
