Judges on the ballot

We did not find any statewide judge on South Carolina's November 3, 2026 ballot. South Carolina's state judges are elected by the General Assembly, not by voters. Voters do elect county probate judges, listed below.

Our South Carolina guide says the General Assembly chooses judges. The legislature's own pages describe a Judicial Merit Selection Commission that screens candidates before the General Assembly votes.

What we found

  • Who chooses. Ballotpedia says South Carolina is one of two states where the legislature, not voters or the governor, elects judges (Virginia is the other). source: ballotpedia.org (unreviewed)
  • Screening. The Judicial Merit Selection Commission screens candidates and sends names to the General Assembly, which votes in joint session. Legislation in 2023 and 2024 changed the commission to twelve members. We did not confirm the current rules. source: SC General Assembly, S.1046

County, district and local judges

Coverage, as of November 4, 2026. Probate judges: we read the State Election Commission candidate tracking list for the November 3, 2026 general election, filtered to the office Probate Judge. It shows 41 candidates in 36 of South Carolina's 46 counties. We list only candidates marked Active. Candidates the list marks as defeated in the June primary are left out. No candidate is listed for 10 counties (Aiken, Bamberg, Calhoun, Clarendon, Dorchester, Georgetown, Lee, Marion, Saluda, Williamsburg). We do not know why. The Commission says candidate data for some local races may be incomplete, so check your sample ballot for those counties. Not covered: magistrates and municipal judges, who are appointed, and any local judicial office outside this list.

South Carolina voters elect probate judges to four-year terms, with party labels. Magistrates are appointed by the governor with Senate consent. Municipal judges are appointed by city or town councils.
