Constitutional Amendment 3Nominating committees for university regents

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): House Joint Resolution 1 (2026), HJR 1 (2026), amends Article 12, Section 13. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Secretary of State amendments and bond questions (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Regents are the boards that run New Mexico's 10 state universities and special schools. Today the governor picks them, and the state Senate approves them. This amendment says the Legislature must set up a nominating committee for each school, and the governor must choose from that committee's list. Student regents would come from a list made by the elected student government, not the school president. People who switched parties shortly before being picked could not serve. Board sizes and terms stay the same. (nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Creates a nominating committee for each of the 10 institutions; no more than half of a committee's members may be from one party. source: nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature)

Who it affects most: State universities and special schools, their students, and the governor.

Cost to the state: No cost estimate was published with the amendment.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Sen. Jeff Steinborn (D-Las Cruces), a sponsor, says governors have used the process to appoint friends or political allies, and that efforts to change it have gone on for years. source: newsfromthestates.com

Why supporters want it

  • Steinborn says the current system has been "abused," that appointees have often lacked higher-education experience, and that the timing was "perfect" because no sitting governor was protecting the power. source: newsfromthestates.com
  • The Legislative Council Service lists: committees could find qualified regents and reduce party influence. source: nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature)

Why opponents object

  • The Legislative Council Service lists: it moves power away from the governor and Senate, makes it harder to know who is responsible for a choice, and committees do not answer to voters. source: nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature)

Who backs each side

  • For: the legislators who sponsored HJR 1, including Sen. Jeff Steinborn. Against: no named group found. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: newsfromthestates.com

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Secretary of State's official list of constitutional amendments and bond questions ↗.

Proposing an amendment to Article 12, Section 13 of the Constitution of New Mexico to establish nominating committees to provide lists of nominees to the governor for appointment of nonstudent members to the boards of regents of state educational institutions; providing that student members of boards of regents shall be appointed from a list provided by an elected student governing body of the institution; providing that, except for a student member, members of the boards of regents shall not have changed their political party registration in the twelve months preceding appointment.
