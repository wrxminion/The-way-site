Constitutional Amendment 2Let school elections be held with November general elections

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Senate Joint Resolution 1 (2026), SJR 1 (2026), amends Article 7, Section 1. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it, for all four 2026 amendments (2026 Voter Guide, General Information, printed page 5 ↗).

Read the full text of the measure: Secretary of State amendments and bond questions (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Since 1910 the state constitution has required school elections to be held apart from elections with party candidates. This amendment removes that rule. School districts could put bond questions (borrowing the district pays back with interest) and property tax questions on the November ballot, and lawmakers could later choose to hold school board races at the same time. The sponsor says school board races would stay nonpartisan. (nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Deletes the constitution's requirement that school elections be held separately from partisan elections. source: nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature)

Who it affects most: School districts, teachers and school staff, and voters who decide school bond and tax questions.

Cost to the state: No official cost estimate. The Legislative Council Service says special all-mail school elections cost $125,000 to $250,000 each; Source NM reports the state, not districts, would pay for school elections held with general elections.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it, for all four 2026 amendments (2026 Voter Guide, General Information, printed page 5 ↗).

  • The separate-election rule dates to the 1910 Constitutional Convention and was reaffirmed by voters in 2008. source: nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature)

Why supporters want it

  • Sen. Linda Trujillo (D-Santa Fe), a sponsor, says it would "streamline" the process for districts seeking bond approval, notes turnout is higher in general elections, and called the rule the "last little semblance" of limits from when women could vote only in school elections. source: newsfromthestates.com
  • The Legislative Council Service lists: lower costs, higher turnout, more flexibility for districts, and less sway for organized groups that dominate low-turnout elections. source: nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature)

Why opponents object

  • The Legislative Council Service lists: longer ballots and voter fatigue, possible future party influence if lawmakers move school board races to partisan ballots, less voting weight for teachers and school staff, and less attention to school issues on crowded ballots. source: nmlegis.gov (New Mexico Legislature)

Who backs each side

  • For: the legislators who sponsored SJR 1, including Sen. Linda Trujillo. Against: no named group found. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: newsfromthestates.com
