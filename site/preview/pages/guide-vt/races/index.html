  • Governor5 candidates

    The state's top executive.

  • Lieutenant Governor2 candidates

    Leads the state Senate's meetings (the presiding officer) and votes only to break a tie.

  • Attorney General2 candidates

    The state's top lawyer.

  • Secretary of State3 candidates

    Oversees elections, keeps the state's historical records, and licenses, investigates and disciplines people in more than 50 professions.

  • State Treasurer3 candidates

    The state's banker and chief investment officer.

  • Auditor of Accounts2 candidates

    The state's spending watchdog.

  • U.S. House (one seat for the whole state)6 candidates

    Vermont's one vote in the U.S.

Vermont elects six statewide officers: governor, lieutenant governor, attorney general, secretary of state, treasurer and auditor of accounts. All six are on the ballot, along with the state's one U.S. House seat. No U.S. Senate seat is up. State judges are appointed by the governor, but county probate judges and assistant judges are elected.

U.S. House

Vermont's one vote in the U.S. House. House members write and vote on federal laws, taxes and spending, and every bill to raise taxes has to start in the House.
