Proposal 4: Equal protectionEqual protection under the law, written into the constitution

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): P.R. 4 (Proposal 4), placed on the ballot by the Legislature as Proposal 4 (legislative page: P.R. 4, "Declaration of rights; government for the people; equality of rights") ↗. Passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

In plain words

This would add a new Article 23 to the Vermont Constitution. It says everyone is guaranteed equal protection under the law and that the state cannot deny equal treatment because of race, ethnicity, sex, religion, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression or national origin. The last sentence says the article cannot be used to stop measures meant to give equal treatment and opportunity to groups that have been treated unfairly in the past. Backers say constitutional rights are harder to take away than laws; opponents say the last sentence favors some groups over others. (sevendaysvt.com ↗)

Biggest change

  • Lists the traits (including gender identity and gender expression) the state may not use to deny equal treatment. source: governor.vermont.gov (Governor's office)
  • The final sentence says the article cannot be read to stop measures meant to provide equal treatment and opportunity for groups that have historically faced discrimination. Vermont Human Rights Commission head Big Hartman says it is needed so future courts do not strike down diversity, equity and inclusion programs. source: sevendaysvt.com

Who it affects most: People in the groups named in the article, and state and local governments and schools that run programs aimed at equal opportunity.

Cost to the state: No cost estimate found. Unknown. Opponent Renee McGuinness says it would raise taxes and bring lawsuits; that is an opinion, not an estimate.

What your vote means

YES

The Vermont Constitution would guarantee equal protection under the law and bar the state from denying equal treatment for the listed reasons, and measures to promote equal opportunity for groups that faced discrimination would be protected.

NO

The constitution stays as it is, with no new equal protection article, and equal treatment rules stay in ordinary state and federal law.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

Why supporters want it

  • Sen. Nader Hashim (D-Windham) argued existing protections have not been enough: "A lot can change, for better or worse, over the course of several decades, or even a few years." source: sevendaysvt.com
  • Mark Hughes of the Vermont Racial Justice Alliance: "Equal protection must mean something in the places where people live." source: wcax.com

Why opponents object

  • Renee McGuinness, who runs noprop4vt.org and is spokesperson for the No on Proposal 4 PAC, objects to the final sentence and says it creates unequal rights: "If you're white, you're now lower on the hierarchy of who gets preferential treatment." source: sevendaysvt.com
  • In a VTDigger opinion piece, McGuinness cites law professor Peter Teachout saying "if you include certain classes in a closed list, you mean to exclude others," and says the amendment would invite lawsuits. source: vtdigger.org
