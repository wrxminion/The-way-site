Proposal 3: Right to collectively bargainA right for workers to organize and bargain together, in the constitution

In plain words

This would add a new Article 23 to the Vermont Constitution. It says workers have a right to form or join a union and to bargain as a group with their employer about pay, hours, working conditions and safety. It says no law can weaken that right. It also says no law can ban a contract between an employer and a union that requires workers to be union members to hold the job. Vermont's constitution has no such right today, so changing it back later would take another statewide vote. (sevendaysvt.com ↗)

Biggest change

  • Puts a worker's right to organize and bargain together into the constitution, so lawmakers could not remove it with an ordinary law. source: sevendaysvt.com
  • Bars "right-to-work" laws, which stop employers and unions from agreeing that workers must belong to the union. Ballotpedia says Illinois is the only other state with such a ban in its constitution. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Who it affects most: Workers who are in a union or want to join one, and employers that bargain with unions.

Cost to the state: No cost estimate found. Unknown.

What your vote means

YES

Workers get a right to organize and bargain together written into the Vermont Constitution, and lawmakers could not ban union-membership requirements in job contracts.

NO

The constitution stays as it is, and the right to organize and bargain together, and any ban on union-membership requirements, stays up to ordinary state law.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

Why supporters want it

  • Rep. Conor Casey (D-Montpelier): "It's largely a defensive measure against some of these attacks on workers by greedy corporate America." source: sevendaysvt.com
  • House Speaker Jill Krowinski (D-Chittenden 16) said the amendment "is about standing up for what is right" and "paving the way for future generations to come." source: news.ballotpedia.org

Why opponents object

  • Clara Morrison, executive director of the Right for Vermont Foundation, says the amendment removes workers' freedom to decline paying union dues in a union shop and takes the question away from lawmakers' ordinary debate. source: sevendaysvt.com
  • Rob Roper, a former chair of the Vermont Republican Party, said the proposal "effectively strips, in key cases, one's right to individually bargain on one's own behalf." source: news.ballotpedia.org
