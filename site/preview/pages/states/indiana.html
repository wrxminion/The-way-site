Voting in Indiana

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
Photo ID required

Without a government-issued photo ID, vote a provisional ballot and follow up with the county election board by noon 10 days after the election. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Mail voting needs a reason

Excuses: age 65+, absent from county, disability/illness, work shift during all voting hours, election/poll worker, religious belief, caregiver, no transportation, ACP participant, military/public safety officer Return deadline: received by 6 p.m. Nov 3 (Election Day).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Oct 6 - noon Nov 2 (in-person absentee, open to all voters without excuse)

After a conviction
Voting rights return when you leave prison

Only people in prison lose the vote; restored automatically on release.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Indiana.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Monday, October 5). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • Without an accepted ID, your ballot is provisional. It only counts if you make a second trip to an election office with ID, within a few days and during office hours.
  • You need an accepted reason to vote by mail. Excuses: age 65+, absent from county, disability/illness, work shift during all voting hours, election/poll worker, religious belief, caregiver, no transportation, ACP participant, military/public safety officer. Everyone else votes in person.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • Indiana polls are open 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. by state law, one of the earliest poll closing times in the nation.Source: WFYI (Indianapolis public media)
