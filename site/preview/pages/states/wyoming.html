Voting in Wyoming

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
By mail or in person

You can register and vote on the same day, including Election Day.

ID at the polls
ID required

Show an accepted ID (photo or certain non-photo IDs, e.g. Medicare card); without it, vote a provisional ballot and give ID to the county clerk by close of business the day after the election. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Anyone can vote by mail

Return deadline: received by 7 p.m. Nov 3 (close of polls).

Early in-person voting
Yes

In-person absentee Oct 6 (28 days before) - Nov 2

After a conviction
Some people lose the right to vote for good unless it's restored

First-time non-violent felony offenders regain rights automatically after completing their sentence; since 2023 (SB 120) others' rights are restored automatically five years after completing the sentence, while some violent offenses require the governor.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Wyoming.

  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • There's no online registration. Signing up means a paper form by mail or a trip to an office.
  • Without an accepted ID, your ballot is provisional. It only counts if you make a second trip to an election office with ID, within a few days and during office hours.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • After a felony conviction: First-time non-violent felony offenders regain rights automatically after completing their sentence; since 2023 (SB 120) others' rights are restored automatically five years after completing the sentence, while some violent offenses require the governor.
  • The Sentencing Project estimated in 2024 that 9,437 people in Wyoming (2.17% of the voting-eligible population, and 8.59% of Black voting-eligible residents) could not vote because of a felony conviction.Source: The Sentencing Project, Locked Out 2024
  • A 2025 Wyoming law requires documentary proof of U.S. citizenship and 30 days of Wyoming residency to register to vote, effective July 1, 2025.Source: Election Law Blog (quoting Wyoming Public Radio)
