Voting in Louisiana

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
Photo ID asked for

Without photo ID, sign an affidavit and vote a regular ballot (this affidavit option ends July 1, 2027 under 2026 SB 319). No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Mail voting needs a reason

Excuses: age 65+, temporarily absent from parish/state, offshore work, student/faculty outside parish, clergy outside parish, moved 100+ miles after registration closed, involuntary confinement, hospitalized, incarcerated (not felony), ACP participant, sequestered juror, disability/homebound Return deadline: received by 4:30 p.m. Nov 2 (day before Election Day); military/overseas/hospitalized by 8 p.m. Nov 3.

Early in-person voting
Yes

Oct 20 - Oct 27 (14 to 7 days before)

After a conviction
Voting rights return after the whole sentence, including parole or probation

People in prison lose the vote; those on probation or parole regain it if they have not been incarcerated in the last five years (2019 Act 636); otherwise restored on completing the sentence.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Louisiana.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Monday, October 5). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • You need an accepted reason to vote by mail. Excuses: age 65+, temporarily absent from parish/state, offshore work, student/faculty outside parish, clergy outside parish, moved 100+ miles after registration closed, involuntary confinement, hospitalized, incarcerated (not felony), ACP participant, sequestered juror, disability/homebound. Everyone else votes in person.
  • A mail ballot envelope needs a witness or notary signature, an extra step that is harder for people who live alone or are homebound.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • After a felony conviction: People in prison lose the vote; those on probation or parole regain it if they have not been incarcerated in the last five years (2019 Act 636); otherwise restored on completing the sentence.
  • A 2019 Leadership Conference Education Fund study found Louisiana had 126 fewer polling locations than in 2012.Source: WRNO (reporting on Leadership Conference Education Fund, Democracy Diverted)
