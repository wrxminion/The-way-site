Voting in Utah

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

You can register and vote on the same day, including Election Day.

ID at the polls
ID asked for

Show photo ID or two non-photo documents with name and address; without them, vote a provisional ballot that counts if identity is verified or ID is provided by the Monday after the election. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Every voter gets a ballot by mail

Ballot mailed automatically to every registered voter; no request needed; HB 300 (2025) phases out automatic mail ballots: from Jan 1, 2029 voters must opt in Return deadline: received by 8 p.m. Nov 3 (HB 300, 2025; postmarks no longer count).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Oct 20 - Oct 30 (14 days before through Friday before; counties may extend, CEIR lists through Nov 2)

After a conviction
Voting rights return when you leave prison

Only people in prison for a felony lose the vote; restored on release.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Utah.

  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • A 2026 law (HB 209) requires documentary proof of citizenship to vote in state races.
