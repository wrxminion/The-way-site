Voting in Mississippi

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
By mail or in person

You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
Photo ID required

Without photo ID, vote an affidavit ballot and show photo ID at the circuit clerk's office within 5 days after the election. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Mail voting needs a reason

Excuses: age 65+, temporarily/permanently disabled, absent from county on Election Day, work shift during polling hours (in person only), students/teachers away, others Return deadline: postmarked by Election Day and received by Nov 10 (5 business days after); upheld in Watson v. RNC.

Early in-person voting
No

No early in-person voting; excuse-qualified in-person absentee at circuit clerk's office until noon Oct 31

After a conviction
Some people lose the right to vote for good unless it's restored

People convicted of any of 23 listed 'disenfranchising crimes' (e.g. murder, theft, bribery, forgery) lose the vote for life unless pardoned or restored by a bill of the Legislature.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Mississippi.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Monday, October 5). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • There's no online registration. Signing up means a paper form by mail or a trip to an office.
  • Without an accepted ID, your ballot is provisional. It only counts if you make a second trip to an election office with ID, within a few days and during office hours.
  • You need an accepted reason to vote by mail. Excuses: age 65+, temporarily/permanently disabled, absent from county on Election Day, work shift during polling hours (in person only), students/teachers away, others. Everyone else votes in person.
  • A mail ballot envelope needs a witness or notary signature, an extra step that is harder for people who live alone or are homebound.
  • There's no early in-person voting open to everyone, so most people who vote in person have to do it on a Tuesday, a workday.
  • After a felony conviction: People convicted of any of 23 listed 'disenfranchising crimes' (e.g. murder, theft, bribery, forgery) lose the vote for life unless pardoned or restored by a bill of the Legislature.
  • The Sentencing Project estimated in 2024 that 68,731 people in Mississippi (3.08% of the voting-eligible population, and 5.37% of Black voting-eligible residents) could not vote because of a felony conviction.Source: The Sentencing Project, Locked Out 2024
  • Mississippi's constitution imposes a lifetime voting ban for convictions of certain crimes, including theft, forgery and bribery; a federal appeals court let it stand in 2024.Source: ABA Journal
