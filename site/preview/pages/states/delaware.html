Voting in Delaware

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

Many people are registered automatically when they deal with the DMV or certain state agencies. You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
ID asked for

Show photo ID or a document with name and address; without it, sign an affidavit affirming your identity and vote a regular ballot. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Mail voting needs a reason

Excuses: absent from state/county, illness/disability, work shift during all voting hours, religious belief, public service/military, caring for ill relative, vacation. Delaware's no-excuse mail law was struck by the state Supreme Court in 2024 (Albence v. Higgin), so an excuse is required Return deadline: received by 8 p.m. Nov 3 (close of polls).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Oct 24 - Nov 1 (at least 10 days, through Sunday before)

After a conviction
Some people lose the right to vote for good unless it's restored

Most people regain the vote automatically after completing their sentence (fines no longer required since 2016), but those convicted of murder/manslaughter, bribery or felony sexual offenses are permanently barred unless pardoned.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Delaware.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Saturday, October 10). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • You need an accepted reason to vote by mail. Excuses: absent from state/county, illness/disability, work shift during all voting hours, religious belief, public service/military, caring for ill relative, vacation. Delaware's no-excuse mail law was struck by the state Supreme Court in 2024 (Albence v. Higgin), so an excuse is required. Everyone else votes in person.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • After a felony conviction: Most people regain the vote automatically after completing their sentence (fines no longer required since 2016), but those convicted of murder/manslaughter, bribery or felony sexual offenses are permanently barred unless pardoned.
  • In October 2022 the Delaware Supreme Court struck down no-excuse vote-by-mail and same-day registration as conflicting with the state constitution, which limits absentee voting to listed excuses.Source: Delaware Public Media
