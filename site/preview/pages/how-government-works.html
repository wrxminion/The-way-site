How government works

Explore the jobs, powers and connections behind your ballot.

This hub is being built. We are writing each role page only from the relevant constitution, statute, charter or official duties page, and we will not publish a job description we cannot source. Until a role page exists here, it is listed as not yet researched, and nothing below describes what an office does.

What you will be able to do

  • Browse by level (federal, state, local), by branch (legislative, executive, judicial) or by role.
  • For each role: what it does, what it cannot decide alone, how someone gets the job, who works with it, and who funds, oversees, audits or reviews it, with a link to each official source and the date it was checked.
  • Follow how roles connect, with every connection labeled.
  • Return to your ballot from any role page.

How connections will be labeled

Government is not one chain of command. Branches, levels of government and independent elected offices relate in different ways, so each connection will carry one of these labels, and the labels will not be shown by color alone:

  • Elected by or appointed by: how a person gets the job.
  • Confirms: approves an appointment.
  • Legislates and administers: writes a rule, or carries it out.
  • Funds: provides money. Funding does not by itself give supervising authority.
  • Supervises: a real line of supervision, shown only where one exists.
  • Audits: checks records or spending.
  • Reviews decisions: for example a court review, which is not ordinary management.

Every connection will name its legal source, and local arrangements will be labeled by jurisdiction because they vary.
