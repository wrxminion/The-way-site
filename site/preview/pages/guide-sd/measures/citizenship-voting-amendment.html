In plain words

The state constitution already lists who may vote (including a citizenship and age rule) and lets the state bar people who are mentally incompetent or convicted of a felony. This amendment adds non-citizens to the list of people who are disqualified, to make clear that only U.S. citizens may vote in South Dakota elections. A 1996 federal law already bars non-citizens from voting in federal elections. (sdsos.gov (Secretary of State) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Adds non-U.S. citizens to the constitution's list of people disqualified from voting in any election or on any question put to the voters. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Who it affects most: All South Dakota voters, because it changes the constitution's voter rules. Opponents say it could open citizens' registrations to challenge under a new law; supporters say it changes no eligible citizen's rights.

Cost to the state: No fiscal note in the Secretary of State's pamphlet or handout. Cost to the state: no estimate published.

What your vote means

YES

The constitution says outright that people who are not U.S. citizens may not vote in South Dakota elections.

NO

The constitution stays as it is; voting is still limited to citizens by its current voter-qualification rule and by state law.

Our one-line summary. The Attorney General's official Yes and No effects are quoted above.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • The Legislature put this on the ballot: the Senate passed it 33-2 on Feb. 3, 2025 and the House 58-11 on March 6, 2025. This is the 15th statewide citizenship-voting measure in the country since 2018; the 14 earlier ones passed with an average of 73%. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Why supporters want it

  • Sen. Taffy Howard (R, District 34), in the official pamphlet: "Vote YES to make it unmistakably clear that only United States citizens may vote in South Dakota elections." Howard says it removes a gap in the constitution and changes no eligible citizen's rights. source: sdsos.gov (Secretary of State)

Why opponents object

  • Rep. Erik Muckey (D, District 15), in the official pamphlet: "Amendment J does not solve anything." Muckey says it is unnecessary and could let citizens' registrations be challenged under a new law. source: sdsos.gov (Secretary of State)

Who backs each side

  • For: Sen. Taffy Howard and the legislators who voted for it (all 32 Republican senators plus one Democrat; most House members). Against: Rep. Erik Muckey and the legislators who voted no. No campaign committee or money found. source: news.ballotpedia.org

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Secretary of State's official ballot question pamphlet ↗.

Constitutional Amendment J. Title: An Amendment to the South Dakota Constitution Clarifying that an Individual Must Be a Citizen of the United States to be Eligible to Vote.

Attorney General explanation: Article VII, § 2 of the South Dakota Constitution defines who is qualified to vote in state elections. Among these qualifications are age, residency, and United States citizenship requirements. Article VII, § 2 also establishes that individuals may be disqualified from voting because of mental incompetence or a felony conviction.

This constitutional amendment adds people who are not United States citizens to the list of individuals disqualified from voting, clarifying the existing requirement that only citizens may vote in state elections.

Vote “Yes” to adopt the amendment.
Vote “No” to leave the Constitution as it is.

The text of this constitutional amendment is one page long with two sections.
