HJR 4: Who can legalize drugsOnly the Legislature could legalize marijuana and other drugs

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): House Joint Resolution 4 (2025), House Joint Resolution 4 (2025). Passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

In plain words

Today Idaho voters can write a law themselves by gathering signatures (an initiative) and then voting on it. This amendment would take marijuana and other mind-altering ("psychoactive") drugs off that list. Only lawmakers could make them legal. (voteidaho.gov (Idaho Secretary of State) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Gives the Legislature sole power to legalize marijuana, narcotics or other psychoactive substances, which would block citizen initiatives on the subject. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Who it affects most: Groups that want to put marijuana or other drug measures on the ballot by petition, such as the medical cannabis campaign that tried for 2026.

Cost to the state: Unknown. The pamphlet summary we read says no fiscal impact statement was provided.

What your vote means

YES

Only the Legislature could make marijuana or other drugs legal; citizens could no longer do it by petition.

NO

Nothing changes: voters could still petition to put drug legalization on the ballot.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

  • The Legislature passed it in March 2025: 58-10 in the House and 29-6 in the Senate. Every Republican voted yes; Senate Democrats voted no. source: news.ballotpedia.org
  • A medical cannabis initiative (the Idaho Medical Cannabis Act) tried to make the 2026 ballot but did not qualify: it fell about 12,700 signatures short and met the signature goal in 13 of the 18 legislative districts it needed. source: idahonews.com

Why supporters want it

  • Pamphlet statement for it (read as a summary; no author named): "Decisions about legalizing marijuana, narcotics, and other psychoactive substances are too important to be made lightly." It also says the amendment would require public hearings and make legislators accountable for their votes. source: voteidaho.gov (Idaho Secretary of State)
  • Sen. Scott Grow (R), a sponsor: "Too many legislatures across this nation have sat back and just waited." source: news.ballotpedia.org

Why opponents object

  • Pamphlet statement against it (read as a summary; no author named): "The people are as capable of making good, careful decisions about drug policy as legislators are." It also says the Legislature could already amend or repeal a poorly written initiative. source: voteidaho.gov (Idaho Secretary of State)
  • Sen. James Ruchti (D): "The people have a right under the initiative and the referendum process to weigh in on these issues." source: news.ballotpedia.org

Who backs each side

  • For: the Republican majority in the Legislature, which placed it on the ballot (sponsor Sen. Scott Grow). Against: Senate Democrats voted no. No public record found yet of campaign committees or money for either side. source: news.ballotpedia.org
