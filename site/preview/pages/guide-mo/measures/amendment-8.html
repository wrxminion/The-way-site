Amendment 8: County sheriffsPut elected county sheriffs in the constitution; limit how they can be removed

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): CCS HCS SS SJR 87 (2026), placed on the ballot by the Legislature as CCS HCS SS SJR 87, 103rd General Assembly, Second Regular Session ↗. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Secretary of State 2026 ballot measures page ↗

In plain words

Today state laws, not the constitution, say counties elect a sheriff every four years and set the sheriff's duties. This amendment moves those rules into the constitution, so lawmakers could not change them without another statewide vote. It also says an elected sheriff can be removed only by a court case the Attorney General starts. St. Louis City, St. Louis County and St. Charles County are left out. (ky3.com ↗)

Biggest change

  • Requires counties to elect a sheriff every four years and moves sheriffs' duties from state law into the constitution. source: sos.mo.gov (Missouri Secretary of State)
  • An elected sheriff may be removed only through a writ of quo warranto started by the Attorney General. source: sos.mo.gov (Missouri Secretary of State)
  • Exempts St. Louis City, St. Louis County and St. Charles County. source: ky3.com

Who it affects most: County sheriffs and voters in the other counties covered.

Cost to the state: State and local governments estimate no costs or savings. It does not raise or lower taxes.

What your vote means

YES

The elected county sheriff and the sheriff's duties go into the constitution, and a sheriff can be removed only by a court case the Attorney General brings.

NO

Sheriffs' election, duties and removal stay set by state law, which lawmakers can change.

Our one-line summary. Missouri does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Missouri Sheriffs United and the Missouri Sheriffs Association worked with lawmakers, including Rep. Bennie Cook (R-Houston), for several years on the amendment. Nearly all Democratic lawmakers voted against it. source: kctv5.com

Why supporters want it

  • Rep. Bennie Cook (R-Houston): "a local control issue; this is a back-the-blue piece, in my opinion." source: ky3.com
  • Kevin Merritt, executive director of Missouri Sheriffs United, says lawmakers could now abolish the office and create a state police force; on concerns about power: "There's no power grab there, that's just a big misunderstanding." source: kctv5.com

Why opponents object

  • Democratic Rep. Mark Boyko says it gives sheriffs too much power: "If Amendment 8 passes, the only way a sheriff can be removed is if a prosecutor goes to court and gets an order," which Boyko says could take months or years. source: kctv5.com

Who backs each side

  • For: Missouri Sheriffs United and the Missouri Sheriffs Association. Against: most Democratic lawmakers voted no. No campaign committees or spending found for either side. source: kctv5.com

What you will see on your ballot

The official ballot title, word for word from the Secretary of State's official ballot measure list ↗.

Shall the Missouri Constitution be amended to support law enforcement by preserving the right of citizens to elect a county sheriff, prohibiting the removal of a county sheriff except by a writ of quo warranto, and recognizing the office of sheriff as part of the administration of justice?
