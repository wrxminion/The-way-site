In plain words

The state's 2008 water bond money is nearly used up. Issue 4 would let the state borrow up to $500 million more, mostly to make loans and grants to local water, sewer and irrigation projects. (uaex.uada.edu ↗)

Example

Illustration only: if the commission sold the full $60 million allowed in one two-year period, that money could fund loans to several city water or sewer systems, which repay the loans over time; the state still owes the bond payments either way. (arkleg.state.ar.us ↗)

Biggest change

  • Up to $500 million in general obligation bonds, backed by the state's full faith and credit. source: arkleg.state.ar.us
  • No more than $165 million for irrigation facilities. source: arkleg.state.ar.us
  • No more than $60 million in any two-year period beginning July 1 of odd-numbered years without legislative approval. source: arkleg.state.ar.us
  • Bonds may run up to 35 years; the commission must prepare a plan reviewed by the governor, chief fiscal officer and legislators before selling bonds. source: uaex.uada.edu

Who it affects most: Cities, rural water systems, farmers using irrigation, and state taxpayers who back the bonds.

Cost to the state: Up to $500 million in state debt plus interest, repaid over up to 35 years. Debt payments get first claim on state general revenue; loan repayments also help pay debt. (UA Extension)

What your vote means

YES

A YES (FOR) vote lets the Arkansas Natural Resources Commission issue up to $500 million in state bonds, repaid over up to 35 years, starting July 1, 2027.

NO

A NO (AGAINST) vote means no new bonds under this act; the legislature would need to use general revenue or other money for these water programs and federal matches.

Our one-line summary. Arkansas does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with A majority of the voters voting on the question (Act 578, sec. 15-20-1519(a))..

  • Arkansas General Assembly (Act 578 of 2025). Sponsors: Sen. Bart Hester and Rep. Howard Beaty Jr. source: arkleg.state.ar.us
  • Voters approved a $300 million water bond in 2008 by 631,767 to 332,507; its last funds are being used. source: uaex.uada.edu
  • UA Extension cites an EPA-identified need of more than $13 billion for Arkansas drinking water, sewer and stormwater projects. source: uaex.uada.edu
  • A Talk Business & Politics/Hendrix College poll in August 2026 found 66% for. source: kuaf.com

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Arkansas General Assembly ↗.

Arkansas Water, Waste Disposal, and Pollution Abatement Facilities Financing Act of 2025.

Shall the Arkansas Natural Resources Commission be authorized to issue general obligation bonds under the authority of the Arkansas Water, Waste Disposal, and Pollution Abatement Facilities Financing Act of 2025, for the financing and refinancing of the development of water, waste disposal, water pollution control, abatement and prevention, drainage, irrigation, flood control, and wetlands and aquatic resources projects to serve the citizens of the State of Arkansas, in total principal amount not to exceed five hundred million dollars ($500,000,000), with no more than one hundred sixty-five million dollars ($165,000,000) in total principal amount of such bonds to be issued to finance and refinance the development of irrigation facilities, in series from time to time in principal amounts not to exceed, without prior approval of the General Assembly, sixty million dollars ($60,000,000) in any two-year period beginning on July 1 of each odd-numbered year, which bonds shall be secured by a pledge of the full faith and credit of the State of Arkansas?
FOR Issuance of State of Arkansas Water, Waste Disposal, and Pollution Abatement Facilities general obligation bonds.
AGAINST Issuance of State of Arkansas Water, Waste Disposal, and Pollution

Copied word for word from the Secretary of State's notice "Constitutional Amendment (or Other Measure) Referred to the People by the General Assembly" for Issue No. 4, page 1 (ar_Issue_No_4_Act_578_of_2026.pdf).
