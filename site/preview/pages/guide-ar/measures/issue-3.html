issue 3Economic development districts

Legislatively referred constitutional amendment: Issue 3 (SJR 15 of 2025), placed on the ballot by the Legislature as Issue 3 (SJR 15 of 2025) ↗. Passes with A majority of the votes cast on the issue..

Read the full text of the measure: official text ↗

In plain words

Issue 3 gives the legislature power to create a new kind of local district to attract projects like housing, stores and infrastructure. Details would come later in new laws. Property in a district would leave the regular tax rolls but could be taxed by the district. (uaex.uada.edu ↗)

Example

Illustration only: if a store site worth $1 million sits inside a district, it would no longer pay the usual school and county property taxes on that value; the district could charge its own tax or fee on it to repay district bonds. Actual amounts would depend on future laws. (uaex.uada.edu ↗)

Biggest change

  • Lets the legislature create programs, loans and grants of public money for economic development, jobs, transportation and commerce. source: arkleg.state.ar.us
  • Lets the legislature allow economic development districts that can issue bonds; those bonds do not count toward certain debt limits. source: arkleg.state.ar.us
  • Property in a district is generally exempt from property taxes except charges the district levies. source: arkleg.state.ar.us
  • Bonds backed by local property taxes still need local voter approval. source: senate.arkansas.gov
  • Overrides conflicting constitutional provisions, laws and court rulings; takes effect Jan. 1, 2027. source: arkleg.state.ar.us

Who it affects most: Cities, counties, school districts, businesses and developers.

Cost to the state: No dollar estimate. UA Extension says property in districts would leave school, county and city tax rolls, and that costs and bonds depend on future laws. SB 647 of 2025, an example implementing bill, died without action.

What your vote means

YES

A YES (FOR) vote lets the legislature pass laws creating economic development districts and funding programs, starting Jan. 1, 2027; no district is created by the vote itself.

NO

A NO (AGAINST) vote keeps current constitutional limits; cities and counties keep only the development tools they have now (such as tax increment financing and existing bonds).

Our one-line summary. Arkansas does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with A majority of the votes cast on the issue..

  • Arkansas General Assembly (SJR 15, 2025 regular session). Sponsors: Sen. Jonathan Dismang and Rep. Howard Beaty. source: arkleg.state.ar.us
  • Passed by the legislature in April 2025 as SJR 15. It builds on a 2016 amendment that allowed public money for private economic development. source: kuaf.com
  • A Talk Business & Politics/Hendrix College poll in August 2026 found 37% for, 25% against, 38% undecided. source: kuaf.com
  • Sponsor Sen. Jonathan Dismang and local officials presented the measure to a legislative committee in August 2026; local officials said border communities lose projects to nearby states. source: senate.arkansas.gov
