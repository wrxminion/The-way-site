Ballot Measure · every Alaska voter

Ballot Measure 2Go back to party primaries and end ranked-choice voting

Citizen initiative (state law): 24ESEG, placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Initiative 24ESEG). Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Division of Elections measure pages and Official Election Pamphlet ↗

In plain words

Since 2020, Alaska has used one primary where every candidate is on the same ballot. The top four finishers move on, and in November voters rank them. This measure would end that. Each party would hold its own primary, and in November voters would pick just one candidate. It would also repeal campaign rules passed with the 2020 measure, including limits on gifts to governor and lieutenant governor teams and rules about showing the true source of money. (elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Ends the open primary where all candidates appear on one ballot, and brings back political party primaries. The winner of each party primary is that party’s nominee. source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Ends ranked-choice general elections: voters vote for only one candidate in each race, and the candidate with the most votes wins. source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Candidates for governor and lieutenant governor run separately in party primaries, then together in the general election. source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Repeals campaign finance rules passed in 2020, including limits and disclosure rules for digital ads, out-of-state donations, undisclosed donations and the true source of donations. source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Brings back party petitions and special runoff elections. source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)

Who it affects most: Every voter, because it changes how primaries and the November ballot work, and candidates and campaign donors, because it changes the money rules.

Cost to the state: The state’s own cost statement estimates $2,630,958 in total. That is $6,975 for the Alaska Public Offices Commission, $15,000 for the Lieutenant Governor’s office and $2,608,983 for the Division of Elections. Most of the Division’s part, $2,500,000, is a public education campaign to tell candidates, voters and election workers about the new election rules.

What your vote means

YES

Alaska goes back to party primaries and one-choice general elections, and several 2020 campaign-finance rules are repealed.

NO

Alaska keeps the open top-four primary and ranked-choice voting in November, and the 2020 campaign-finance rules stay.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Voters passed ranked-choice voting, the open top-four primary and new campaign-disclosure rules in 2020 (Ballot Measure 2). A repeal measure in 2024 failed by about 0.24%, or 664 votes. source: thealaskacurrent.com
  • The Alaska Supreme Court ruled on Aug. 28, 2026 that the state's ballot summary is 'true and impartial' but ordered one addition: governor and lieutenant governor candidates would run separately in primaries. source: alaskasnewssource.com
  • Repeal Now, the campaign for the measure, and a group represented by attorney Scott Kendall (a sponsor of the 2020 measure) both sued over the summary wording. The court rejected most of Repeal Now's requested changes. source: alaskasnewssource.com

Why supporters want it

  • Statement in support, signed by former Lt. Governors Mead Treadwell, Craig Campbell and Loren Leman: “Voting Yes on Ballot Measure 2 restores Alaska’s elections to how they were before 2020.” source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Same statement: “Ranked choice ballots are a complicated mess. Ending ranked choice voting will return to simple, One Person, One Vote elections.” source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Same statement: “Yes on 2 will defeat the largest source of dark money Alaska has ever seen.” source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Rep. Kevin McCabe (Republican) says party-controlled primaries act as a useful filter for candidates. source: thealaskacurrent.com
  • Bernadette Wilson, a Republican candidate for governor, is one of three main sponsors of the repeal effort, per the campaign site. No statement of reasons was quoted in the sources read. source: bernadetteforgovernor.com
  • Rep. Jamie Allard (Republican) announced in July 2026 a vote for the measure, per Wikipedia; no reason was quoted. source: en.wikipedia.org

Why opponents object

  • Statement in opposition, signed by Nicole Borromeo (President, ANCSA Regional Association), Sen. Cathy Giessel (R) and Joelle Hall (President, Alaska AFL-CIO): “Measure 2 would flood Alaska with untraceable campaign cash, take away our freedom to vote for any candidate we choose, and allow politicians to win without a majority of the vote.” source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Same statement: “Ballot Measure 2 also gives political party bosses the right to deny unaffiliated voters the ability to vote in primary elections. 60%+ of Alaskan voters are unaffiliated.” source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • League of Women Voters of Alaska urges a NO vote. source: en.wikipedia.org
  • First Alaskans Institute's board opposes the measure. source: en.wikipedia.org
  • Attorney Scott Kendall, a sponsor of the 2020 measure, argued in court that the ballot summary should state more clearly that each party could close its primary to unaffiliated voters. source: alaskasnewssource.com

Who backs each side

  • For: the pamphlet’s statement in support is signed by former Lt. Governors Mead Treadwell, Craig Campbell and Loren Leman. Against: the statement in opposition is signed by Nicole Borromeo (ANCSA Regional Association), Sen. Cathy Giessel (R) and Joelle Hall (Alaska AFL-CIO). What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Division of Elections' official ballot measure pages ↗.

24ESEG - An Act Restoring Political Party Primaries, Ending Ranked-Choice General Elections, and Repealing Recent Campaign Finance Laws

This act would get rid of open primary elections, where all candidates appear on one ballot. It would also get rid of ranked-choice general elections. It would bring back political party primaries and single-choice general elections. It would also repeal certain campaign finance rules that were added by a 2020 ballot measure.

Elections would occur as they did before open primaries and ranked choice voting. In the primary election, voters would choose a party’s ballot, unless prohibited by the party’s bylaws. They would only vote for candidates on that ballot. The winning candidate from each primary would be the party’s nominee. In the general election, voters would vote for only one candidate in each race. The candidate with the most votes would win. Party petitions, special runoff elections, and other processes would return. Candidates for governor and lieutenant governor would run separately in political party primaries and then together in the general election.

This act would also repeal campaign finance rules that were passed in 2020. It would remove the limits on donations to joint campaigns for governor and lieutenant governor. It would remove limits and disclosure rules under current law, including for digital ads, out-of-state donations, undisclosed donations, and the true source of donations. It would change the meaning of a campaign expenditure and remove some fines.
