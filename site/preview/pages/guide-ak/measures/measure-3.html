Ballot Measure 3Say in the law that only U.S. citizens may vote

Citizen initiative (state law): 25USCV, placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Initiative 25USCV (United States Citizens Voter Act)). Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Division of Elections measure pages and Official Election Pamphlet ↗

In plain words

Alaska law already limits voting to U.S. citizens, and sponsors and critics agree on that. This measure changes the wording to say that ONLY citizens may vote. Supporters say the new wording is clearer. Critics say it changes nothing in practice. The measure does not add ID rules, paperwork or registration steps, according to campaign materials. (elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Says that only those who are United States citizens and meet the other requirements may vote. source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Does not change the requirements to vote (the state’s own summary). source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)

Who it affects most: No voter is described as affected directly. Supporters say it matters for how courts or lawmakers read the rule in the future.

Cost to the state: The state’s own cost statement estimates $114,050 in total. That is $15,000 for the Lieutenant Governor’s office and $99,050 for the Division of Elections: $82,443 to review the petition, $1,605 to print voter booklets and $15,002 to translate the measure.

What your vote means

YES

The voting law would say 'Only a person who is a citizen of the United States' may vote, instead of 'a person may vote'.

NO

The voting law keeps its current wording; citizens can vote, and noncitizens already cannot.

Our one-line summary. Alaska does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Lt. Gov. Nancy Dahlstrom announced on March 17, 2026 that 34,944 valid signatures across 40 House districts had been verified. The minimum was 34,098 across 30 districts. source: news.ballotpedia.org
  • Supporters cite a 2023 state case in which a group of American Samoans (U.S. nationals, not citizens) were charged with voter misconduct in Whittier. Alaska does not currently allow noncitizen voting. source: alaskawatchman.com
  • Similar citizenship-only measures are on the ballot in other states in 2026 (Ballotpedia News counts Alaska as the fifth). source: news.ballotpedia.org

Why supporters want it

  • Statement in support, by the sponsors, former Sen. John Coghill Jr., former House Speaker Charles M. Chenault and former Sen. Joshua C. Revak: “Current law says citizens may vote, but it does not specifically state that only citizens may vote. This measure closes that gap and makes Alaska law unmistakably clear.” source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)
  • Former state Sen. John Coghill, a sponsor, says the clearer wording would make plain that U.S. nationals cannot vote in Alaska. source: alaskawatchman.com
  • Former state Sen. John Coghill and former House Speaker Mike Chenault told the Anchorage Daily News the measure sends a message that Alaska does not tolerate noncitizen voting (as reported by The Alaska Current). source: thealaskacurrent.com
  • The Alaskans for Citizen Voting campaign says a clear rule on who may vote is a stabilizing safeguard. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Why opponents object

  • State Sen. Bill Wielechowski (Democrat): the measure 'has no realistic impact on the current law at all' (Wielechowski also called it anti-immigrant), per The Alaska Current. source: thealaskacurrent.com
  • Journalist Stan Jones argues Alaska already requires voters to be citizens and that no bill, court case or town is challenging that rule. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Who backs each side

  • For: the initiative’s sponsors, John Coghill, Mike Chenault and Josh Revak, wrote the pamphlet’s statement in support. Against: the pamphlet says no one submitted a statement in opposition. Campaign committees and money are on file with the Alaska Public Offices Commission; we have not yet read those reports. source: elections.alaska.gov (Division of Elections)

25USCV - An Act Addressing Voter Qualification

Alaska law requires that someone must be a United States citizen and meet other requirements to vote. This act would specify that only those who are United States citizens and meet the other requirements may vote. This act would not change the requirements to vote.
