Amendment 1A bigger rainy-day fund

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Ballot No. 1, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as House Joint Resolution 5019 (2025) ↗. Passes with at least 60% of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Division of Elections full-text file (PDF) ↗

In plain words

The Budget Stabilization Fund is Florida's savings account for emergencies, often called the rainy-day fund. Amendment 1 lets it grow much bigger and makes lawmakers add money to it every year until it does. (ballot title ↗)

Example

Biggest change

  • Lawmakers would have to move up to $750 million a year into the emergency fund until it reaches the new, higher limit. They can skip the deposit in a year the fund is tapped for a budget shortfall or an emergency, or, by a two-thirds vote of each chamber (at most once every five years), when they decide a critical state need comes first. source: flsenate.gov
  • The most the fund can hold goes from 10% to 25% of the general tax money the state collects in a year. source: initiativepetitions.dos.fl.gov
  • Lawmakers could also take money out for a one-time "critical state need", but only by a two-thirds vote of each chamber, only when the fund holds more than 15%, and never leaving it below 10%. source: flsenate.gov

Who it affects most: The state budget. Money set aside for the fund is money that can't be spent that year on services or tax cuts.

Cost to the state: No new tax. It moves up to $750 million a year from spending into savings.

What your vote means

YES

The state must save up to $750 million a year until its emergency fund holds up to 25% of a year's general tax money, instead of the 10% limit today.

NO

The emergency fund stays limited to 10% of a year's general tax money, and the new yearly deposit rule doesn't start.

Our one-line summary. Florida does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with 60% of the votes cast on it.

  • Florida last drew on the fund in 2008, during the Great Recession. The Legislature passed the proposal 100-1 in the House and 29-4 in the Senate in 2025. source: wusf.org

Why supporters want it

  • Then-House Speaker Daniel Perez (R): "This is just a preparation of being prepared for the unexpected." He added, "The state of Florida was not prepared for that recession." source: wusf.org
  • The Republican Party of Florida endorsed it, saying it "will help strengthen Florida's rainy day fund" so the state can handle storms and downturns "without raising taxes or raiding the budget." source: wusf.org

Why opponents object

  • Gov. Ron DeSantis (R): "This is a foolish constitutional amendment proposal." He said, "We already have a maxed out rainy day fund and have run the largest surpluses in state history," and urged a NO vote, saying extra money should go to lower taxes instead. source: wusf.org

Who backs each side

  • For: the Legislature's leaders and the Republican Party of Florida. Against: Gov. Ron DeSantis. We have not found a campaign committee or reported campaign money for either side. source: wusf.org

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Division of Elections' full-text file ↗.

BUDGET STABILIZATION FUND.—

Proposing an amendment to the State Constitution to increase the amount of funds that may be retained in the budget stabilization fund from 10% to 25% of general revenue collections, require the legislature to transfer the lesser of $750 million or the amount required to reach 25% of the general revenue collections each year unless certain conditions are met, and allow the legislature to withdraw funds for critical state needs.
