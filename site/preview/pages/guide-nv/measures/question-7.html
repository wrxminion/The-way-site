question 7Voter ID for in-person and mail voting

Constitutional amendment (initiative, second vote): State Question No. 7: Amendment to the Nevada Constitution (Initiative Petition C-02-2023), placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (State Question No. 7: Amendment to the Nevada Constitution (Initiative Petition C-02-2023)). Passes with Majority YES. This is the second and final vote: it passed in 2024 and must pass again in 2026 to become part of the Nevada Constitution..

In plain words

Question 7 would add voter ID rules to the Nevada Constitution. In-person voters would show a photo ID. Mail voters would write part of an ID number. (clarkcountynv.gov ↗)

Biggest change

  • In-person voters must show a photo ID that is current or expired no more than four years. Voters over 70 may use an expired ID of any age if it is otherwise valid. source: clarkcountynv.gov
  • Mail voters must give the last four digits of their Nevada driver's license number. If they have none, the last four digits of their Social Security number. If they have neither, the number the county clerk gave them when they registered. source: clarkcountynv.gov
  • The fiscal note assumes a free ID would be offered to voters who lack one, but the measure does not say which agency would provide it. source: clarkcountynv.gov
  • If approved, the Legislature could pass related laws in 2027, and the changes would apply in the 2028 election cycle. source: clarkcountynv.gov

Who it affects most: All voters, especially those without a current photo ID; county election offices.

Cost to the state: About $6,750 for computer system changes, needed before 2028. The cost of giving ID to voters who lack one "cannot be determined with any reasonable degree of certainty."

What your vote means

YES

A YES vote puts a voter ID rule in the Nevada Constitution. The Legislature would set details in 2027, and the rule would apply starting with the 2028 elections.

NO

A NO vote leaves the Nevada Constitution unchanged. Most voters would keep voting without showing ID, as they do now.

Our one-line summary. Nevada does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with Majority YES. This is the second and final vote: it passed in 2024 and must pass again in 2026 to become part of the Nevada Constitution..

  • Citizen initiative petition (C-02-2023). Backed by Repair the Vote (David Gibbs). Voters approved it the first time in Nov 2024. source: clarkcountynv.gov
  • Voters approved Question 7 the first time in Nov 2024 by more than 46 percentage points. source: news3lv.com
  • In 2025 the Legislature passed a voter ID bill (AB 499) as part of a deal, but Gov. Lombardo vetoed it. source: kolotv.com
  • Secretary of State Aguilar has said the office is preparing to put voter ID in place if it passes. source: news3lv.com
