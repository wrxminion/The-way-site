Question 1: More time for the Senate to act on judgesMore time for the state Senate to confirm judges

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Senate Bill 121 (2025), placed on the ballot by the Legislature as Senate Bill 121 (2025) ↗. Passes with more yes votes than no votes, and yes votes equal to at least half of all ballots cast (blank ballots count against it).

Read the full text of the measure: Office of Elections 2026 General Election Voter Guide (PDF) ↗

In plain words

In Hawaii, judges are picked, not elected. The governor (or, for some courts, the chief justice) picks from a list, and the state Senate has 30 days to say no. If it does nothing, the judge is approved. Most of the year the Senate is not in session, so it often has to call a special session to meet that deadline. This amendment gives the Senate 60 days for picks made from April 1 to December 31. (elections.hawaii.gov ↗)

Biggest change

  • For judges picked between April 1 and December 31, the Senate gets 60 days instead of 30 to reject the pick. If it does not reject the pick in time, the pick is approved. source: data.capitol.hawaii.gov

Who it affects most: State senators, people picked to be judges, and courts waiting for an open judge seat to be filled.

Cost to the state: No official cost estimate. The Senate Judiciary Committee said "each special session costs approximately $5,000 for airfare and per diem for neighbor island senators," plus senators' and staff time. Fewer special sessions could save some of that.

What your vote means

YES

The Senate gets 60 days instead of 30 to act on judges picked between April 1 and December 31.

NO

The Senate keeps 30 days to act on every judge pick, all year.

Our one-line summary. Hawaii does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with more yes votes than no votes, and yes votes equal to at least half of all ballots cast (a blank vote on it works like a no).

  • The Legislature passed SB 121 in 2025 to put this question on the 2026 ballot. The regular session ends in spring, so judges picked later in the year often need a special session to meet the 30-day deadline. source: alohastatedaily.com
  • Hawaii's judges are not elected. They are appointed from a list of nominees sent by the Judicial Selection Commission and confirmed by the Senate. source: data.capitol.hawaii.gov

Why supporters want it

  • The Senate Judiciary Committee, chaired by Sen. Karl Rhoads, wrote that "this measure will provide flexibility in the scheduling of special sessions." source: data.capitol.hawaii.gov

Why opponents object

  • Rodney A. Maile, Administrative Director of the Courts, wrote in testimony that the change "could leave judicial vacancies unfilled for an extended period of time, depending on when a vacancy occurs." source: alohastatedaily.com

Who backs each side

  • For: the state Legislature, which placed it on the ballot; Sen. Karl Rhoads led it. Concerns: the Judiciary (Administrative Director of the Courts). No campaign committee or money for either side found in public reporting. source: alohastatedaily.com

The official ballot title, word for word from the Office of Elections' official voter guide ↗.

Shall the Constitution of the State of Hawaii be amended to allow the Senate more time to consider and act on the appointments of justices and judges, by increasing the timeframe from thirty days to sixty days for appointments made between April 1 and December 31 when the senate is not in regular session or is about to adjourn the regular session?
