Question 2: Fill-ins on the judge-discipline commissionLets the Commission on Judicial Disabilities use temporary fill-in members

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Chapter 878 of 2026 (Senate Bill 933), placed on the ballot by the Legislature as Senate Bill 933 (Chapter 878 of 2026) ↗. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: State Board of Elections file (PDF) ↗

In plain words

The Commission on Judicial Disabilities looks into complaints about Maryland judges. Today, open seats are filled only when the governor appoints someone and the state Senate agrees. This amendment lets the commission's chair call back a former member to fill in for a while, or ask the governor to name a temporary fill-in or keep a current member on until a replacement is named. A fill-in must meet the same rules as the member being replaced, and no one the Senate already rejected for the commission can serve. (news.ballotpedia.org ↗)

Biggest change

  • Creates a way to seat temporary substitute members on the Commission on Judicial Disabilities when a member is recused, disqualified, or has a term expire without a replacement. source: thebanner.com

Who it affects most: People who file complaints about judges, and judges under review, since cases could move ahead without waiting for open seats to be filled.

Cost to the state: Unknown. No cost figure found; see the fiscal and policy note for SB 933.

What your vote means

YES

The judge-discipline commission can use temporary fill-in members when seats are empty or members step aside.

NO

The commission keeps having no fill-in process; empty seats wait for a governor's appointment approved by the Senate.

Our one-line summary. Maryland does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • The commission was created by a 1966 constitutional amendment. Open seats are filled by the governor with the Senate's consent. source: news.ballotpedia.org
  • This replaces a similar amendment passed in 2025 (HB 788). The only difference: a person the Senate already rejected for the commission cannot serve as a fill-in. It passed unanimously (Senate 41-0, House 134-0). The sponsor was Sen. Will Smith, chair of the Senate Judicial Proceedings Committee. source: mgaleg.maryland.gov

Why supporters want it

  • Shaoli Sarkar, advocacy director of the Maryland State Bar Association, said the 2025 version would let the commission "continue its work without delay due to member recusals or vacancies." source: news.ballotpedia.org

Why opponents object

  • No public statement found.

Who backs each side

  • For: the General Assembly passed it with no votes against, and the Maryland State Bar Association backed the earlier version. Against: no organized opposition found. No public record found of a campaign committee or money for either side. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Question 2
Constitutional Amendment
(Ch. 878 of the 2026 Legislative Session)
Temporary Appointments on the Commission on Judicial Disabilities

Authorizing temporary substitute appointments to the Commission on Judicial Disabilities when a member is recused or disqualified, or when a member's term expires. In these situations, the Commission Chair may recall a former member of the Commission, or the Governor, at the request of the Commission Chair, may appoint a temporary substitute member or extend the term of a current member until a permanent replacement is appointed. Only individuals who meet the constitutional eligibility requirements may be recalled or appointed to the Commission.

A vote FOR this amendment means that temporary appointments to the Commission are allowed if a member is recused, disqualified, or if a member's term ends without a replacement. The Commission Chair can temporarily fill an opening on the Commission with a former member. The Governor can, at the request of the Commission Chair, pick a temporary substitute to fill an opening on the Commission or extend the term of a current member until a replacement is appointed. Only individuals who meet the constitutional eligibility requirements may be recalled or appointed to the Commission.

A vote AGAINST the amendment means that current membership rules for the Commission stay the same. Temporary appointments are not allowed if a member is recused, disqualified, or if a member's term ends.

(Amending Article IV of the Maryland Constitution)
