Amendment 3: A state fund for modern 911Lets lawmakers put existing 911 money in a fund used only for a modern statewide 911 system

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): House Resolution 1243 (2026), House Resolution 1243 (Ga. L. 2026, p. 1109). Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Secretary of State's 2026 constitutional amendments booklet (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Georgia's 911 system has changed little since 1968. "Next Generation 911" is an internet-based system that would let callers send texts, photos and video, and let 911 centers pass calls to each other. Normally Georgia's constitution does not let lawmakers lock money into one use. This amendment lets them set up a fund that can be spent only on 911, and that does not go back to the general budget at the end of the year (it is "nonlapsing"). Lawmakers would pass a later law saying what money goes in. (ajc.com ↗)

Biggest change

  • Creates a fund whose money can only go to building, keeping up and running 911 systems in Georgia, and that does not lapse at the end of the budget year. source: ballotpedia.org
  • The 911 fee stays at $1.50 a month; the measure "would not create a new 911 fee." source: gacities.com

Who it affects most: Anyone who calls 911, and the city and county 911 centers that answer the calls.

Cost to the state: No new tax or fee. Lawmakers put $5.7 million in the amended 2025 budget as start-up money for the project. How much would go into the fund later is up to a future law.

What your vote means

YES

Lawmakers may set up a state fund, paid from existing 911 money, that can be spent only on 911 systems.

NO

No dedicated 911 fund; 911 money stays as it is handled today.

Our one-line summary. Georgia does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Lawmakers have talked about a statewide Next Generation 911 system for years, but the money was uncertain. A House study committee recommended a steady source by moving a local 911 charge into the fund. source: theaugustapress.com
  • The House passed it 162-0 on March 4, 2026. In the Senate it first fell short (31-14 on March 19) when Democrats voted no to protest being left out of talks, then passed 49-0 on March 31. source: ballotpedia.org
  • Sen. Derek Mallow (D-Savannah) said Democrats' first no vote was about not being consulted, not about 911: until "we can find a way to work together in this chamber, I'm going to vote no." source: theaugustapress.com

Why supporters want it

  • Rep. Chuck Martin (R-Alpharetta): "It makes the first responders safer and gives them the ability to react to the situation." source: capitol-beat.org
  • Senate Minority Leader Harold Jones (D-Augusta), on slow 911 answers: "We hear many stories: They call 911, a person may not answer." source: capitol-beat.org

Why opponents object

  • No public statement found.

Who backs each side

  • For: both parties in the General Assembly (unanimous final votes). Against: no organized opposition found. The Georgia Municipal Association says it is watching the measure and has not taken a position. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: gacities.com
