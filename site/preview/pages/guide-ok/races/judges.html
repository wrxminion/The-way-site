Judges on the retention ballot

Appellate judges in Oklahoma don't run against opponents. Voters decide whether each one stays in office (yes or no).

  • Appellate judges on the retention ballot have no opponents and no party label; voters answer yes or no on whether each judge should be retained. source: okpolicy.org
  • A judge needs a simple majority of yes votes to be retained. source: okpolicy.org
  • Retained judges serve six-year terms; terms are staggered so some appellate judges are on the ballot every even-numbered year. source: okpolicy.org
  • If a judge is not retained, the office becomes vacant and the governor appoints a replacement. source: okpolicy.org
  • A newly appointed appellate judge stands for retention at the next general election after serving one year. source: ballotpedia.org
  • Under the Code of Judicial Conduct, appellate judges cannot raise campaign funds or set up campaign committees. source: okpolicy.org
  • The State Election Board list shows the same nine retention questions in the county ballot listings checked (statewide). source: hosting.okelections.gov

What each court does

  • Supreme Court: Oklahoma's highest court for everything except criminal cases: lawsuits, family and business disputes, and challenges to state laws. Its rulings are final on state law in those cases, and it oversees all state courts. Nine justices. source: ballotpedia.org
  • Court of Criminal Appeals: Oklahoma's highest court for criminal cases. It has the final word on state criminal appeals, including death penalty cases. Five judges. source: ballotpedia.org
  • Court of Civil Appeals: Hears most appeals in non-criminal cases, which the Supreme Court sends to it. The Supreme Court can review its decisions. source: ballotpedia.org
