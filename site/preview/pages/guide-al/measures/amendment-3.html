Amendment 3: Pledge of Allegiance and student-led prayerDaily Pledge of Allegiance and student-led prayer in public schools

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Act 2026-379, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as House Bill 511 (Act 2026-379) ↗. Passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Fair Ballot Commission statements (PDF) ↗

In plain words

The constitution has no rule today about the Pledge or student prayer in public schools. This amendment would make local school boards adopt a policy to hold the Pledge of Allegiance at the start of each school day in every public K-12 school, and a policy allowing student-initiated, student-led prayer. Students could choose not to take part in either, and no student could be made to join a prayer. The local superintendent would look into reported violations. (sos.alabama.gov ↗)

Biggest change

  • Requires every public K-12 school to hold the Pledge of Allegiance at the start of each school day, with students free to decline. source: sos.alabama.gov
  • Requires each local board to adopt a policy allowing student-initiated, student-led prayer; local boards decide where students who opt out go. source: alabamareflector.com

Who it affects most: Public K-12 students, teachers and local school boards.

Cost to the state: The Fair Ballot Commission says there are no costs or taxes tied to Amendment 3.

What your vote means

YES

Every public K-12 school starts the day with the Pledge, and every school board must allow student-led prayer, written into the constitution.

NO

The constitution stays silent on the Pledge and student prayer; current state policy and federal law still apply.

Our one-line summary. Alabama does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Rep. Reed Ingram (R-Pike Road) sponsored it. The House passed it 94-4 and the Senate 30-0. An earlier version, HB 43, would have cut state money by 25% for school boards that did not take part; that penalty was removed. source: alabamareflector.com
  • Ingram said a state policy already calls for the Pledge, but many schools do not follow it. source: alabamareflector.com

Why supporters want it

  • Rep. Reed Ingram, the sponsor, said the goal is to "give [students] the option to be able to fellowship with other students who are Christians," and that schools should follow the existing Pledge policy (said about the earlier version, HB 43). source: alabamareflector.com
  • Senate Minority Leader Bobby Singleton (D-Greensboro) said Democrats chose not to fight it and felt more school prayer was needed. source: alabamareflector.com

Why opponents object

  • About the earlier version (HB 43), A'Niya Robinson of the ACLU of Alabama said it was "not only unconstitutional but also creates a hostile educational environment," and that "Students should have the freedom to recite (or not recite) the pledge." source: alabamareflector.com
  • About the earlier version, Nik Nartowicz of Americans United for Separation of Church and State said families, not politicians, should decide how children engage with religion. source: alabamareflector.com

Who backs each side

  • For: sponsor Rep. Reed Ingram and the Legislature (94-4 House, 30-0 Senate). Against (earlier version): ACLU of Alabama and Americans United for Separation of Church and State. No statement found yet from either group on the final version (HB 511). No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: alabamareflector.com
