Amendment 4: Weekly national anthem in schoolsNational anthem played in public schools every week

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Act 2026-424, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as Senate Bill 5 (Act 2026-424) ↗. Passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Fair Ballot Commission statements (PDF) ↗

In plain words

The constitution has no rule today about the national anthem in schools. This amendment would make local school boards adopt a policy so every public K-12 school plays the first verse ("stanza") of The Star-Spangled Banner, or allows a live performance of it, at least once a week during school hours. A school-approved performance using the Library of Congress archived version is allowed instead of a broadcast. (sos.alabama.gov ↗)

Biggest change

  • Requires each local school board to adopt a policy for a weekly broadcast or performance of the anthem's first verse in every public K-12 school. source: sos.alabama.gov

Who it affects most: Public K-12 students, teachers and local school boards.

Cost to the state: The Fair Ballot Commission says there are no costs or taxes tied to Amendment 4.

What your vote means

YES

Every public K-12 school must play or perform the anthem's first verse at least once a week, written into the constitution.

NO

The constitution sets no anthem rule; schools and local boards keep deciding for themselves.

Our one-line summary. Alabama does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Sen. Gerald Allen (R-Cottondale) sponsored it. The Senate passed it 22-5. source: alabamareflector.com

Why supporters want it

  • Sen. Gerald Allen, the sponsor, said it would "help us celebrate the 250th anniversary of this great country." source: alabamareflector.com

Why opponents object

  • Sen. Vivian Davis Figures (D-Mobile) voted no because the state constitution is already long: "I just don't think it should be a constitutional amendment." source: alabamareflector.com
  • Sen. Rodger Smitherman (D-Birmingham) raised concerns about the anthem's third verse, which refers to slavery: "Nothing should be glorified about that." (The amendment covers only the first verse.) source: alabamareflector.com

Who backs each side

  • For: sponsor Sen. Gerald Allen and the Legislature. Against: no group found; five senators voted no. No public record found of campaign money for either side. source: alabamareflector.com
