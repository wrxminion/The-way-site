Question · every Virginia voter

Question 2: MarriageRemove the same-sex marriage ban from the state constitution

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): HJR 3 (2026), House Joint Resolution 3 (2026 session); first passed in 2025 as HJR 9 and SJR 249. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Department of Elections proposed amendments brochure (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Virginia's constitution still says only a marriage between one man and one woman is valid here, and bans civil unions with the same effect. That ban can't be enforced today because of a 2015 U.S. Supreme Court ruling (Obergefell v. Hodges), but it would come back into force if that ruling were overturned. This amendment removes the ban. In its place, it says marriage is a vital personal right, bars the state from refusing a marriage license to two adults because of their sex, gender or race, and requires the state to treat all lawful marriages of two adults equally. (elections.virginia.gov (Department of Elections) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Deletes the words saying only a union of one man and one woman may be a valid marriage, and the ban on civil unions or other legal statuses like marriage. source: elections.virginia.gov (Department of Elections)
  • Bars the state and local governments from denying a marriage license to two adults on the basis of sex, gender or race. source: elections.virginia.gov (Department of Elections)
  • Requires the state and local governments to recognize any lawful marriage between two adults and treat such marriages equally under the law. source: elections.virginia.gov (Department of Elections)

Who it affects most: Same-sex couples who are married or want to marry in Virginia.

Cost to the state: Unknown. No cost estimate was published with the amendment.

What your vote means

YES

The same-sex marriage ban comes out of the state constitution, and the state must treat all lawful marriages of two adults equally.

NO

The same-sex marriage ban stays in the state constitution; it stays unenforceable unless the U.S. Supreme Court overturns its 2015 ruling.

Our one-line summary. Virginia does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • The official explanation says the ban on same-sex marriage in Virginia's constitution is no longer enforceable because of the U.S. Supreme Court's 2015 decision in Obergefell v. Hodges, but it remains in the constitution and would be enforceable again if the Court overturned that decision. source: elections.virginia.gov (Department of Elections)
  • The ban was added to the constitution in 2006 (the Marshall-Newman amendment). The General Assembly first passed this repeal in 2025 (SJR 249: Senate 24-15, House 58-34). source: ballotpedia.org
  • The General Assembly passed it a second time in January 2026: House 67-31 (64 Democrats and 3 Republicans for), Senate 26-13 (21 Democrats and 5 Republicans for). source: news.ballotpedia.org
  • In Virginia a constitutional amendment must pass the General Assembly twice, with a House of Delegates election in between, and then go to the voters. source: law.lis.virginia.gov

Why supporters want it

  • Sen. Adam Ebbin (D): "Senate Democrats stand for fairness and equality, and this amendment reflects those values." source: ballotpedia.org
  • LaTwyla Mathias of Progress Virginia said the 2006 amendment "has been a stain on Virginia's constitution for too long." source: ballotpedia.org

Why opponents object

  • The Family Foundation said the amendment "would enshrine gender ideology into our state constitution," which it argues would threaten girls' safety and sports and religious liberty. source: ballotpedia.org
  • John Reid, the 2025 Republican nominee for lieutenant governor: "It doesn't provide protection for people who sincerely don't agree with gay marriage." source: ballotpedia.org

Who backs each side

  • For: Virginians for Marriage Equality, a registered referendum committee whose largest support is in-kind contributions from the ACLU of Virginia (as of July 2026). source: vpm.org
  • Against: Virginians Against 3, registered by the Family Foundation; its filings list "Opposing the marriage amendment" as its scope (as of July 2026). source: vpm.org
  • Ballotpedia also lists Equality Virginia as a supporter, and Heritage Action for America and The Family Foundation as opponents. source: ballotpedia.org
