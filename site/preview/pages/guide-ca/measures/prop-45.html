Proposition 45: Environmental review changesDeadlines and narrower lawsuits for environmental review of "essential" projects

Citizen initiative (statute): , placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Initiative statute). Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Official Voter Information Guide (PDF) ↗

In plain words

CEQA, the California Environmental Quality Act, requires agencies to study a project's effects on the environment before approving it, and people can sue if they think the study was inadequate. For projects the measure defines as "essential," this sets deadlines (for example, 365 business days to finish a full environmental report), judges a project by the rules in place when it applied, caps comment periods, limits tribal consultation to federally recognized tribes, and narrows lawsuits so a court can stop only the part of a project that broke the rules. Applicants can choose the new or the old process. (lao.ca.gov (Legislative Analyst's Office) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Covers most housing, transportation, water, health, wildfire mitigation, school building, internet access and clean energy projects; sets deadlines for agencies and lawsuits and limits the evidence courts may consider and the relief they can order. source: sos.ca.gov (Official Voter Information Guide)

Who it affects most: Builders and agencies behind housing, transportation, water and health projects, and residents and groups who comment on or sue over such projects.

Cost to the state: Initial state and local costs, including courts, likely in the high tens of millions of dollars a year, possibly over $100 million, partly offset by fees; longer-term effects uncertain (Legislative Analyst).

What your vote means

YES

Essential projects can use a faster environmental review with deadlines and narrower lawsuits.

NO

All projects keep using today's environmental review and lawsuit rules.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with a simple majority.

  • The California Chamber of Commerce placed it on the ballot, saying CEQA is outdated and fuels costly delays and lawsuits. CalMatters reports courts would have to rule on related lawsuits within 270 days. source: CalMatters

Why supporters want it

  • Official argument in favor: approval for essential projects is too slow and costly; "Prop. 45 does not exempt a single project from environmental review." Signed by Ann-Louise Kuhns (California Children's Hospital Association), Jennifer Capitolo (California Water Association) and Jenna Abbott (California Council on Affordable Housing). source: voterguide.sos.ca.gov (Secretary of State)

Why opponents object

  • Official argument against: "Prop. 45 eliminates many of CEQA's existing protections" and reduces the public's ability to be heard. Signed by Joe Lyou (Coalition for Clean Air), Jennifer Clary (Clean Water Action) and Matt Abularach-Macias (California Environmental Voters). source: voterguide.sos.ca.gov (Secretary of State)

Who backs each side

  • For: California Chamber of Commerce, California Building Industry Association, California Hospital Association, California Children's Hospital Association, California Primary Care Association, California Water Association, California Council for Affordable Housing, California Hispanic Chambers of Commerce, California LULAC, California Republican Party. Against: California Labor Federation, California Nurses Association, Sierra Club California, Clean Water Action, Coalition for Clean Air, National Wildlife Federation, Western Center on Law & Poverty, League of Women Voters of California, California Democratic Party. source: CalMatters
  • Money (Secretary of State, through Oct. 6, 2026): committees in support reported $40,056,350; committees in opposition reported $18,905,427. source: sos.ca.gov (Secretary of State)

Official summary by the Attorney General (tap to open)
Amends California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) to expedite environmental review for specified project categories (including most housing, transportation, water, health, wildfire mitigation, educational facility, internet access, and clean energy projects). For these types of projects, this measure: Sets deadlines for public agencies to complete environmental review and take required actions. Allows expedited review of environmental impacts, limiting public agencies’ current obligation to consider a range of feasible project alternatives to reduce environmental impacts. Establishes deadlines for filing and resolving lawsuits challenging project approvals; limits evidence courts may consider and relief courts can order.
(sos.ca.gov (Official Voter Information Guide) ↗)
