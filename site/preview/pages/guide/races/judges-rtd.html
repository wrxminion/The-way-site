Judges and RTD board

Colorado judges do not run against opponents. Voters decide whether each judge stays in office for another term (a "retention" vote: yes or no). 124 judges are on ballots somewhere in Colorado this year; you will see only the ones for your court districts.

The state's Office of Judicial Performance Evaluation publishes an evaluation of each judge, written by citizen commissions. Read them at judicialperformance.colorado.gov ↗. This guide lists the names and does not add its own view.

What each court does

  • Colorado Supreme Court: Colorado's highest court. Its rulings bind every other state court. Voters decide whether each justice keeps the job (yes or no); there is no opponent. source: coloradojudicial.gov
  • Colorado Court of Appeals: The court that reviews most appeals from trial courts before any case reaches the Supreme Court. Voters decide whether each judge keeps the job (yes or no). source: coloradojudicial.gov
  • District Court: The main trial court: civil cases of any size, felonies, divorce and custody, juvenile, probate (wills and estates) and mental health cases. Voters decide whether each judge keeps the job (yes or no). source: coloradojudicial.gov
  • County Court: Handles civil cases under $25,000, misdemeanors (smaller crimes), traffic tickets, small claims and protection orders. Voters decide whether each judge keeps the job (yes or no). source: coloradojudicial.gov

RTD Board of Directors

What this job does

One of 15 elected directors who run RTD, the Denver area's bus, light rail and commuter rail system. Each represents a district of about 200,000 people. A term is four years. source: en.wikipedia.org

The Regional Transportation District board is nonpartisan, so candidates have no party label. Only voters in an RTD district with a seat on the ballot vote on that seat.
