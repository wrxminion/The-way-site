In plain words

Article II, Section 36 of the Arizona Constitution already bans the state from giving preferential treatment to, or discriminating against, people. This measure adds detail. The state, cities, counties, public universities, community colleges and school districts could not require applicants, employees, students or contractors to back race-based preferences or race-based "diversity, equity and inclusion" ideas as a condition of school, a job, a contract or a scholarship. They also could not spend public money on offices or positions at public schools and colleges that promote race-based preferential treatment, or use discipline that treats students or employees differently by race or ethnicity. The measure has exceptions (HCR 2044, proposed Section 36 C(2) and D): academic, research and creative work, student organizations, health services, specified federal compliance, tribal programs and historical discussion. Each exception qualifies a particular prohibition, so this does not mean all teaching or all diversity-related activity is banned. (azleg.gov (Arizona Legislature) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Adds a ban on requiring applicants, employees, students or contractors to support race-based preferences or "diversity, equity and inclusion" ideas. source: azleg.gov (Arizona Legislature)
  • Bans public money for offices or positions at public schools and colleges that promote race-based preferential treatment. source: azleg.gov (Arizona Legislature)
  • Bans discipline that treats students or employees differently by race or ethnicity. source: azleg.gov (Arizona Legislature)

Who it affects most: State and local governments, public schools and colleges, and the people who work, study or contract with them.

Cost to the state: Unknown. The Legislative Council analysis text we read has no fiscal section. Opponents argue it could cost public universities research money (their claim, not checked).

What your vote means

YES

The constitution's existing ban would add details: no requiring people to back race-based preferences or "diversity, equity and inclusion" ideas to get schooling, a job or a contract, no public money for positions that promote them, and no different discipline by race.

NO

Nothing changes: the current ban in the constitution stays as it is.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the voters voting on it.

  • The bill is House Concurrent Resolution 2044 (2026). It amends Article II, Section 36 of the Arizona Constitution. source: azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Under Article 21, Section 1, a proposed amendment passes if a majority of the voters voting on it approve. source: azleg.gov (Arizona Legislature)

Why supporters want it

  • Victor Riches, president of the Goldwater Institute: a carve-out in the constitution lets race-based preferences continue when federal law calls for them; this would bar such treatment in schools, agencies and contracting for good. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Nathan Duell, Heritage Action for America: DEI has failed Arizonans, and the measure protects free speech by banning DEI loyalty statements as job conditions. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Kimberly Miller, Arizona Women of Action: opportunity should rest on merit, not race. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Scot Mussi, Arizona Free Enterprise Club: the federal-mandate exception lets race-based policies take hold whatever local voters want; the measure targets the practice, so renaming a program would not get around it. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Seven arguments for are printed in the pamphlet; most repeat these points. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)

Why opponents object

  • Pinny Sheoran, League of Women Voters of Arizona: state law already bans discrimination; this goes further by restricting programs, and later legislatures could ban more ideas without a vote of the people. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Darrell Hill, ACLU of Arizona: the measure bars certain ideas in colleges and government workplaces, which he says violates the First Amendment. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • State Sens. Mitzi Epstein and Lauren Kuby: it would bar good-faith efforts to diversify faculty, including recruiting ads, and could cut medical research funding. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Marie Sansone: Article II, Section 36(A) already bans preferential treatment and discrimination, so this adds confusion and cost. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Monica Sandschafer, Mi Familia Vota: it would create "DEI police" who review every grant, class and program. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
  • Sixteen arguments against are printed in the pamphlet. source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Secretary of State's ballot language file ↗.

PROPOSED AMENDMENT TO THE CONSTITUTION BY THE LEGISLATURE RELATING TO THE PROHIBITION OF PREFERENTIAL TREATMENT AND DISCRIMINATION
OFFICIAL TITLE
AMENDING ARTICLE II, SECTION 36, CONSTITUTION OF ARIZONA.
DESCRIPTIVE TITLE
ADDS FURTHER DETAILS TO THE CONSTITUTIONAL PROHIBITION AGAINST PREFERENTIAL TREATMENT OR DISCRIMINATION BY THE STATE BASED ON RACE, ETHNICITY, OR OTHER CLASSES.

A “yes” vote shall have the effect of amending the Arizona Constitution’s current prohibition against preferential treatment or discrimination by the state based on race, ethnicity, or other classes, to specifically prohibit: (1) compelling individuals to support preferential treatment or discrimination based on race or ethnicity as a condition of education, employment, or contract opportunities; (2) spending public money on positions in public education responsible for promoting preferential treatment or discrimination based on race or ethnicity; and (3) implementing disciplinary practices that treat students or employees differently based on race or ethnicity. Prohibited conduct includes requiring or asking an applicant, employee, student, or contractor to provide a statement in support of race-based diversity, equity, and inclusion beyond upholding the federal Equal Protection Clause or asking such a person to discuss the person’s race or ethnicity or experience with others’ race or ethnicity. The amendment contains certain exceptions for health services, legal, and other reasons.
A “no” vote shall have the effect of maintaining the current prohibition in the Arizona Constitution against preferential treatment or discrimination by the state based on race, ethnicity, or other classes.
