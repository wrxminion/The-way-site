Local measure · Butler County

Butler County QuestionButler County schools: 6.3-cent property tax increase for school facilities

What your vote means

YES

A YES (FOR) vote lets the Butler County school board raise the school property tax by 6.3 cents for every $100 of taxed property value, about $63 a year on a $100,000 property.

NO

An AGAINST vote cancels the 6.3-cent increase, so the school property tax stays at its current rate of 42.1 cents per $100.

Our one-line summary of the official wording.

In plain words

Our summary of the official materials and local reporting.

  • The school board approved a tax increase on Oct. 1, 2025, raising the rate from 42.1 cents to 48.4 cents per $100 of taxed value (the 6.3 cents is the difference). source: wbko.com
  • Three residents filed a petition on Nov. 17, 2025 asking for a public vote on it; the county clerk certified the petition on Dec. 17, 2025, so the question is on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot unless the board calls a special election. source: wbko.com
  • The district calls this a 'recallable nickel': a tax increase the board can pass, but voters can force a vote on it. It says the state budget already counts about $780,000 in matching state money for Butler County if the increase stands. source: butler.kyschools.us

Cost: About $63 a year on a $100,000 property, says the district (cars and trucks are not taxed). The district says the state would add about $1.95 for every $1 raised locally.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

  • Butler County Schools says its costs are rising: property insurance, an aging bus fleet and aging buildings, federal funding cuts, ending grants, and about $370,000 a year in state requirements the state does not pay for. source: butler.kyschools.us

Why supporters want it

  • Butler County Schools (the district) says the money is needed for rising property insurance costs, an aging bus fleet and buildings, federal cuts and unfunded state mandates, and that its rate would still be one of the lowest in Kentucky and the lowest among neighboring counties. source: butler.kyschools.us

Who backs each side
