Economic Development (Question 3)$100 million in bonds for business sites, Quonset, the I-195 District, ocean and life-science industries

Legislative referral (general obligation bond): 2026-H 7127 Sub A as amended (FY2027 budget), Article 5, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as House Bill 7127 Sub A as amended (FY2027 budget), Article 5 ↗. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: House Bill 7127 Sub A as amended, Article 5, Section 1 (RI General Assembly) ↗

In plain words

Bonds are borrowing the state pays back with interest. If this passes, the state can borrow up to $100 million to get land ready for business and to support growth industries. (webserver.rilegislature.gov ↗)

Biggest change

  • Lets the state borrow up to $100 million: $55 million to prepare land and sites for business (including the Quonset Business Park and the I-195 District) and $45 million for buildings and projects that support Rhode Island's ocean, defense, life sciences and data analytics industries. source: webserver.rilegislature.gov

Who it affects most: Businesses and workers in the areas named, and taxpayers who repay the bonds.

Cost to the state: Unknown for this question alone: the bill does not give a total cost. Interest adds to the amount borrowed. The bill says the bonds must be paid off within 20 state fiscal years after the year they are issued (Article 5, Section 4). The state's yearly payments come from its regular budget.

What your vote means

YES

The state can borrow up to $100 million for business sites and for ocean, defense and life-science industries.

NO

The state cannot borrow this money for these projects.

Our one-line summary. Rhode Island does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • The General Assembly put all five bond questions on the ballot in Article 5 of the state budget bill for the year that starts July 2026 (House Bill 7127). Voters decide each question separately, and a project is approved if a majority of the people voting on it vote to approve it. source: webserver.rilegislature.gov

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Rhode Island General Assembly's text of House Bill 7127 (the budget), Article 5 ↗.

(3) Economic Development $100,000,000
Approval of this question will allow the State to issue general obligation bonds, refunding bonds, and/or temporary notes in an amount not to exceed one hundred million dollars ($100,000,000) to fund industrial facilities infrastructure improvements for economic development purposes to fund site development and growth industry infrastructure , to be allocated as follows:
(a) Site Development $55,000,000
Provides fifty-five million dollars ($55,000,000) for land acquisition or assembly, environmental remediation, infrastructure and utility installation, site preparation or development, and project investments. Eligible uses include, but are not limited to, large-scale industrial site development to create pad-ready locations and/or facilities, infrastructure improvements and investments within the Quonset Business Park, and land acquisition, preparation, and project investments within the I-195 District.
(b) Growth Industry Infrastructure $45,000,000
Provides forty-five million dollars ($45,000,000) for infrastructure, facilities, projects, and investments that support Rhode Island's ocean, defense, life sciences, data analytics and related industries. Eligible uses include, but are not limited to, water-based test ranges and onshore ocean or defense-related innovation/production facilities, as well as facilities, projects, and investments that advance businesses and job growth in the life sciences.

Shall the action of the general assembly, by an act passed at the January 2026 session, authorizing the issuance of bonds, refunding bonds, and temporary notes of the State of Rhode Island for the capital projects and in the amount with respect to each such project listed below be approved, and the issuance of bonds, refunding bonds, and/or temporary notes authorized in accordance with the provisions of said act?
