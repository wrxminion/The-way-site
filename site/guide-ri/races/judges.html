Judges on the ballot

We did not find any state judge or justice on Rhode Island's November 3, 2026 ballot. The governor appoints judges with help from a nominating commission and the Senate confirms.

We could not open an official Rhode Island judiciary page. The details below come from Ballotpedia and news reports and are not independently reviewed.

What we found

  • Selection. Ballotpedia says all Rhode Island judges are appointed by the governor with a nine-member nominating commission. For lower courts the governor picks from the commission's list and the Senate approves. source: ballotpedia.org (unreviewed)
  • Informal practice. The Boston Globe reports the governor, the Senate president and others traditionally negotiate judicial appointments together. source: bostonglobe.com (unreviewed)

County, district and local judges

What this covers. Whether any Rhode Island judge is chosen by voters in November. We found none. State judges are appointed (see above). Probate courts and municipal courts are run by cities and towns. In the statutes we read, the town or city council chooses the judge, not voters. Section 8-9-4 says a town council "shall elect" a judge of probate for the town when it has been given that power. Section 45-2-24 lets the Coventry town council set up a municipal court and appoint its judge. We read these on November 4, 2026. What it does not cover. We did not check the charter of each of Rhode Island's 39 cities and towns, so we cannot rule out a local exception. No local judges are listed because we found none on a ballot.

  • Probate judges source: R.I. Gen. Laws 8-9-4 (unreviewed)Chosen by the town or city council in the statute we read, not at the November election.
  • Municipal court judges source: R.I. Gen. Laws 45-2-24Appointed by the town or city council. The statute we read covers Coventry only.
