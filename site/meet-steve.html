Steve and the shark.

Play the game

Free. No sign-in. Works with a mouse, a keyboard or a touch screen.

Facts get mixed up all the time, by people and by AI tools. Two cartoon characters help show the difference: one collects facts you can check, and one makes them up.

This is just an example of what we have so far. It is an early tester demo and it may change. The shark's name is a placeholder.

This is Steve.

Scuba Steve is a marine biologist. He dives for facts the way other scientists collect samples. Everything in his bag is a true paper with a named source, such as NOAA, so you can follow the link and check it yourself.

Steve is the careful one. If he cannot name where a fact came from, he leaves it in the water.

This is the shark.

The shark is a random AI hallucination, and a factarian: it only eats papers. It does not read them first. It just swallows them and makes up something that sounds right, with a source like "trust me".

It is silly on purpose, and it is never dangerous. The shark only eats papers, and it never eats Steve.

The game

Steve's Fact Dive

Each bubble holds a fact. Shoot the made-up ones to pop them. Swim Steve into the true ones to catch them. A true fact always names its source, and the list at the end links to every one you caught.

It works like the old browser games. It runs inside your browser and stays there. The game sends nothing anywhere and saves nothing about you, and it is safe for kids. Close the page and it is gone.

It is fun for adults too. Try to collect every good fact.
