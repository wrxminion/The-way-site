Free federal court records: the Open Courts Act

When

Friday, January 1, 2027: the PACER fee rises from 10 to 12 cents a page.

What it is

Federal court records are public, but most are not free. To read them online you use PACER, the federal courts' own system. It charges 10 cents a page, up to $3 for most documents. Anyone who runs up $30 or less in a quarter pays nothing.

On June 26, 2026 the Judicial Conference, which sets policy for the federal courts, approved a rise to 12 cents a page starting January 1, 2027. It lasts five years. The free amount goes up to $40 a quarter. The courts say the money pays for a new, more secure case system that will cost $700 million to $800 million over six years.

The Open Courts Act of 2026 (S. 4667) would replace PACER and the courts' filing system with one new system and make public court records free to read. Senator John Kennedy (R-La.) introduced it on June 2, 2026, with Senator Ron Wyden (D-Ore.) as cosponsor. It went to the Senate Judiciary Committee that day. The official record shows no action since.

Under the bill, users who spend more than $25,000 a quarter and federal agencies would still pay, according to Fix the Court, a group that supports it.

This is not new. The House passed the Open Courts Act of 2020 (H.R. 8235, from Representatives Hank Johnson, D-Ga., and Doug Collins, R-Ga.) on December 8, 2020, but the Senate never voted on it. A 2021 version did not pass either.

Any bill that has not passed both the House and the Senate when the new Congress begins on January 3, 2027 dies. S. 4667 would then have to be introduced again.

If you think court records should be free

You can ask your senators to support the Open Courts Act (S. 4667), and to bring it back in 2027 if it runs out of time. The "Write to your senators about this" link near the bottom of this page builds a letter with sourced facts.

You can read many records free today, and share the ones you buy so others can read them free too. See the free tools below.

If you think the courts need the fees to pay for a secure system, the same letter tool has the facts for that side too. The points on both sides are listed below the same way.

What supporters of free records say

  • Sponsors come from both parties: Kennedy (R) and Wyden (D) in 2026, Johnson (D) and Collins (R) in 2020. S. 4667 record (govinfo) ↗ Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, December 11, 2020 ↗
  • Senator Wyden said the bill would save "more than $60 million" a year in operating costs. Justia News, June 3, 2026 ↗
  • Fix the Court cites IT experts who put a new free system at $10 million to $20 million to build and $3 million to $5 million a year to run. Fix the Court, June 2, 2026 ↗
  • Free Law Project says PACER took in $154.1 million in fees in 2025, and that the Congressional Budget Office estimated an earlier version would cut yearly operating costs from $64 million to $15 million. Free Law Project, October 21, 2026 ↗
  • In a lawsuit over PACER fees, a judge found the courts collected more than $920 million from 2010 to 2016. A $125 million settlement was approved in 2024 and upheld on appeal in March 2026. Free Law Project, October 21, 2026 ↗
  • Supporters say paying per page makes it harder for the public, reporters and researchers to check what courts do. Free Law Project, October 21, 2026 ↗ Fix the Court, June 2, 2026 ↗

What the federal judiciary says

  • The courts say they share the goal: "We also share the goal that PACER be provided without charge to most ordinary users, which is already the case." Judiciary analysis of the Open Courts Act of 2021 (H.R. 5844, S. 2614) ↗
  • The Judicial Conference opposes paying for free PACER by raising filing fees. In 2020 it estimated filing fees would have to rise about $750 per case, taking the $350 civil filing fee to $1,100. Judicial Conference letter to the House Judiciary Committee, September 23, 2020 ↗
  • It said 87 percent of PACER money comes from less than 3 percent of accounts. Judicial Conference letter to the House Judiciary Committee, September 23, 2020 ↗
  • It says money from Congress is "neither stable nor predictable" and wants a reliable source in place before fees end. Judiciary analysis of the Open Courts Act of 2021 (H.R. 5844, S. 2614) ↗ Administrative Office letters to Rep. Hank Johnson, April 26 and September 27, 2021 ↗
  • In 2021 the head of the courts' administrative office wrote: "If PACER fees are eliminated without a sufficient replacement, we will be unable to successfully pursue this project," and said the bill could set a timeline "that is likely not feasible." Administrative Office letters to Rep. Hank Johnson, April 26 and September 27, 2021 ↗ The Indiana Lawyer, January 18, 2022 ↗
  • In June 2026 Judge Robert Conrad, who heads that office now, said: "without a modest increase in fees, we will not be able to collect enough money to cover the costs of delivering the case management system." ABA Journal, June 29, 2026 ↗ U.S. Courts, June 26, 2026: Judiciary approves funding for case management and public access modernization ↗
  • We found no published judiciary position on the 2026 bill yet. The points above are about the 2020 and 2021 versions and the 2026 fee decision. U.S. Courts, June 26, 2026: Judiciary approves funding for case management and public access modernization ↗

Free ways to read federal court records now

  • RECAP Archive on CourtListener ↗ Millions of PACER documents that others already bought, free to search and read, from the nonprofit Free Law Project.
  • RECAP for Chrome ↗ A free browser add-on. When you buy a document on PACER it shares a copy to the free archive, and it tells you when a free copy already exists.
