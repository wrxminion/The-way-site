We wish we had this

Some public records are hidden, only partly public, or hard to get. Here are 10 we wish everyone could see. Each one has a fact we checked, with sources, and a button to ask the people who represent you for it. The letter tool lets you take either side. The Way does not take a side.

Boulder and Colorado

We start close to home, with Boulder. More cities and states will be added as we check their records.

Boulder, Colorado

Police misconduct payouts

$5.47 millionin 7 settlements, 2020 to 2024

We have a record of at least $5,470,000 of Boulder tax dollars paid to settle lawsuits and claims about Boulder police from 2020 to 2024.

What we wish we had: We found no single city list of these payments. We added them up from city council papers and news reports, so the real total may be higher.

Under Colorado law, an officer who did not act in good faith may pay 5 percent of the damages, up to $25,000. The city pays the rest. In 2021 a company offered Colorado officers their own liability insurance for about $25 a month.

If you think police should carry their own liability insurance, or that every city should post what it pays each year, contact your representatives and ask for it.

Colorado

State court records

$10.50to search every Colorado state court for one name

Colorado state court records are public, but the courts do not offer a case search on their own website. A statewide name search on CoCourts, a private company, costs $10.50.

What we wish we had: In the courts' own words: "Access to court records is not available directly through the Colorado Judicial Branch website." The documents themselves are not on the paid sites. You ask the court that has the case.

The price was $10 in 2021, after a rise from $7.

If you think Colorado court records should be free to search online, contact your Colorado lawmakers and ask for it. State courts are run by the state, so Congress cannot change this.

National

Federal police misconduct records

Shut downJanuary 20, 2025

A federal database of misconduct records, commendations and awards for federal officers opened in December 2023. It was shut down when the order that created it was revoked on January 20, 2025.

What we wish we had: There is no national public record of police misconduct cases or of what cities pay to settle them.

NPR reported that without the database it is harder for agencies to catch job applicants with problems in their past. A separate list of decertified officers, run outside the federal government, still exists.

If you think there should be a national record of police misconduct and payouts, contact your representatives and ask for it.

National

Dark money donors

$1.9 billionin 2024 federal races, a record

Groups that do not have to name their donors, like some nonprofits and shell companies, put $1.9 billion into 2024 federal races. That is about double the 2020 record of $1 billion.

What we wish we had: Who gave the money. Since May 2020, tax-exempt groups other than charities and political groups do not even list big donors on their IRS forms. They keep the names and give them to the IRS only if asked.

About $1.2 billion of it backed Democrats and about $664 million backed Republicans. People who favor donor privacy point to NAACP v. Alabama (1958), where the Supreme Court protected a group's member list.

If you think voters should know who pays for election ads, contact your representatives and ask for it.

National

Earmarks, followed to the end

$39 billionfor 20,000+ earmarked projects since 2022

Since 2022, Congress set aside about $39 billion for more than 20,000 earmarked projects back home. By September 30, 2024, about 16 percent of it had been paid out.

What we wish we had: An easy way to follow each earmark from the bill to the money spent and what it built. Sixteen of 19 agencies told GAO they had problems that affected their oversight.

For fiscal 2026, Congress has enacted nearly $15.5 billion more in earmarks.

If you think every earmarked dollar should be tracked in public, contact your representatives and ask where your area's earmarks went.

National

How agencies get their money

5 monthsoffline in 2025, March to August

The White House budget office releases money to agencies in steps. A law says it must post each step online. It took the website down in March 2025, and it came back in August 2025 after a court order.

What we wish we had: Spending detail from the agencies. On July 21, 2025, Judge Emmet Sullivan ruled the takedown broke the law. The government is appealing.

The administration said posting meant sharing "sensitive, pre-decisional and deliberative information" and takes staff time.

If you think spending decisions should be posted for everyone to see, whoever is President, contact your representatives and ask for it.

National

The black budget

$73.3 billionone number, fiscal 2025

The intelligence budget for fiscal 2025 was $73.3 billion. That one number is all the public was told. The Defense Department's separate intelligence budget was $27.8 billion, also with no detail.

What we wish we had: Where the money goes, even totals by agency. The Director of National Intelligence said "there will be no other disclosures of currently classified NIP budget information."

If you think more of the intelligence budget can be shown without risk, such as totals by agency, contact your representatives and ask for it.

National

White House visitor logs

Not releasedsince January 2025

On February 11, 2025, the White House said it will not release visitor logs during President Trump's second term. It said a records law protects them until five years after the President leaves office.

What we wish we had: Who visits the White House and whom they meet. Releases have changed with each President: from 2009 under Obama, stopped in 2017, again from 2021 under Biden, stopped in 2025.

If you think every White House should release its visitor logs, whoever is President, contact your representatives and ask for it.

National

Foreign lobbying (FARA)

Feb. 5, 2025when enforcement narrowed

People who lobby here for foreign governments must register, and their filings are public. Since February 5, 2025, the Justice Department brings criminal charges under the law only for "conduct similar to more traditional espionage by foreign government actors."

What we wish we had: Who is working for foreign governments without registering. The same memo disbanded the FBI's Foreign Influence Task Force. The memo said the change frees resources for more pressing priorities.

If you think the foreign agent law should be fully enforced again, contact your representatives and ask for it.

National

Judges' homes and finances

Not on the forma judge's own home

When a federal judge buys or sells the home they live in, the law does not require it on their yearly financial report. Rental and investment property over $1,000 must be reported.

What we wish we had: How big purchases like a home were paid for. To read judges' reports online, you must register with your name and address each visit, and reports are destroyed six years after they are filed.

If you think judges should report home purchases and how they paid for them, contact your representatives and ask for it.

How we checked

Every number on this page comes from a record we read, listed under Sources on its card. We look for two sources and one official record for each number. When we found fewer, the card says so.

A settlement, a filing or a rule is not proof that anyone did something wrong. We say only what the records show, and we write about every party the same way.

The buttons open our letter tool with the issue picked. You pick your representative and your side, check the facts you want, and copy a letter or a call script. Nothing you type is saved or sent.
