Dates to watch

  • Wednesday, November 18, 2026

    House Democrats vote on their leader for the next Congress in a closed caucus vote. Hakeem Jeffries is running for Speaker, and no one has said they will run against him. The full House elects the Speaker on January 3, 2027. Write to your House member about it →

    This is a party vote, not a House vote, so it is not on any official record. Source is news reporting. Source: Punchbowl News, November 4, 2026 ↗ Newsweek, November 2026 ↗

  • Tuesday, December 1, 2026

    Senate Democrats elect their leader for the next Congress (Chuck Schumer or Chris Van Hollen) in a closed, secret-ballot caucus meeting. Who is involved, the money and the records → Write to your senators about it →

    This is a party vote, not a Senate vote, so it is not on any official record. Source is news reporting. Source: TIME, November 13, 2026 ↗

  • Friday, December 11, 2026

    Federal government funding runs out unless Congress passes new funding. The same day ends the extensions of the National Flood Insurance Program, surface transportation (highway) programs and many other programs. Who is involved, the money and the records →

    From the law now in force, Public Law 119-103 (the Continuing Appropriations and Extensions Act, 2027, H.R. 6500, signed September 2, 2026), Section 106(3). Source: Public Law 119-103 on govinfo ↗

  • Friday, January 1, 2027

    The PACER fee for reading federal court records online rises from 10 to 12 cents a page, for five years. The Open Courts Act (S. 4667) would make public court records free. Who is involved, the money and the records → Write to your senators about it →

    From the federal judiciary's June 26, 2026 announcement and the Library of Congress bill record. Source: U.S. Courts, June 26, 2026: Judiciary approves funding for case management and public access modernization ↗ S. 4667 bill record (govinfo) ↗

  • Sunday, January 3, 2027

    The new Congress (the 120th) begins at noon. House members elected on November 3 take office, and every bill that has not passed both chambers by then dies and must start over. Who is involved, the money and the records →

    Set by the 20th Amendment to the Constitution. Source: 20th Amendment, Constitution Annotated (Congress.gov) ↗
