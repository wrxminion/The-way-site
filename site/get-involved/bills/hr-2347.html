Why this bill matters in the Senate

In the Senate this House bill is the vehicle for the permitting reform bill, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (BAAJA, S. 5653). On November 10, Senator Thune moved to send it to the Senate Finance Committee with instructions to report it back at once with amendment SA 6888. The Senate Democrats' floor page calls H.R. 2347 the "legislative vehicle for the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act."

What the permitting bill (S. 5653) does, in plain words. S. 5653 changes federal permitting to speed up projects. It narrows environmental reviews under the National Environmental Policy Act, limits some Clean Water Act reviews and permit terms, and shortens some Endangered Species Act consultation timelines. On energy, it gives the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission more power to permit some interstate power lines, tells it to set protections for ratepayers from very large power users such as data centers, drops permit requirements for onshore oil and gas drilling on nonfederal land, and speeds up renewable, geothermal, hydropower and offshore projects. It also limits the scope of National Historic Preservation Act reviews.

S. 5653 sponsor: Sen. Capito, Shelley Moore [R-WV]. Cosponsors: Lee (R-UT), Whitehouse (D-RI), Heinrich (D-NM).

What it does

As the House passed it, the bill keeps damages received because of a sexual act or sexual contact (other than punitive damages) from being taxed as income, even without medical records or visible injuries. Today the tax law generally leaves out only damages for physical injury or sickness. If a court ruling or settlement says the damages come from such conduct, the IRS carries the burden of proof in tax court. The IRS must also tell the public about this rule. This summary describes the House bill, not the permitting text the Senate may put in it.
