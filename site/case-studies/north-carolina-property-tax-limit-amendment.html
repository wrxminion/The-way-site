North Carolina property tax limit amendment: limits on local levy increases

What the property tax limit amendment would require of the legislature, who sponsored it, how each chamber voted, what each side says, and what is still unknown.

This page gives no recommendation for or against any measure. A sourced first draft: no reviewer has signed off, and each source says how much of it was read. Prepared October 31, 2026.

What the measure does

Sourcing note: the North Carolina legislature and State Board of Elections sites are blocked from where this draft was written. Sponsors and vote counts come from the legislature's bill pages as retrieved. We could not retrieve the bill PDF for this measure, so the exact ballot question wording is taken from news reports and is marked as such.

This is a legislatively referred constitutional amendment on North Carolina's November 3, 2026 ballot. It is House Bill 1089 of the 2025-2026 session, short-titled "Const. Amend. Property Tax Levy Limit," ratified as Session Law 2026-5 on May 21, 2026. News reports say it requires the General Assembly to pass laws limiting how much local governments can increase property tax levies, and that it sets no specific cap or formula; the limits, and any exceptions, would come from later legislation. Ballotpedia's news site says the constitution now has no such restriction. S1 S2 S3 S4

What a vote means

WUNC quotes the ballot wording as "Constitutional amendment requiring limits on property tax increases by local governments." We did not find the wording on the legislature's pages. A yes vote adds the requirement to the constitution; a no vote leaves the constitution without it. News reports say it needs a simple majority of votes cast on it and that the governor has no veto over it. S2 S3

Who wrote it and how the legislature voted

  • Primary sponsors on the bill page: Representatives Echevarria, Howard, Setzer and Paré. S1
  • House, May 20, 2026, second and third readings: 73 yes, 46 no, 1 excused absence. S1
  • Senate, May 20, 2026, second and third readings: 31 yes, 15 no, 3 excused absences. Three Senate amendments were tabled, each 30 to 16. We have not read them. S1

Ballotpedia's news site reports the House vote as 71 to 46 and says both chambers split along party lines. The legislature's bill page shows 73 to 46, so we use that. We have not read the roll call pages. S1 S4

Arguments

No official statement of arguments is saved. These are news-reported statements, attributed, and we have not checked them.

  • Supporters. WUNC quotes Representative Brian Echevarria (R-Cabarrus) saying families need relief and that "the increases have not only been high, but some would say extreme." WSOC reports supporters say limits could give property owners more predictability. S2 S3
  • Opponents. WUNC quotes Representative Maria Cervania (D-Wake) warning of a "one-size-fits-all" approach: "Bertie County is not Mecklenburg County." WSOC reports opponents, including local government officials and some lawmakers, say limits could affect counties' and cities' ability to pay for services. S2 S3

What it would cost

We have not read a fiscal note. Since the amendment sets no limit, its effect on local budgets depends on the law the legislature writes afterward, which no source we read describes.

What we do not yet know

We do not know what limit the legislature would set, what exceptions it would allow, or how it would apply to different counties and cities. We do not know the full constitutional language, which we could not retrieve.
