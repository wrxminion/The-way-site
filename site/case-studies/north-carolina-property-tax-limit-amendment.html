North Carolina property tax limit amendment: limits on local levy increases

What the property tax limit amendment would require of the legislature, who sponsored it, how each chamber voted, what the official fiscal note says, what each side says, and what is still unknown.

This page gives no recommendation for or against any measure. A sourced first draft: no reviewer has signed off, and each source says how much of it was read. Prepared October 31, 2026.

What the measure does

Sourcing note: bill text, fiscal notes, sponsors and vote counts below come from the legislature's own pages, and the ballot wording is also checked against the State Board of Elections referendum list dated September 2, 2026. We have not read the Board's explanation of the amendments. Saved copies of these files are kept with our research.

This is a legislatively referred constitutional amendment on North Carolina's November 3, 2026 ballot. It is House Bill 1089 of the 2025-2026 session, short-titled "Const. Amend. Property Tax Levy Limit," ratified as Session Law 2026-5 on May 21, 2026. In the third edition of the bill, Article V, Section 2(5) keeps the rule that the General Assembly shall not authorize counties, cities, towns, special districts or other local units to levy property taxes except for purposes authorized by general law uniformly applicable throughout the State, unless a majority of the unit's voters approve. It adds: "The General Assembly shall enact general laws limiting the amount by which the levy of taxes on property may increase, which may include exceptions." S1 S2

The amendment sets no number. The Fiscal Research Division says the bill does not specify the levy limit and that a future fiscal note would address it if voters approve. News reports say the same. S3 S4 S5

What a vote means

The ballot question, word for word from the third edition of the bill: "Constitutional amendment requiring limits on property tax increases by local governments," with boxes for FOR and AGAINST. The State Board of Elections list, dated September 2, 2026, shows the same wording under "Property tax levy limit." A yes vote adds the requirement to the constitution; a no vote leaves the constitution without it. News reports say it needs a simple majority of votes cast on it and that the governor has no veto over it. S2 S4 S5 S8

Who wrote it and how the legislature voted

  • Primary sponsors on the bill page and in the fiscal note: Representatives Echevarria, Howard, Setzer and Paré. S1 S3
  • House, May 20, 2026, second reading (roll call H-661) and third reading (H-662): 73 yes, 46 no, 1 excused absence. S1
  • Senate, May 20, 2026, second reading (S-547) and third reading (S-548): 31 yes, 15 no, 3 excused absences. Three Senate amendments were tabled, each 30 to 16 (S-544, S-545, S-546). We have not read them. S1
  • Carolina Public Press, in a report carried by WFAE, says the vote was mostly along party lines, with Representatives Nasif Majeed and Carla Cunningham and Senator Dan Blue (D-Wake) joining Republicans. S6

Ballotpedia's news site reports the House vote as 71 to 46. The legislature's bill page shows 73 to 46, and the saved roll call transcripts (H-661 and H-662) show 73 to 46 too, so we use that. We checked each total in the transcripts for H-661, H-662 and S-544 to S-548 but have not gone through how individual members voted. S1 S7

Arguments

No official statement of arguments is saved. These are news-reported statements, attributed, and we have not checked them.

  • Supporters. WUNC quotes Representative Brian Echevarria (R-Cabarrus) saying families need relief and that "The (property tax) increases have not only been high, but some would say extreme." WFAE quotes House Speaker Destin Hall saying the amendment gives lawmakers "clear direction and clear authority to go in and reform that process," and Senator Tom McInnis (R-Moore) saying it may help "stem the tide" of rising property taxes, though he was unsure it was the full answer. WSOC reports supporters say limits could give property owners more predictability. S4 S5 S6
  • Opponents. WUNC quotes Representative Maria Cervania (D-Wake) warning of a "one-size-fits-all" approach: "Bertie County is not Mecklenburg County." WFAE quotes House Minority Leader Robert Reives saying lawmakers "either cut your services or we raise your property taxes" and calling the amendment false hope, Todd Warren of Down Home North Carolina asking whether voters trust Raleigh to control property taxes, and Senate Minority Leader Sydney Batch calling it a tool to boost Republican turnout. WSOC reports opponents, including local government officials and some lawmakers, say limits could affect counties' and cities' ability to pay for services. S4 S5 S6

What it would cost

The Fiscal Research Division's note (May 5, 2026) estimates no fiscal impact for the state or for local governments, because the amendment itself sets no limit, and says the extra ballot cost is minimal. For context it reports that in the 2024-25 fiscal year, combined county and municipal property tax revenues were $15.1 billion, up 8.1 percent, with counties collecting $10.5 billion and municipalities $4.6 billion. S3

WFAE reports a hypothetical: the NC Housing Coalition analyzed a 2 percent cap applied to 2026 and estimated counties would lose $587.8 million and the 75 largest municipalities $362.2 million, with an average homeowner saving $84 a year. No limit has been set, so this is an illustration by one group, not a forecast. S6

What we do not yet know

We do not know what limit the legislature would set, what exceptions it would allow, or how it would apply to different counties and cities.
