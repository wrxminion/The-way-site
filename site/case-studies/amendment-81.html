Amendment 81: law enforcement and federal immigration notice

What Amendment 81 would require, how it sits next to the laws Colorado passed in 2019 and 2025, what the official arguments say on each side, and what has not been measured.

This page gives no recommendation for or against any measure. A sourced first draft: no reviewer has signed off, and each source says how much of it was read. Prepared October 31, 2026.

What the measure does

Amendment 81 is a citizen initiative. It would add a new section 17 to article XVIII of the Colorado Constitution. It needs 55 percent of the vote to pass. S1

The text says "law enforcement" means a certified peace officer employed by a law enforcement agency, a correctional officer, or an attorney or investigator employed by a district attorney. Those people would have to notify the federal Department of Homeland Security within 72 hours after charging a person with a crime when two things are true: the person is not lawfully present in the United States, or their status is still unknown after a reasonable attempt to find out; and either the charge is a crime of violence as defined by Colorado statute, or the person has been convicted of a prior felony. The text also gives law enforcement "a duty to make a reasonable effort" to determine whether such a person is lawfully present. S1

The Blue Book adds three points about scope. The duty starts when a person is formally charged, not at arrest, and before any court decides guilt or innocence. The prior-felony route applies to any prior felony conviction "regardless of when it occurred," and then to any new charge, including traffic violations, petty offenses and misdemeanors. The crime-of-violence route covers crimes that cause serious bodily harm or death or involve a deadly weapon, which the Blue Book says include murder, assault, arson, escape, extortion, kidnapping, aggravated robbery, and certain sexual offenses and human trafficking. S1

The measure's own opening words are "Notwithstanding any other provision of Colorado law." S1 That is how the text is written; what a court would do with it if it conflicted with a statute has not been tested, because the measure is not law.

What a yes or no vote means

Official wording, Blue Book
VoteWhat the Blue Book says
Yes"requires law enforcement to notify federal immigration authorities after filing certain criminal charges against individuals who are not confirmed to be lawfully present."
No"maintains current law, which allows cooperation with federal immigration authorities in some criminal matters, but limits state and local law enforcement involvement in civil immigration enforcement."

The official ballot title asks: "Shall there be an amendment to the Colorado Constitution requiring law enforcement to notify the department of homeland security when a person is charged with either a violent crime or any crime if the person has a prior felony conviction if law enforcement cannot determine that the person is lawfully present in the United States?" What does that look like in action?

The example below is the Blue Book's own illustration of the rule's reach, adapted. It is not a report of a real case.

A person was convicted of a felony many years ago. Today they are charged with a traffic violation. If the prosecutor cannot confirm the person is lawfully in the United States after a reasonable effort, the measure's text would have the prosecutor notify the Department of Homeland Security within 72 hours of filing the charge, before any trial. S1

The other direction matters too. The measure's text and the Blue Book both say the duty applies when status is "unknown." A person who is lawfully present but whose status could not be confirmed inside 72 hours would, by the text, still fall under the notice rule. Whether that happens often is not something any source we read measures.

How it fits current law

The Blue Book says Colorado law currently lets state and local law enforcement cooperate in enforcing federal criminal law but limits state and local participation in civil immigration enforcement. State and local agencies and employees may not share certain nonpublic personal information with federal immigration authorities unless federal law, a court order or a judicial warrant directs it, and the Blue Book says employees who violate this may face a fine of up to $50,000. It also says neither federal nor state law requires agencies to notify federal immigration authorities when people who are not lawfully present are charged with crimes, and that state and local authorities do not track or publish how many such people are charged with crimes of violence or with any crime after a prior felony. S1

Local governments may set their own level of cooperation unless state law forbids it. The Blue Book gives two examples: Denver bars federal immigration authorities from city-controlled property such as courthouses, and El Paso County requires officers to notify federal immigration authorities when they suspect a person in custody lacks legal status. S1

Two state laws shaped the current rules, according to the General Assembly's own bill pages:

  • HB19-1124, signed May 28, 2019. As enacted, it lets officers cooperate in executing a federal judge's or magistrate's warrant and in honoring a court writ on transferring a prisoner, bars arrests or detention based only on a civil immigration detainer, bars probation staff from giving an individual's personal information to federal immigration authorities, and requires that a person in custody be advised of the right to decline and to speak to an attorney before a telephone or video interview with federal immigration authorities. S2
  • SB25-276, signed act dated May 23, 2025. As enacted, it extends several of these limits: the detainer bar to officers designated by the state, the probation limit to pretrial staff, and personal-information rules to local governments and their employees. It also sets minimum rules for schools, child care centers, health-care facilities and libraries on information and access, and bars a jail from delaying a release for an immigration enforcement operation. S3

We read the bill pages' official summaries, which say they describe each bill "as enacted." We did not read the signed acts themselves, so this page does not say how each law's wording would interact with Amendment 81's. That is a legal question for a lawyer.

Who wrote it and how it reached the ballot

The Blue Book labels this measure as placed on the ballot by citizen initiative. It says the text of the measure "was drafted by the proponents of the initiative," and that the proponents "gathered the required amount of petition signatures." S1

What we do not have: the proponents' names, the filing and Title Board hearing record, earlier drafts of the text, and the signature totals. None of these is in the sources we saved, so this page does not name anyone. Where to get them: the Secretary of State's initiative file for this measure and its Title Board record, and the Secretary of State's list of organizations supporting and opposing each 2026 measure, which the Blue Book gives at coloradosos.gov/pubs/elections/Initiatives/ballot/contacts/2026.html. Arguments in the Blue Book
ForAgainst
People who live in the United States unlawfully and are violent or repeat offenders should be reported to federal immigration authorities, which could lead to removal. Supporters say the measure increases coordination to identify people who may pose a threat to public safety "without targeting those who are otherwise law-abiding."Requiring reports to the Department of Homeland Security and Immigration and Customs Enforcement before a court decides guilt undermines due process and the presumption of innocence. Criminal charges are allegations, not findings of guilt. People who are lawfully present, or who are found not to have committed a crime, could be caught up in enforcement if they cannot prove their status inside 72 hours.
A single statewide standard replaces varied local policies, which supporters say improves public safety and lets the criminal justice system work more efficiently.Key terms such as "reasonable effort" and "lawful presence" are not defined, leaving enforcement to individual officers, who might rely on language or appearance. Opponents say this erodes trust and may deter some people from reporting crimes, cooperating with investigations or seeking help.

What it would cost

For state budget year 2026-27, the Blue Book's fiscal impact says two departments need system updates: the Department of Revenue needs $50,000 to access federal databases and make programming changes, and the Department of Natural Resources needs $10,000 for computer systems. That is $60,000. The measure names no funding source, so the Blue Book assumes the General Fund. Workload may also rise in the departments of Higher Education, Human Services, Judicial, Law and Public Safety, mostly for work done by local governments. S1

For local governments, the Blue Book says police, district attorney offices and Regional Transportation District officers would have more workload and possible spending on systems updates. Because the determination happens after charges are filed, it says most of the workload would fall on district attorney offices, and that the amount varies by how many people are charged in each place. It gives no local dollar figure. S1

What research says

We have not yet gathered research for this study. Questions that research could address include how often people charged under the measure's categories are found to be unlawfully present, how many lawfully present people are asked to prove status, and whether notice rules change crime reporting. We will not list a finding here until we have read the study and can say who ran it, what period and place it covers, and what it cannot show.

What we do not yet know

No source we read says how many people each year would meet the measure's two tests, how a "reasonable effort" would be carried out in practice, or what share of the people notified would be found lawfully present. The Blue Book says the state does not track the underlying numbers. S1
