Oklahoma State Question 845: the Judicial Nominating Commission

What State Question 845 would change about the commission that vets Oklahoma's judicial nominees, who wrote it, how each chamber voted, how the ballot title was rewritten, and what is still unknown.

This page gives no recommendation for or against any measure. A sourced first draft: no reviewer has signed off, and each source says how much of it was read. Prepared October 31, 2026.

What the measure does

State Question 845 (Legislative Referendum 379) is a constitutional amendment the Oklahoma Legislature referred to voters on the November 3, 2026 ballot. It changes Section 3 of Article VII-B of the Oklahoma Constitution, which sets up a 15-member Judicial Nominating Commission as part of the Judicial Department. The commission's powers in the text include deciding whether nominees for judicial office meet the qualifications. S1 S3

The Attorney General's final ballot title describes the changes in these words. Currently, appointments are based on congressional districts as constituted in 1967. If approved, the 15 members would be: (1) six appointed by the Governor, including one from each congressional district as of the time of appointment; (2) six appointed by the Oklahoma Bar Association, including one from each congressional district as of the date of selection; and (3) three at-large members selected by not less than eight members of the commission. The measure "will permit any number of the commissioners to be licensed attorneys in Oklahoma or another state." The prohibition on immediate family members practicing law is removed. At-large commissioners would serve a two-year term and all others a six-year term. It "removes restrictions on political party membership for members appointed by the Governor and at-large members," and permits a commissioner to succeed himself or herself. Vacancies are filled in the same manner as the original appointment or selection, for the rest of the term. The ballot title says the measure "will not have a fiscal impact." S1

What a vote means

The ballot reads "Shall the proposal be approved?" with "For the proposal - YES" and "Against the proposal - NO." A yes vote adopts the changes above; a no vote leaves Section 3 of Article VII-B as it is. All registered Oklahoma voters may vote on state questions. S1 S9

How the ballot title was written

The resolution as passed carried a ballot title written by the legislature (a "gist" of the proposition). S3 Oklahoma's Attorney General reviews legislative ballot titles. KGOU reported in May 2026 that the Attorney General determined none of the four questions then sent to the Secretary of State was sufficiently clear as written, and that he rewrites such titles within 10 to 15 days of notifying the Secretary of State. S7

The saved Attorney General letters show the sequence for SQ 845: on April 24, 2026 he notified the Secretary of State that the proposed title did not comply with 34 O.S. 2021, section 9(B); on May 7, 2026 he sent a preliminary title for comment by the Senate President Pro Tempore, the Speaker of the House and the principal authors; on May 29, 2026 he sent a final title taking the chambers' comments into account. The saved final title is stamped as filed August 13, 2026 and marked "Corrected (CLEAN)." The letters say the review is not an Attorney General Opinion. S1 S2

The preliminary and final titles differ in ways a reader may care about. The preliminary title said non-at-large commissioners would serve a two-year term and all others six years, and that the measure removes party restrictions "permitting up to nine of the fifteen commissioners to be from one party." The final title says at-large commissioners serve two years and others six, and drops the "nine of fifteen" figure, saying instead that it removes restrictions on party membership for Governor-appointed and at-large members. S1 S2

Who wrote it and how the legislature voted

The measure is House Joint Resolution 1024 of the 2025-2026 legislature. The saved bill history shows it as "by Moore and Gollihare." S4

  • Authors: Representative Anthony Moore (principal House author, identified in the Attorney General's letters as Speaker Pro Tempore), with Representative Harris as coauthor; Senator Todd Gollihare (principal Senate author, identified in the letters as Assistant Majority Floor Leader). S2 S4
  • House Rules Committee, March 5, 2025: "do pass as amended by committee substitute," 9 yes and 1 no. Yes: Bashore, George, Harris, Hefner, Lawson, Moore, Osburn, Pfeiffer, T. West. No: Fugate. The committee report gives the title as "Oklahoma Judicial Nominating Commission Reform Act of 2025." S5
  • House passage, March 12, 2025: third reading, ayes 78, nays 13. S4
  • Senate Rules Committee, March 4, 2026: "do pass," 15 ayes and 3 nays. Ayes: Bergstrom, Bullard, Coleman, Daniels, Green, Hall, Hamilton, Howard, Murdock, Paxton, Rader, Rosino, Seifried, Stanley, Thompson. Nays: Boren, Hicks, Jech. S6
  • Senate passage, April 9, 2026: measure passed, ayes 35, nays 8. A floor amendment by Senator Gollihare was filed April 1, 2026; we have not read it. S4
  • Special election votes. The bill history also records "Special Election failed: Ayes: 27 Nays: 17" on April 9, 2026, a motion to reconsider adopted, and "Special Election failed: Ayes: 29 Nays: 15" on April 14, 2026. We do not interpret these entries, but the question is on the November 3 general election ballot, as KGOU and the Oklahoma Gazette report. S4 S7 S8
  • Final steps: returned to the House April 14, enrolled April 15, filed with the Secretary of State April 16, 2026. S4

These counts are from the legislature's bill history page and the committee reports. The roll call pages (names of each member's vote) are linked from the history page but are not saved.

The text

The enrolled resolution as saved is a PDF whose strike-through and underline marks were lost in conversion, so old and new language appear mixed together. We therefore describe the changes from the Attorney General's final ballot title and not from the raw text. Two details in the raw text are worth flagging. It includes a clause that no commissioner shall "serve more than twelve (12) consecutive years, whether elected or appointed," which the Attorney General's final title does not mention and which the Oklahoma Gazette describes as extending term limits from six to 12 years. And it contains a clause making the changes apply to any position that ends by expiration of its term or by vacancy. S3 S8

Arguments

We have no official statement of arguments for or against SQ 845 in the saved sources, and we have not found a voter booklet for Oklahoma comparable to Colorado's Blue Book. The only descriptions saved are neutral news summaries. The Oklahoma Gazette, republishing Oklahoma Watch, describes the question as proposing "several changes to the Judicial Nominating Commission, including extending term limits from six to 12 years and striking a requirement that certain members have no immediate family members who are attorneys." S8 We will not write arguments for either side without a named source. Sources to add: floor and committee debate on HJR 1024, statements from the Oklahoma Bar Association and from groups on each side, and the Secretary of State's supporting documents for the question.

What it would cost

The Attorney General's ballot title says the measure "will not have a fiscal impact." The legislature's bill page lists no fiscal impact statements for HJR 1024 in either chamber. S1 S4

What research says

We have not yet gathered research for this study. Questions it could address include how states choose members of judicial nominating bodies and what difference attorney or party membership makes to who is nominated, and how Oklahoma's commission has worked since it was created. We will not list a finding until we have read the study and can say who ran it, what place and period it covers, and what it cannot show.

What we do not yet know

We have not saved a description of what the commission does beyond the text's own wording, how its members have been chosen in practice, or how many members now are attorneys. We do not know exactly how the twelve-year clause in the text relates to the term changes in the ballot title. We do not know who supports or opposes the question.

Open work before this could be called a reviewed study: get a clean copy of the enrolled resolution showing the strike-through and underline; read the roll call pages and Senator Gollihare's floor amendment; read the Secretary of State's SQ 845 supporting documents; find the current Section 3 text and a plain description of the commission's role; gather named arguments from both sides; and get a reviewer who knows Oklahoma law.
