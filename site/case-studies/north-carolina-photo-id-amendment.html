North Carolina photo ID amendment: photo ID for every voter

What the photo ID amendment would change about mail voting, who sponsored it, how each chamber voted, what each side says, and what is still unknown.

This page gives no recommendation for or against any measure. A sourced first draft: no reviewer has signed off, and each source says how much of it was read. Prepared October 31, 2026.

What the measure does

Sourcing note: the North Carolina State Board of Elections site is blocked from where this draft was written, so we have not read its explanation of the amendments or its list of statewide referendums. Bill text, fiscal notes, sponsors and vote counts below come from the legislature's own pages as retrieved.

This is a legislatively referred constitutional amendment on North Carolina's November 3, 2026 ballot. It was Senate Bill 921 of the 2023 session, ratified as Session Law 2024-58 on December 13, 2024. It changes Article VI, Sections 2 and 3 of the state constitution. As the ratified bill reads on the legislature's PDF (which shows no strike-through marks in the version we read), the present language covers "Voters offering to vote in person" and says they "shall present photographic identification before voting," with the General Assembly to "enact general laws governing the requirements of such photographic identification, which may include exceptions." The amendment widens this from in-person voters to all voters. The House's summary of the session law says it takes effect on certification of the referendum results. S1 S2 S3

Descriptions of current practice differ in wording. Ballotpedia says the photo ID requirement applies to in-person voting and that mail-in absentee voters must include a photocopy of ID, with exceptions, or substitute a driver's license number or the last four digits of a Social Security number. Carolina Public Press, in a report carried by WFAE, says current law already requires photo ID for all voters but the constitution mentions only in-person voting. We have not read the statute. Ballotpedia adds that the amendment would not change current voting requirements but would put them in the constitution, making them harder for future legislatures to change. S4 S7

What a vote means

The ballot question, word for word from the ratified bill: "Constitutional amendment to require all voters, not just those presenting to vote in person, to present photo identification before voting," with boxes for FOR and AGAINST. A yes vote adds the all-voter requirement to the constitution. A no vote leaves the constitution with only the in-person requirement. News reports say the amendment needs a simple majority of the votes cast on it and that the governor has no veto over it. S2 S6

Who wrote it and how the legislature voted

  • Primary sponsors on the bill page: Senators Hise, Daniel and P. Newton. S1
  • Senate, December 2, 2024, second and third readings (roll calls S-676 and S-677): 30 yes, 19 no, 1 excused absence. Ballotpedia says all Republicans voted yes and all voting Democrats voted no. S1 S4
  • House, December 11, 2024, second and third readings (roll calls H-809 and H-810): 73 yes, 45 no, 2 excused absences. Ballotpedia says all 72 Republicans voted yes, along with one Democrat. S1 S4

Counts and roll call numbers are from the legislature's bill page. We have not read the roll call pages that name each member's vote.

Arguments

No official statement of arguments is saved. These are news-reported statements, attributed, and we have not checked them. S4 S5

  • Supporters. WUNC quotes Representative Mike Schietzelt (R-Wake) saying the requirement increases security as mail-in voting has grown. Ballotpedia lists Senator Warren Daniel (R), former Senator Paul Newton (R), the Republican Party of North Carolina and Americans for Tax Reform as supporters. WFAE says supporters want to make the rule permanent. S4 S5 S7
  • Opponents. WUNC quotes Representative Pricey Harrison (D-Guilford) calling it an unnecessary hurdle that would be difficult to undo once in the constitution. Ballotpedia lists the Democratic Party of North Carolina, the North Carolina AFL-CIO, Democracy North Carolina, the North Carolina Black Alliance and others as opponents, and also lists two Republican election officials, Commissioner Eric Pope and former Commissioner Johnnie Carswell. S4 S5

Ballotpedia reports one registered campaign committee as of October 2, 2026, NC By the People, which opposes the measure, with $59,305.62 in contributions and $9,305.62 in expenditures, and no registered committee in support. S4

What it would cost

Ballotpedia's page for the measure contains no fiscal impact information, and we have not found a fiscal note for Senate Bill 921. We read the legislature's fiscal notes for the other two North Carolina amendments (they estimate no fiscal impact); we have not read one for this measure. S4

What we do not yet know

We do not know how many North Carolina mail voters now lack the ID the amendment would require, or what exceptions the legislature would allow. We have not read the statute that now governs mail voters, so the two descriptions of current practice above are unreconciled.
