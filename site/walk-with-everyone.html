The Way to Walk With Everyone

皆と共に歩む道

Minna to tomo ni ayumu michi

Twenty-One Precepts for a Common Path

Raise the floor. Impose no ceiling.

Inspired by the spirit of Miyamoto Musashi’s Dokkōdō (独行道), the Way of Walking Alone — and written for those who seek to walk together without demanding that others walk alike.

I — VII · How we seek truth

  1. I

    Seek truth, even when it opposes your beliefs.

    Truth does not change to accommodate conviction. When evidence contradicts what you believe, change your belief rather than the evidence.

  2. II

    Question everything, especially yourself.

    No person, institution, tradition, or ideology is beyond examination. Apply the same scrutiny to your own reasoning that you apply to others.

  3. III

    Follow evidence, not authority.

    An assertion is not made true by the power, wealth, reputation, or popularity of the person making it. Demand reasons that can be examined and tested.

  4. IV

    Admit what you do not know.

    Uncertainty is not weakness. Never fill the absence of knowledge with the comfort of an invented answer.

  5. V

    Distinguish suspicion from proof.

    Investigate what gives reasonable cause for concern, but never condemn a person on speculation. Record what is known, what is disputed, and what remains unknown.

  6. VI

    Never conceal the truth to protect your cause.

    A truth that damages your position is still the truth. No worthy cause requires deception to survive.

  7. VII

    Judge actions by their consequences, not merely their intentions.

    Good intentions do not erase harm. Good outcomes do not excuse every means. Examine both what was intended and what occurred.

VIII — XIV · How we exercise power

  1. VIII

    Grant others the rights you claim for yourself.

    Rights are not privileges earned through agreement, citizenship, wealth, obedience, or popularity. A principle abandoned for an enemy was never truly a principle.

  2. IX

    Raise the floor without imposing a ceiling.

    A just society asks what every person needs in order to live, learn, and pursue fulfillment, and weighs the evidence on how best to provide it. Another's success need not require another's suffering.

  3. X

    Let no person's worth be measured by wealth or power.

    Money may purchase goods and services. It should never be taken as a measure of a person's dignity, or of their claim to be heard and treated justly.

  4. XI

    Let power answer to those it affects.

    Authority must be visible, accountable, limited, and open to challenge. The greater the power, the greater the burden of justification.

  5. XII

    Demand evidence before punishment.

    An accusation does not establish guilt. Preserve due process, the presumption of innocence, and the right to question those who wield force.

  6. XIII

    Never surrender liberty merely because another is its target.

    The authority used against a stranger today may be used against you tomorrow. Judge the power itself, not your affection for its intended subject.

  7. XIV

    Follow the money, but do not mistake association for guilt.

    Make financial influence and competing interests visible. Examine relationships and incentives without inventing motives or conclusions the evidence cannot support.

XV — XXI · How we walk together

  1. XV

    Speak so others may understand, without hiding what they need to know.

    Clarity is not the removal of inconvenient facts. Make knowledge accessible while preserving the information necessary for informed judgment.

  2. XVI

    Listen to understand, not merely to answer.

    Disagreement is an opportunity to test understanding. Seek the strongest version of another's argument before attempting to refute it.

  3. XVII

    Defend the freedom to walk a different path.

    No one must share your faith, philosophy, culture, or ambitions to deserve equal consideration. Freedom includes the right to disagree.

  4. XVIII

    Never place loyalty above principle.

    Do not excuse in an ally what you condemn in an opponent. The standard must remain when the names, parties, and circumstances change.

  5. XIX

    Preserve knowledge, including our failures.

    Do not erase evidence because it becomes embarrassing or inconvenient. Preserve corrections, disagreements, outcomes, and history so those who follow may learn.

  6. XX

    Change course when a better path is demonstrated.

    No conclusion is sacred. Test, measure, reconsider, and improve. To correct an error is progress, not defeat.

  7. XXI

    Walk your own way, but never forget that others walk beside you.

    Every life follows a different path through a shared world. Act with the understanding that your choices affect others, and theirs affect you. We need not walk alike to walk together.

道
Everything has its own way, yet the Way is the same for everyone.

The Way does not belong to a person, a party, or a generation.
It belongs to all who choose to walk it.
