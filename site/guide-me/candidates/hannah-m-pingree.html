Candidate · Governor

Hannah M. Pingree

What this job does: Maine's top state official. The governor runs the state's departments, proposes the state budget, signs or vetoes bills passed by the Legislature, and picks judges and department heads (the state Senate must confirm them). A term is four years, and a governor can serve no more than two terms in a row.

Democratic · listed 3 of 3 on the Maine Secretary of State's list · on the ballot as "Hannah M. Pingree"

Occupation
Former Speaker of the Maine House; former director of the Governor's Office of Policy Innovation and the Future
Lives in
North Haven

Background

  • Pingree served in the Maine House of Representatives from 2002 to 2010 and was elected Speaker of the House in 2008. source: stateside.com
  • Gov. Janet Mills appointed Pingree in 2019 to lead the Governor's Office of Policy Innovation and the Future, where Pingree also led the Children's Cabinet and the Cabinet on Aging. source: stateside.com
  • Pingree was raised on the island of North Haven, has run a small business and has chaired the local school board. source: hannahforgovernor.com
  • Pingree worked as development director of Waterman's Community Center (2002-2004) and as a political editor and producer at iVillage.com (1999-2001). source: ballotpedia.org
  • Pingree won the June 9, 2026 Democratic primary after four rounds of ranked-choice counting, with 56.2% in the final round over Nirav Shah. source: en.wikipedia.org
  • Pingree is the daughter of U.S. Rep. Chellie Pingree, who represents Maine's 1st District. source: stateside.com

In their own words

What the candidate says they will work on, from their campaign site or their answers to news-outlet questionnaires. We list it as stated and do not rate it.

  • An affordability agenda aimed at housing, health care, energy and property taxes. source: stateside.com
  • $100 million a year for housing: construction, help for first-time buyers, redevelopment, and faster permits. source: yahoo.com
  • Have utility regulators penalize poor service and limit rate increases; expand heat pumps, efficiency programs and local solar. source: stateside.com
  • A state-backed health plan, state power over drug prices, and programs to add primary care workers. source: stateside.com
  • Widen the Property Tax Fairness Credit, double the homestead exemption, and give towns more ways to raise local revenue. source: stateside.com
