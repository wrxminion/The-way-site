Judges on the ballot

In Michigan, judges are listed without party labels in the nonpartisan part of the ballot. The two Michigan Supreme Court seats are listed first below. Court of Appeals seats and a first set of county and local judges follow. Trial court judges are elected on the nonpartisan ballot.

Michigan Supreme Court: vote for 2

Six candidates are on the ballot for two eight-year seats, and the two with the most votes win. The seats are the two now held by Megan Cavanagh and Noah Hood. The ballot shows no party labels, but each candidate was picked at a party convention. Official candidate list: source: Michigan Bureau of Elections

  • Megan Cavanagh (Democratic Party nominee) source: Bridge Michigan source: Michigan PublicSitting justice and chief justice, chosen by her colleagues in 2025. First elected to the court in 2018. Practiced at a Detroit law firm before the court and earlier worked as an appellate attorney. Her father, Michael Cavanagh, served on the court from 1983 to 2015.
  • Noah Hood (Democratic Party nominee) source: Bridge Michigan source: Michigan PublicSitting justice, appointed by Gov. Gretchen Whitmer in 2025 to fill a vacancy, now running for a full eight-year term. Earlier served on the Michigan Court of Appeals and the Wayne County Circuit Court, and was an assistant U.S. attorney.
  • Michael D. Warren Jr. (Republican Party nominee, also endorsed by the Libertarian Party) source: Bridge Michigan source: Michigan PublicNot a sitting justice. Judge on the Oakland County Circuit Court since 2002. Served on the state Board of Education from 1999 to 2002 and teaches constitutional law at Cooley Law School.
  • Casandra Morse-Bills (Republican Party nominee) source: Bridge Michigan source: Michigan PublicNot a sitting justice. Presiding judge of the 23rd Judicial Circuit (Alcona, Arenac, Iosco and Oscoda counties), on the bench since 2018 and a former elected prosecutor. A former Oscoda County clerk has filed a federal whistleblower lawsuit that claims the clerk was fired for revealing that Morse-Bills exchanged text messages with a juror in a sexual assault case. In the Michigan Public interview she calls the claim false, says she spoke with an alternate juror after the trial ended, and says she expects the case to be dismissed. The lawsuit is pending and these claims are unproven.
  • Thomas W. Howe (U.S. Taxpayers Party nominee) source: Bridge Michigan source: Michigan PublicNot a sitting justice. A retired automotive engineer from Fenton who is not an attorney. Michigan Public reports he does not meet the state requirements to serve as a justice (a law license and five years of practice).
  • Jody White (U.S. Taxpayers Party nominee) source: Bridge Michigan source: Michigan PublicNot a sitting justice. A real estate broker in Southeast Michigan who ran for the state House in 2018 and the state Senate in 2022. Not an attorney. Michigan Public reports she does not meet the state requirements to serve as a justice.

County, district and local judges

Coverage: this section covers Oakland County (all judicial seats on its list) and Wayne County (Third Circuit, Probate and Court of Appeals names). It does NOT cover the other 81 counties, and it does not match Wayne County district court judges to their districts. We could not open the Bureau of Elections report for the general election, the Kent County list (the file would not open for us) or Macomb County. Check your sample ballot.

How Michigan fills these seats: circuit, probate and district court judges are elected on the nonpartisan part of the ballot for six-year terms. These are contested elections, not retention votes. When a seat is listed with one name, the candidate is unopposed on that list. When a seat is listed with no name, no candidate had filed when the list was made. A governor appointment fills a vacancy until the next election.
