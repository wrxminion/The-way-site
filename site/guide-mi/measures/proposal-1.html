Proposal 1: Constitutional conventionHold a convention to rewrite the state constitution

Automatic constitutional convention question: Automatic question under Article XII, Section 3 of the Michigan Constitution, Automatic question under Article XII, Section 3 of the Michigan Constitution. Passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Bureau of Elections ballot questions (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Every 16 years, Michigan's constitution (the state's basic rulebook) makes voters decide whether to hold a convention to rewrite it. If Yes wins, voters would elect one delegate from each state Senate and state House district within six months. The delegates would meet in Lansing starting October 5, 2027, with no deadline to finish. Any new constitution they write would go to voters at least 90 days after the convention ends. If voters turn it down, the current constitution stays. (votebeat.org ↗)

Biggest change

  • Starts a process, not a new constitution: voters would elect 148 delegates (one per state Senate and House district), and any draft they write must later be approved by voters. source: wcmu.org
  • A convention would have no limit on what it could take up. Delegates could write a whole new constitution or propose specific amendments. source: michamber.com

Who it affects most: Every Michigan resident, since a convention could propose changes to any part of the constitution, including voting rules, taxes, abortion rights and how amendments get on the ballot.

Cost to the state: Unknown. No official cost estimate for the delegate elections or the convention was found.

What your vote means

YES

Voters elect 148 delegates in 2027 to write a new or revised constitution, which voters would then accept or reject at a later election.

NO

No convention is held; the current constitution stays as it is, and the question returns in 2042.

Our one-line summary. Michigan does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Article XII, Section 3 of the current constitution puts this question on the ballot automatically every 16 years. If voters say no, it comes back in 2042. source: michamber.com
  • The current constitution was written at a 1961-62 convention, took effect in 1964, and is Michigan's fourth. Voters turned down a new convention in 1978, 1994 and 2010; in 2010 it failed 67% to 33%. source: wcmu.org
  • Recent changes to voting rules came through voter-approved amendments: Election Day registration and no-excuse absentee voting (2018), and early voting and a permanent absentee list (2022). A convention could keep or remove them. source: wcmu.org

Why supporters want it

  • House Speaker Matt Hall (R) said at the 2025 Mackinac Policy Conference that "they stuff so many things in this constitution," pointing to the 2018 voter-approved amendments on marijuana and the independent redistricting commission. source: votebeat.org
  • Senate Minority Leader Aric Nesbitt (R) has said he wants to update many parts of the constitution, including how university boards are chosen. source: votebeat.org

Why opponents object

  • The coalition Protect MI Constitution from Special Interests says a full convention "opens a Pandora's Box" with "potentially devastating consequences for Michigan citizens for decades to come." source: votebeat.org
  • Michigan Chamber: "A Constitutional Convention is a long, drawn-out process that risks years of uncertainty and gridlock in Michigan." League of Women Voters of Michigan: "Voting access could be stripped away." source: michamber.com
  • Former U.S. Rep. Dan Kildee (D): "I'm concerned what reopening the entirety of the state constitution might mean for this state." Former Attorney General Bill Schuette (R): "A constitutional convention would just be a circus." source: wnem.com

Who backs each side

  • For: no campaign committee supporting Proposal 1 had registered as of July 2026, per state campaign finance records. Against: Protect MI Constitution from Special Interests, whose members include Voters Not Politicians, the League of Women Voters of Michigan, the ACLU of Michigan, the Michigan AFL-CIO, the Michigan Council of Carpenters & Millwrights, the Detroit Regional Chamber, the Michigan Chamber of Commerce, the West Michigan Policy Forum and the Michigan League of Conservation Voters. As of late April 2026 it had raised more than $680,000, including $385,000 from The Fairness Project, a Washington, D.C., group. source: votebeat.org
  • Also opposed: the Michigan Education Association and Business Leaders for Michigan. source: michamber.com
