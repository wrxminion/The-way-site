Supreme Court justices on the ballot

In New York, the "Supreme Court" is the main trial court, not the top court. Justices are elected by judicial district for 14-year terms, and they can run on party lines like other candidates. 37 seats are up in 11 of the 13 districts; the 6th and 7th districts have none this year. The top court, the Court of Appeals, is appointed by the governor and is not on the ballot.

How to read this page

Each name below is on the certified ballot. Party lines in parentheses are the lines the candidate was nominated on, so one person can appear on several lines and gets the votes from each. Where we found a party convention result or a screening panel result, we say so and link it. A screening panel is a group a party or bar association sets up to review candidates; it is not the State of New York and its finding is one view. Where we have not yet found more, the entry shows only the name and party line.

Jump to a district: 1st · 2nd · 3rd · 4th · 5th · 8th · 9th · 10th · 11th · 12th · 13th. The 6th and 7th districts have no seats up this year. County, district and local judges are in their own section further down. Not sure of your district? Find it on your voter lookup record ↗.
