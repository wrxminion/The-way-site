Amendment · every Florida voter

Amendment 2No property tax on farm equipment

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Ballot No. 2, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as House Joint Resolution 1215 (2025) ↗. Passes with at least 60% of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Division of Elections full-text file (PDF) ↗

In plain words

"Tangible personal property" means business belongings you can touch that aren't land or buildings: tractors, tools, machines, sometimes furniture. Florida counties tax them every year. Amendment 2 ends that tax for such belongings on farmland that are used for farming or agritourism (farm visits and tours that bring in money). (mynews13.com ↗)

Example

Illustration only: a farm with $200,000 of taxable equipment, in a county where the total tax rate is $15 for every $1,000 of value, pays about $3,000 a year on it today and $0 if Amendment 2 passes.

Biggest change

  • Farm and ranch owners (or the people who lease the land) would no longer pay local property tax on business belongings that normally stay on farmland and are used to grow or raise farm products or for agritourism. State economists estimate local governments would collect about $31 million a year less. source: mynews13.com

Who it affects most: Farmers, ranchers and agritourism businesses, and the counties, schools and special districts that collect the tax, mostly in rural areas.

Cost to the state: None for the state. Local governments would collect about $31.0 million a year less, starting in the 2027-28 budget year (state Revenue Estimating Conference).

What your vote means

YES

Tractors, tools and other business belongings kept on farmland and used for farming or agritourism stop being taxed, starting in 2027.

NO

Those farm belongings keep being taxed the same way as other business equipment.

Our one-line summary. Florida does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with 60% of the votes cast on it.

  • The Legislature voted in 2025 to put the question on the ballot. It changes Article VII, Section 3 of the Florida Constitution. source: mynews13.com

Why supporters want it

  • State Rep. Danny Alvarez (R-Riverview): "Tangible personal property tax is a pretty offensive thing to me." source: mynews13.com

Why opponents object

  • State Rep. Anna Eskamani (D-Orlando) warned that "large actors tap into this tax break multiple times to again give an unfair advantage to big guys over small guys." source: mynews13.com

Who backs each side

  • For: the Legislature, which put it on the ballot. Against: some Democratic lawmakers. We have not found a campaign committee or reported campaign money for either side. source: mynews13.com

Claim check

We check facts that people on each side state about this measure. Verdicts: True, Mostly true, Mixed, Mostly false, False, or Too early to tell.

  • Rep. Anna Eskamani (D), opposes it: says Amendment 2 would cost local governments about $30 million a year. True
    What we found: The state Revenue Estimating Conference says local property tax money would drop by $31.0 million in the 2027-28 budget year. The loss grows to $32.3 million in 2028-29 and $33.6 million in 2029-30. The state's estimate shows no effect on state revenue. source: Florida Senate bill analysis source: Spectrum News 13
  • Rep. Danny Alvarez (R) and Sen. Keith Truenow (R), the sponsors, supports it: says farmers are taxed on their land and again on their equipment, and Amendment 2 ends the tax on the equipment. True
    What we found: Today, farm land is taxed, and movable property like tractors and tools is taxed as 'tangible personal property' after the first $25,000 of value, which is already exempt. Amendment 2 would exempt this property if it is usually kept on land classified as agricultural, is used to grow farm products or for agritourism, and is owned by the landowner or leaseholder. It would start with the 2027 tax year. source: Florida Senate bill analysis source: Florida Senate bill summary
  • Rep. Anna Eskamani (D), opposes it: says Amendment 2 has no limit, so most of the benefit goes to a handful of large agribusinesses, not small farms. Too early to tell
    What we found: It is true that the amendment sets no dollar cap on the exemption; the Legislature may set conditions by law. The state analysis does not say how many farms would benefit or how the $31.0 million in savings would be split between large and small operations. Small farms with under $25,000 in equipment value already pay no tax on it under current law. source: Florida Senate bill analysis source: amendment text (Division of Elections)
  • Hillsborough County Democrats, opposes it: says Amendment 2 would take money from local services that rely on property taxes. Too early to tell
    What we found: The state estimates local governments would collect $31.0 million less in 2027-28, rising to $33.6 million in 2029-30. The amendment does not say which services, if any, would be cut; each county, city or district would decide whether to cut, raise rates, or use other money. No political committee had registered for or against the amendment as of recent tracking. source: Florida Senate bill analysis source: Spectrum News 13

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Division of Elections' full-text file ↗.

EXEMPTION OF TANGIBLE PERSONAL PROPERTY ON AGRICULTURAL LAND FROM TAXATION.—

Proposing an amendment to the State Constitution to exempt tangible personal property habitually located or typically present on land classified as agricultural, used in the production of agricultural products or for agritourism activities, and owned by the landowner or leaseholder of the agricultural land from ad valorem taxation. If approved this amendment would first apply for tax years beginning January 1, 2027.
