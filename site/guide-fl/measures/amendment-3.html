Amendment 3Bigger homestead tax break, lower cap on other property

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Ballot No. 3, placed on the ballot by the Legislature as House Joint Resolution 1F (2026 special session) ↗. Passes with at least 60% of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Division of Elections full-text file (PDF) ↗

In plain words

"Homestead exemption" means the part of your home's value that property taxes skip, if it is the home you live in. Today non-school taxes skip about $50,000 and school taxes skip $25,000. Amendment 3 would raise the non-school amount to $250,000 by 2028. It lowers the annual cap on how fast the assessed value of non-homestead property can grow, from 10% to 5%, for non-school taxes; tax bills also depend on rates and other rules. (wlrn.org ↗)

Example

Illustration only: a home you live in, taxed on $300,000, in a place where city and county taxes add up to $12 for every $1,000 of value. Today those taxes apply to about $250,000 of value: about $3,000 a year. In 2028 they would apply to $50,000: about $600 a year. School taxes, which don't change, are left out of this example. Florida Realtors, who back Amendment 3, estimate the average homeowner would save about $1,000 in 2027 and $1,800 in 2028. (wlrn.org ↗)

Biggest change

  • For the home you live in (your "homestead"), city, county and other non-school property taxes would skip the first $150,000 of its taxed value in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, then more each year with inflation. School taxes are not changed. source: wlrn.org
  • For other property (rentals, second homes, businesses), the taxed value could go up at most 5% a year instead of 10%. source: wlrn.org
  • Cities and counties could use property tax only for a listed set of uses: public safety, schools, roads and other infrastructure, flood control and natural resources, debt payments, employee retirement, and running local government, including other spending their officials approve unless state law forbids it. source: initiativepetitions.dos.fl.gov
  • People moving to Florida after 2026 would get only the old $50,000 break for their first four years with a homestead exemption. Starting in 2030, a county or city could shorten that wait by a two-thirds vote. source: initiativepetitions.dos.fl.gov
  • Counties and cities could choose to raise the break further, up to the home's full value. Special districts (such as fire or hospital districts) could do so only with a local vote. source: initiativepetitions.dos.fl.gov

Who it affects most: Homeowners who live in their home (who pay less), cities, counties and special districts (which collect less), and the people who use their services. Owners of rentals, second homes and businesses don't get the bigger homestead break, but their property's taxed value could rise only 5% a year instead of 10%.

Cost to the state: The official Senate analysis ↗ estimates local governments (non-school taxes) would collect $4.95 billion less in 2027-28, $8.78 billion less in 2028-29 and $11.86 billion less a year on a recurring basis. That is a forecast of lower local revenue, not a guarantee of service cuts, and the state's own administrative cost is separate. Critics' larger projections, such as one reported by Florida Phoenix, are their own estimates.

What your vote means

YES

City and county taxes would skip the first $150,000 of the taxed value of the home you live in from 2027 and $250,000 from 2028, and the taxed value of other property could rise at most 5% a year instead of 10%.

NO

Your home keeps today's smaller break (about $50,000 of value skipped for city and county taxes), and other property can still rise up to 10% a year.

Our one-line summary. Florida does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with 60% of the votes cast on it.

  • The Legislature passed it in a 2026 special session, with strong backing from Gov. Ron DeSantis. source: wflx.com
  • In August 2026 Leon County Circuit Judge David Frank ruled the original ballot wording "clearly and conclusively defective" and ordered the Attorney General to rewrite it. The rewrite removed a list of promised benefits and added that property taxes must be used for listed services. source: wjhg.com

Why supporters want it

  • Gov. Ron DeSantis (R): "You vote for it, you'll pay less. End of story." source: wflx.com
  • Margy Grant, CEO of Florida Realtors: "Amendment 3 is ultimately about giving Florida homeowners actual meaningful tax relief." source: wlrn.org

Why opponents object

  • Polk County Sheriff Grady Judd (R): "Amendment 3 doesn't protect law enforcement, firefighters or emergency services, it defunds them." source: wlrn.org
  • Miami-Dade County Mayor Daniella Levine Cava: "It would leave us paying more while getting less." She estimates the county would lose about 10% of its general fund money in the first year and 20% in the second. source: wflx.com
  • Jackson Peel of Save Our Services, which sued over the ballot wording, called it a "scam and tax shift" and said, "We're going to lose things like health care services, money for EMTs..." source: wjhg.com

Who backs each side

  • For: Gov. Ron DeSantis and the Vote Yes on 3 committee, which has received $18 million from Florida Realtors. source: wlrn.org
  • Against: Save Our Services; sheriffs, police and fire chiefs; local officials such as Miami-Dade's mayor, with labor, business and civic groups; environmental groups 1000 Friends of Florida and the Sierra Club; and the Florida Policy Institute, a research group. We have not found reported totals for the opposing side's money. source: wlrn.org

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Division of Elections' full-text file ↗.

INCREASED HOMESTEAD EXEMPTION; LOWER CAP ON INCREASES IN NON-HOMESTEAD PROPERTY ASSESSMENTS.—

This amendment increases the homestead exemption, for all non-school taxes, to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028, and adjusts for inflation thereafter. It requires the Legislature to prescribe a uniform procedure for counties and municipalities, for their respective levies, to increase the homestead exemption up to full assessed value, and allows special districts, subject to referendum approval, to do the same.

Persons who are not Florida residents on December 31, 2026, will receive the existing homestead exemption upon qualifying for a homestead exemption, with the increased homestead exemption beginning with the fifth year of exemption, to the extent permitted by the U.S. Constitution.

This amendment reduces the annual cap on assessment increases for non-homestead properties from 10% to 5%.

This amendment requires counties and municipalities to use property taxes solely for public safety, education and schools, infrastructure, natural resources, bond debt service, retirement benefits for employees, and operations and administration. Other expenditures may be approved by county officers or county or municipal governing bodies unless prohibited by general law, notwithstanding Article VII, Section 9(a) of the Florida Constitution, which allows counties and municipalities to levy property taxes for their respective purposes.

This amendment takes effect January 1, 2027.
