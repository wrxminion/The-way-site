Judges on the retention ballot

Florida's Supreme Court justices and appeals court judges don't run against opponents. Voters decide whether each one stays in office for another six years (yes or no). Trial judges who face opponents are elected in nonpartisan races.

  • Each justice and appeals judge is on the ballot as a yes/no question: "Shall Justice (or Judge) ... be retained in office?" A majority of yes votes keeps them for another six-year term; if no wins, the governor appoints a replacement from a list sent by a nominating commission. source: ballotpedia.org
  • Supreme Court justices are on every Florida ballot. Appeals court judges are on the ballot only in the counties their court covers. source: ballotpedia.org

What each court does

  • Supreme Court: Florida's highest court (7 justices). It has the final word on Florida law, hears every death penalty appeal, and checks that citizen-proposed amendments are clear enough to go on the ballot. Justices are appointed by the governor and then face yes/no retention votes every six years. source: flcourts.gov
  • District Court of Appeal: Florida's middle-level appeals courts (six of them, each covering a region). They hear most appeals from trial courts, and for most cases their ruling is the final word. Judges face yes/no retention votes every six years. source: flcourts.gov

Circuit Court runoffs

Unofficial results on the Florida Election Watch circuit page (read November 4, 2026): 9th Circuit Group 1, Hampton-Johnson 64.69 percent and Hart 35.31 percent. 11th Circuit Group 5, McNeil 51.29 percent and Segura 48.71 percent. 11th Circuit Group 69, Baez 58.33 percent and Jones-Peabody 41.67 percent. 17th Circuit Group 52, Hamilton 53.79 percent and Fry 46.21 percent. source: FL Election Watch, circuit

    Temika Hampton-Johnson and Charles Hart source: FL Division of Elections source: WLRN (unreviewed)    Both nonpartisan. Both beat incumbent Judge Mikaela Nix in the August primary. News coverage reports Hampton-Johnson led with about 44 percent and Hart had 28 percent. Not independently reviewed.

    Arthur LeMar "Marty" McNeil and Monica Segura source: FL Division of Elections source: WLRN (unreviewed)    Both nonpartisan. Open contest after the primary. News coverage describes McNeil as a public defender and Segura as corporate counsel at an insurance company. Not independently reviewed.

    Rita Maria Baez and Bonita Jones-Peabody source: FL Division of Elections source: WLRN (unreviewed)    Both nonpartisan. News coverage reports a near tie in the primary, about 37 percent to 35 percent. Not independently reviewed.

    Tamar Hamilton and David Andrew Fry source: FL Division of Elections source: WLRN (unreviewed)    Both nonpartisan. News coverage describes Hamilton as a private-practice attorney and special magistrate and Fry as an assistant public defender. Not independently reviewed.

Every other circuit judge seat in Florida was unopposed or decided in August. The Division list marks those candidates as won or unopposed. Unopposed judges do not appear on the ballot.
