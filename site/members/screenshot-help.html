How to take a clean screenshot

A screenshot is a picture of your screen. A reviewer can use it to see what you saw. A clean screenshot shows only the page. It does not show your other apps, your accounts or your messages.

A screenshot is always optional. You can skip it.

The short version

  1. Open the page in a private window.
  2. Take a picture of only that window.
  3. Look at the picture before you share it.

Step 1. Open a private window

A private window does not use your sign-ins. It helps keep your own accounts out of the picture. Open the page again in the private window.

On a computer
  • Chrome: press Ctrl + Shift + N. On a Mac press Command + Shift + N. This is called Incognito.
  • Edge: press Ctrl + Shift + N. This is called InPrivate.
  • Firefox: press Ctrl + Shift + P. On a Mac press Command + Shift + P.
  • Safari on a Mac: press Shift + Command + N.

You can also open the browser menu and look for "New private window" or "New Incognito window". Menu names can differ.

On a phone or tablet
  • Open your browser. Look for the tabs button or the menu button.
  • Pick "New Incognito tab" or "Private". The name depends on your browser.
A private window is not magic. It does not sign you in to sites. Some browsers still show the bookmarks bar, and some still show extension buttons. That is why step 2 matters: take a picture of only the browser window and check the result.

In the private window, close any other tabs. Then only one tab shows in the picture.

Step 2. Take the screenshot

Steps can differ by phone model and computer. If these do not work for you, search the web for "screenshot" and your device name.

Keep the address bar of the browser in the picture. It shows the web address. Reviewers need it. Do not include the taskbar, the dock or your other open apps.

Windows
  1. Press Windows key + Shift + S. The screen gets darker and a small bar appears at the top.
  2. If the bar has a choice for a window, pick it and click your browser window. If not, pick the box shape and drag a box around only the browser window.
  3. A small note appears in the corner. Click it to open the picture and save it.
Mac
  1. Press Shift + Command + 4. The pointer turns into a small cross.
  2. Press the Space bar. The pointer turns into a camera.
  3. Click your browser window. Only that window is saved.
  4. The picture is usually saved on your Desktop.

Another way: press Shift + Command + 5 to see a small bar with choices.

iPhone
  1. Show the page you want.
  2. Press the side button and the volume up button at the same time. Let go fast.
  3. The picture is saved in your Photos.

Older iPhones with a Home button use different buttons.

Android
  1. Show the page you want.
  2. Press the power button and the volume down button at the same time. Let go fast.
  3. The picture is saved in your photos or gallery.

Steps can differ by phone model.

Chromebook
  1. Press Ctrl + Shift + the Show windows key. That key looks like a box with lines next to it.
  2. A small bar appears. Pick the choice for a window, or the choice for part of the screen.
  3. Click your browser window, or drag a box around it.

On a phone: hide the top of the screen

A phone screenshot can show the little icons and message alerts at the top. Before you take it, turn on Do Not Disturb or Focus mode. After you take it, you can crop the top off. Open the picture and look for Edit, then Crop.

Step 3. Check before you share

Look at your picture. Then answer each question.

  • Is there a message alert or a pop-up on it?
  • Can you see other apps, other tabs or a taskbar?
  • Can you see your name, email, phone number, address or a photo of you?
  • Can you see the web address of the page?
  • Does the picture show the part of the page that matters?

If you see something private, cover it, crop it away, or take the picture again. If you cannot fix it, leave the screenshot out. Your review still counts without one.
