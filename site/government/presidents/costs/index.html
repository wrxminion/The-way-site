What a president costs

Travel, protection, money paid to a president’s own property, family trips, who gets the contract money, and the White House’s own budget. There is no official total for any president, so every number here says where it came from and what kind of number it is.

The rule

The same rule for every president.

Presidents are in time order. Every figure carries one of these labels. Nothing is added up across labels.

  • Record A government record: a budget, contract, GAO report, FOIA release or financial disclosure.
  • News report A newspaper or magazine report of government records.
  • Advocacy group A count by a group that takes sides, built from government records.
  • Estimate Someone multiplied a rate. Not a bill or a record.
  • Statement What someone said. Not a record of money.

Money is assigned to the president in office on the date of each contract action. Layer 6 / Where the detail stops

Down to the toilet paper?

No. Public detail stops well before single purchases.

$15,000

Purchases at or under this amount (the micro-purchase threshold since October 1, 2025, up from $10,000) do not have to be reported to the federal contract database, and government purchase card buys under it are left out. So toilet paper, coffee or a fast food order will almost never show up item by item.

Record Federal Register, Aug 27, 2025: govinfo.gov ↗ Federal Acquisition Regulation 4.606: acquisition.gov ↗

About $1 million

The finest public line for the White House’s everyday buying: “Supplies and materials” for a whole year.

Record Fiscal 2027 budget, object class tables: whitehouse.gov ↗
