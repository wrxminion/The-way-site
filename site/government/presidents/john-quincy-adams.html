One thing to research

The Tariff of 1828

Became law May 19, 1828.

A very high protective tariff, with a 38 percent tax on some imported goods and 45 percent on certain imported raw materials. It passed the House 105 to 94 and the Senate 26 to 21, and Adams signed it.

Supporters said

The main goal was to protect Northeastern factories from cheaper European goods. Western farmers and Mid-Atlantic manufacturers argued that a stronger nation served the whole country.

Critics said

Southern opponents, above all in South Carolina, said it hurt the Southern economy and was unconstitutional, and named it the "Tariff of Abominations." It led to the nullification crisis of 1832 and 1833.
