Layer 1 / The two parts

One agency, 12 banks.

Washington, D.C.

The Board of Governors

A federal agency. The Fed says the Board “is an agency of the federal government that reports to and is directly accountable to Congress.”

  • 7 governors, each for a staggered 14-year term
  • Nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate
  • The Chair and Vice Chair serve four-year terms and can be appointed again

Source: Federal Reserve Board: federalreserve.gov ↗

12 districts

The 12 Federal Reserve Banks

Each one is a corporation created under federal law. The Federal Reserve Act says each bank “shall become a body corporate.” The Fed calls the 12 banks and their 24 branches “the operating arms of the Federal Reserve System.”

  • 1 Boston
  • 2 New York
  • 3 Philadelphia

Where rates are set

The Federal Open Market Committee

The committee that “sets crucial U.S. monetary policy at meetings held at least 8 times each year.” It has 12 voting members: the 7 governors, the president of the New York Reserve Bank, and 4 of the other 11 Reserve Bank presidents, who take turns in one-year terms.

Source: Federal Reserve Board ↗

What Congress asked for

Its goals

The Fed says it sets monetary policy “to promote maximum employment and stable prices in the U.S. economy,” the goals set by Congress.

Congress created the Fed with the Federal Reserve Act, which President Woodrow Wilson signed on December 23, 1913.

Layer 2 / Who owns the banks?

Banks hold the stock, with strings attached.

The answer depends on what “own” means, so here are the rules as written.

Who holds the stockMember banksHolding it is a condition of joining the Federal Reserve System
Can they sell it?NoThe law says the shares “shall not be transferred or hypothecated” (sold or pledged)
DividendCapped by law6% a year for banks with $10 billion or less in assets. For larger banks, the lower of 6% or the 10-year Treasury rate
Run for profit?NoThe Fed says the Reserve Banks “are not operated for profit”
How do different sources put it?

The Board of Governors: “The Federal Reserve System is not “owned” by anyone,” and “owning Reserve Bank stock is quite different from owning stock in a private company.”

The St. Louis Fed, asked if the Fed is public or private: “The answer is both.” It describes the Board as “an independent government agency” and the Reserve Banks as “set up like private corporations,” and says the Reserve Banks “are not a part of the federal government.”

FactCheck.org (2008): member banks “cannot sell or trade their stock or even use that stock as collateral to borrow money.” Its 6 percent dividend line was written before the current lower limit for large banks, so the dividend above comes from the law itself.

Layer 3 / Who picks whom

The President, the Senate, the Board and the banks.

  1. The Presidentnominates the 7 governors
  2. The Senateconfirms them
  3. Member bankselect 6 of the 9 directors of their Reserve Bank: 3 Class A directors who represent the banks, and 3 Class B directors who represent the public
  4. The Board of Governorsappoints the other 3, Class C directors, who represent the public
  5. Class B and C directorsappoint their Reserve Bank’s president for five years, with the Board’s approval

Follow the money

Where the profits go.

After paying expenses and the capped dividend, and keeping a surplus that the law also caps, the Reserve Banks must send their net earnings to the U.S. Treasury. The Fed says they “are required by law to transfer net earnings to the U.S. Treasury.”
