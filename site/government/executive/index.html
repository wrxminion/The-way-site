What can the president do?

The president carries out federal laws, commands the armed forces, makes treaties with the consent of two-thirds of the Senate, nominates judges and other officers for Senate confirmation, and can sign or veto bills.

Constitution, Article II: archives.gov ↗
Who can be president?

A natural-born citizen, at least 35 years old, who has lived in the United States for 14 years. No one can be elected president more than twice.

    Layer 2 / The departments

    15 departments do most of the work.

    Their heads form most of the president’s Cabinet. The heads shown are today’s. Pick a president above to see who led each department in that president’s years. They are listed in the order of the presidential line of succession. Open a question for the next layer.

    Where the past heads come from

    Each department’s own list of past heads is used wherever it could be read, and each card links to it. Where that list gives only names or years, or could not be read, the dates come from the United States Cabinet Appointments Dataset by Tait Chamberlain, built from Congressional Research Service records (CC BY 3.0), and, from 2017 on, from Wikipedia. Those rows say so in small print.

    Acting heads who filled in between appointments are not shown, so a gap means no appointed head is listed for those days. Where the sources disagree, the department’s own list is shown. Every disagreement and correction is written down in check.txt ↗.

                                  Layer 3 / The building

                                  The White House.

                                  The president’s home and office, at 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue in Washington, D.C.

                                  When was it built?

                                  George Washington selected the site in 1791, and the cornerstone was laid the following year. President John Adams and Abigail Adams moved into the still-unfinished house.

                                  Source: whitehouse.gov ↗
                                  Was it ever burned?

                                  Yes. During the War of 1812 the British set fire to the President’s House. James Hoban, its original architect, was appointed to rebuild it.

                                  Source: whitehouse.gov ↗
                                  How big is it?

                                  The Residence has 132 rooms, 35 bathrooms and 6 levels.

                                  Source: whitehouse.gov ↗
                                  Where does the president work?

                                  In the West Wing, first built as a temporary office building. President William Howard Taft had the Oval Office built inside an enlarged office wing.

                                  Source: whitehouse.gov ↗
                                  What is changing?

                                  The White House says a new ballroom will replace the East Wing, which was built in 1902. It says construction began in September 2025.

                                  Source: whitehouse.gov ↗
                                  Who takes care of it?

                                  The White House is owned by the American people and cared for by the National Park Service.

                                  Source: nps.gov ↗
