Proposition 37: Home buyer loansState-backed loans to help middle-income buyers of new homes

Citizen initiative (statute): , placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Initiative statute). Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Official Voter Information Guide (PDF) ↗

In plain words

The California Housing Finance Agency could borrow up to $25 billion from investors and use it to make loans that cover up to 17% of the price of a newly built home. Buyers must have lived in California for one year, put at least 3% down, live in the home, and earn less than twice the typical (median) income in their area. Homes must be new (or the first sale of a converted non-home building) and priced below about $1 million to $1.5 million, depending on the county. The bonds are paid back by the buyers' loan payments, not the state budget. Builders who meet higher labor standards get more flexible rules on construction-defect claims. (sos.ca.gov (Official Voter Information Guide) ↗)

Example

Illustration only: on an $800,000 new home, a buyer puts $24,000 down (3%), the new program lends up to $136,000 (17%), and a regular mortgage covers the rest. (sos.ca.gov (Official Voter Information Guide) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Creates a "middle-class homeownership loan" of up to 17% of a new home's price, funded by up to $25 billion in revenue bonds. source: lao.ca.gov (Legislative Analyst's Office)
  • Developers can choose a "qualified builder option" with higher labor standards in return for more flexible construction-defect rules. source: lao.ca.gov (Legislative Analyst's Office)

Who it affects most: Middle-income first buyers of newly built homes and home builders.

Cost to the state: No direct state or local costs; the bonds are repaid by buyers' loan payments (Legislative Analyst).

What your vote means

YES

The state housing agency can borrow up to $25 billion to lend buyers of new homes up to 17% of the price, repaid by the buyers.

NO

No new state loan program for buyers of new homes is created.

Our one-line summary. The official "What your vote means" statements are in the Secretary of State's Official Voter Information Guide, linked below.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with a simple majority.

  • A coalition of real estate interests and labor unions placed the initiative on the ballot. source: CalMatters

Why supporters want it

  • Official argument in favor: "Zero Taxpayer Cost—No Taxpayer Risk"; supporters say it helps working families who can afford a mortgage but cannot save a large down payment, and will spur home building. Signed by Danny Curtin (California Conference of Carpenters), Fiona Ma (CalHFA board member and State Treasurer) and David West (California State Commanders Veterans Council). source: voterguide.sos.ca.gov (Secretary of State)

Why opponents object

  • No argument against Proposition 37 was submitted for the official voter guide. source: voterguide.sos.ca.gov (Secretary of State)
  • Opponents named by CalMatters (Reform California, League of Women Voters) say the state should not be in the mortgage market, the measure does not address why housing is expensive, and it pushes buyers into deeper debt. source: CalMatters

Who backs each side

  • For: United Nurses Associations of California, California Conference of Carpenters, State Treasurer Fiona Ma, California Association of Realtors, SEIU, Xavier Becerra, California Democratic Party. Against: Reform California, League of Women Voters. source: CalMatters
  • Money (Secretary of State, through Oct. 6, 2026): "Yes on 37, California Homeownership" reported $19,618,927. No opposing committee was identified. source: sos.ca.gov (Secretary of State)

Official title copied word for word from the Official Voter Information Guide (page 40). The title and summary are prepared by the Attorney General.

Official summary by the Attorney General (tap to open)
Authorizes up to $25 billion in bonds to offer eligible buyers fixed-rate mortgages for up to 17% of the purchase price of a “qualified new home” (new construction or first sale of converted nonresidential property, priced below about $1 million–$1.5 million, depending on county, adjusted annually). Borrowers must be California residents for one year, occupy the home, earn less than twice the area’s median income, and pay at least 3% down. Requires that bonds be repaid by homeowners’ mortgage payments, not the State.
(sos.ca.gov (Official Voter Information Guide) ↗)
