Judges on the retention ballot

Kansas Supreme Court justices and Court of Appeals judges don't run against opponents. Voters decide whether each one stays in office (yes or no). KCUR reports seven judges on the ballot this year: two Supreme Court justices and five Court of Appeals judges. No Kansas judge has ever been removed in a retention vote.

The facts below are from one news report (source: KCUR via HPPR, Oct. 26, 2026), which draws the attorney survey results and performance grades from the Kansas Bar Association. We have not confirmed them against the Kansas Bar Association's own report or the Secretary of State's list. Ballotpedia now lists two Supreme Court seats (Justices Rosen and Walsh) and five Court of Appeals positions (2, 3, 5, 7 and 13) up for retention, which matches KCUR. It says Judges Stephen Hill and Thomas E. Malone did not file for retention, so they are not on the ballot. Ballotpedia, KS appeals (unreviewed)

Judges on your ballot

  • Kansas Supreme Court
    Eric S. Rosen source: KCUR via HPPR, Oct. 26, 2026    Chief Justice since January 2026. Appointed in 2005 by Gov. Kathleen Sebelius. In the Kansas Bar Association survey, 88 percent of attorneys said they would recommend retention and his overall performance grade was B+. Former Gov. Sam Brownback led a 2014 campaign to remove him; voters kept him by about five percentage points, and in 2020 by about 40 points. KCUR reports some Republican groups are lobbying against his retention this year. Voters decide yes or no.
  • Kansas Supreme Court
    Larkin E. Walsh source: KCUR via HPPR, Oct. 26, 2026    Appointed in 2025 by Gov. Laura Kelly. 91 percent of surveyed attorneys recommend retention; overall grade B+. In Kelly v. Kobach the court narrowly dismissed Gov. Kelly’s petition and Walsh wrote a concurring opinion that joined the court’s only Republican-appointed justice. Americans for Prosperity called her "yet another liberal progressive" appointed by Kelly, and KCUR reports some Republican groups are lobbying against her retention. Voters decide yes or no.
  • Kansas Court of Appeals
    Lesley Ann Isherwood source: KCUR via HPPR, Oct. 26, 2026    Appointed in 2021 by Gov. Kelly. 75 percent of surveyed attorneys recommend retention; overall grade C. Attorneys’ main concern is delay in delivering opinions. Before the bench she was a Sedgwick County prosecutor. Voters decide yes or no.
  • Kansas Court of Appeals
    Amy Fellows Cline source: KCUR via HPPR, Oct. 26, 2026    Appointed in 2020 by Gov. Kelly. 83 percent of surveyed attorneys recommend retention; overall grade B. Earlier at a full-service law firm. Voters decide yes or no.
  • Kansas Court of Appeals
    Kim R. Schroeder source: KCUR via HPPR, Oct. 26, 2026    Appointed in 2013 by Gov. Brownback, the only judge on this ballot appointed by a Republican. 89 percent of surveyed attorneys recommend retention; overall grade B+. Earlier a district court judge. Voters decide yes or no.
  • Kansas Court of Appeals
    Lori Ann Bolton Fleming source: KCUR via HPPR, Oct. 26, 2026    Appointed in 2025 by Gov. Kelly (Gov. Brownback had earlier appointed her to the 11th Judicial District, where she was chief judge). 91 percent of surveyed attorneys recommend retention; overall grade A. Voters decide yes or no.
  • Kansas Court of Appeals
    Jacy Hurst source: KCUR via HPPR, Oct. 26, 2026    Appointed in 2021 by Gov. Kelly. 78 percent of surveyed attorneys recommend retention; overall grade C. Attorneys’ main concern is delay in issuing opinions. Voters decide yes or no.

County, district and local judges

Coverage. Kansas picks district judges two ways, depending on the judicial district: partisan elections in some districts, and governor appointment from a nominating commission followed by retention votes in others. This section covers two of the 31 districts: Sedgwick County (18th District, partisan) and Johnson County (10th District, retention). Not covered: every other district, and county-level offices other than the judges named here. The Kansas Secretary of State candidate list page would not load for us, and its table is for state and national offices, so we used county sources. Read November 4, 2026.
