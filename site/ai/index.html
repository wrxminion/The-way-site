Why and where we use AI

We use AI to make large amounts of public information easier to explore, not to replace evidence, expertise or human accountability. AI can be wrong, incomplete or overconfident.

Yes, AI is used. But it never creates facts, and it cannot change them. AI only finds them or helps verify them. Every one is verified by a human, or the page says plainly that it is not.

What that promise means

  • A fact is what a source says. A law, a court filing, a vote record, a budget, a transcript. The source stays as it is. AI cannot edit it, and neither can we without a visible, dated correction.
  • AI finds and flags. It helps us locate the right document, pull out a date or an amount, and notice when two records disagree. A flag is a lead, not a finding.
  • AI also helps with wording and code. It drafts plain-language text and helps build the site. That text is marked, and a person fixes it. A draft is not a fact either.
  • People decide. A person compares each claim with its source before the page says it was checked. If no person has done that yet, the page says so in a label at the top.

Where AI may be used

  1. Research discovery: search terms, document leads, duplicate finding. Leads are not findings.
  2. Document processing: transcription help, extracting dates and amounts, link suggestions. We keep the original file.
  3. Analysis: flagging possible contradictions or data revisions. A pattern an AI finds does not prove intent, wrongdoing or cause.
  4. Writing and accessibility: plain-language drafts, alt text. AI-written prose is kept separate from quoted source text.
  5. Software and graphics: help building maps, timelines and tests. Illustrations made by AI are labeled and never passed off as archival records.
  6. Workflow: deciding what a person should check first, and keeping the correction log.

What AI does not decide

AI does not certify truth, declare anyone guilty, decide which beliefs are acceptable, or find legal violations. The Institute decides what is published, and a person checks each source against each claim before it is called checked.

Our promises about evidence

  • We keep the exact source, the date and the version where the law allows.
  • We separate primary records, news reports, later scholarship, AI-made guesses and unresolved allegations.
  • Material claims point to records you can open, and show whether a person has reviewed them.
  • Corrections stay visible with dates and reasons. We do not quietly rewrite history.

What the labels on AI-written pages mean

  1. Unchecked
  2. Skimmed
  3. Read
  4. Under review
  5. Confirmed accurate by a human

Voter guides, ballot measures, history cases, the government guide, news pages and research packages are written with AI. Each one carries a label that says how far a person has gone in checking it. Open the label for details.

  • AI written, unchecked: no person has looked at it yet. Treat it as a draft.
  • AI written, human skimmed: a person looked it over for obvious problems. This is a light check. The sources have not all been verified.
  • AI written, human read: a person read all of it. The sources have not all been verified.
  • Under review: a person is verifying the sources one by one, and some are done.
  • Confirmed accurate by a human: a person verified every source.

Stronger labels stay as they are. "Not yet reviewed by a person" and "AI draft, unchecked" are not replaced by these. Changes to a label are logged with who made them and when.

We would much rather have people do this work

We mean it. Reading sources and comparing them with claims is exactly the job a person should do. But there is far too much of it for a handful of us, and that is the only reason AI is in the loop at all.

If you can read carefully, you can help. Join as a member (18 and over) and help move pages up the ladder, one source at a time.

Why AI makes some of our graphics

Our images are fillers. A page needs a banner or an illustration, and we do not have a staff of artists to make hundreds of them. So an AI-made image holds the place until a person's work can take it. They are not archival records, they are never passed off as photographs of real events, and we label them as AI-made.

  • We would rather use real art. Every AI image is a stand-in with a job: keep the page from being bare while we find an artist.
  • We keep the images neutral on purpose. No faces of real people, flags, logos, party colors or text in the image, so a graphic never nudges how you read the facts.
  • Graphics never change the facts. Same rule as everything else on this page: the image decorates, the sources carry the content.

Do you make art? Help us retire the "AI slop."

That is our own nickname for the filler, and we mean it kindly: it is a placeholder, and you can replace it. If you paint, draw, photograph, design or make anything that fits our look, donate it.

  1. You are always credited. Every artist gets a credit by name or handle, as you choose. All of them.
  2. If your image is accepted, it replaces the AI image on that page.
  3. Then it joins a rotation. Accepted art goes into a pool that cycles across the site, so more people see it, and you stay credited wherever it shows.

We cannot pay. This is a donation, and we use only work you made yourself, not AI-generated art.

The terms, in plain words

  • Freely given. Donated art is freely given for fair use, and for publications and research documents that researchers or schools produce by or through the site.
  • Never sold. We will never sell, lease, rent or license any image.
  • No political ads. No political party or politician may use donated work in their ads.
