How a piece of research gets made

Yes, AI is used here. It never creates facts. It finds sources and suggests where to dig, and a person decides what is checked. Here is the path, step by step.

  1. A person

    A person has an idea

    A member asks a question or spots a claim worth looking into. It is about a policy or a record, never about whether a candidate or party is good or bad.

  2. AI helps

    AI refines it and finds sources

    AI sharpens the question, looks for documents that bear on it, and suggests deeper dives. It does not write facts. A fact is what a source says, and the source stays exactly as it is.

  3. AI helps

    A second AI runs a challenge check

    A different AI argues the other side. It hunts for weak or missing sources and flags counterarguments the first pass left out. It writes no facts and gives no verdict. Its flags are leads for the reviewer, and the reviewer decides which ones matter.

  4. AI helps

    It is posted as "needs review"

    The sources are organized and saved with their dates, so anyone can open them. The page carries the label AI written, unchecked until a person works on it.

  5. A person

    A person reviews it

    A member compares each claim with its sources. Each claim needs at least two sources, one of them a primary record, and we look at the strongest case on both sides. The label moves up the ladder as the work is done.

  6. A person

    Posted as human reviewed, added to the research packages

    Only when a person has checked every source does the page say Confirmed accurate by a human. Then it joins the research packages below. Readers also get the source check record for each package: which sources a person checked, when, and a saved copy of each source as it looked that day. If no person has finished, the page says so plainly.

How the site currently works

The operational notes below describe the site's stated workflow. The research paper above sets out the broader proposed standard.

What the status labels mean

The Development Ledger records each decision about the site. A row is marked LIVE_VERIFIED only after a person opened the live page and the date was written down. IMPLEMENTED_PENDING_REVIEW, or a row that says it was merged, means the work is in the repository but a person has not yet checked the live page.

On a page, a label such as "initial draft, not independently reviewed" stays until a person has compared each claim with its sources. We do not remove a caveat to make a page look finished.

Sources

  • A fact needs a source link. We do not publish invented numbers, quotes or sources.
  • Source preservation is in progress. The intended record includes a dated original file, screenshot where needed, readable text, a fingerprint and provenance. Coverage is incomplete; a citation does not establish that a preservation copy exists. Evidence copies are kept outside the public repository.
  • We respect robots.txt. Where a state host disallows automated download, the source is recorded as blocked and a second host is sought.
  • AI-written prose is kept apart from quoted source text. See How we use AI.

News

  • A collector runs every 2 hours and a weekly check re-runs the method. It gathers headlines and links by state.
  • The site shows the newest 200 items per state and 3,000 overall. A private archive keeps everything collected, with the time each item was fetched.
  • Links are collected automatically. A headline does not mean we agree with it, and a link may not lead where the headline suggests.
  • Tags (place and ballot measure) are applied by software and can be wrong; the uncertainty is shown on the page.

Code and changes

  • The site is static HTML, CSS and JavaScript. The source is public at github.com/wrxminion/The-way-site. The main branch is the live site and publishes within about 15 minutes of a merge.
  • Larger changes go through a pull request that the maintainer reviews. Small fixes and the news data are committed directly by the maintainer's setup.
  • Pages must work under a strict content security policy: no inline scripts, no inline styles, no outside images or fonts.
  • URLs and record IDs stay stable. A page that moves leaves a redirect.
  • Database definitions are in the repository under supabase/ as reference only; they are not deployed by the site.
