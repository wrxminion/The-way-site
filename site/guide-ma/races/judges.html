Judges on the ballot

We did not find any state judge or justice on Massachusetts's November 3, 2026 ballot. The governor appoints judges and the Governor's Council approves them.

Massachusetts judges are appointed, so no judge is on the ballot. The one elected court office is register of probate, a county court records officer who is not a judge. Names of the 2026 party nominees are in the county section below. Our Massachusetts guide also lists the Governor's Council races, because the council votes on the governor's judicial picks. Those are council seats, not judgeships.

What we found

  • How a judge is selected. The state publishes an explainer on how a judge is selected in Massachusetts. source: Mass.gov
  • Nominating commission. A judicial nominating commission, created by executive order, screens candidates for the governor. Governor Healey re-established it by executive order. source: Mass.gov
  • Council approval. Ballotpedia says judges are appointed by the governor with the advice and consent of the Governor's Council. source: ballotpedia.org (unreviewed)

County, district and local judges

What this covers. Registers of probate, the only elected office tied to the courts, in all 14 counties. The names are the party primary winners listed in the Massachusetts Elections Division archive for the September 1, 2026 primary, which we read on November 4, 2026. Each was the only candidate on their party's primary ballot. The archive does not list the general election ballot, so a county may also have unenrolled or other candidates that we did not see. What it does not cover. Clerks of courts: we found no 2026 clerk of courts election in the archive, so we list none. We did not read county sample ballots. Judges of the Probate and Family Court, District Court and all other courts are appointed.
