Question 7Allow single-family homes on 5,000 sq ft lots

Statewide ballot question: Law proposed by initiative petition: single family homes, placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Law proposed by initiative petition: single family homes). Passes with more YES than NO votes, and YES votes from at least 30% of all voters who cast a ballot.

Read the full text of the measure: Information for Voters page for this question ↗

In plain words

Allows a single-family home on any residential lot of at least 5,000 square feet that has public water and sewer. (sec.state.ma.us ↗)

Biggest change

  • Single-family homes allowed on lots of 5,000 sq ft or more source: sec.state.ma.us
  • 50 feet of frontage and public water and sewer required source: sec.state.ma.us
  • Local rules on height, setbacks, open space, parking, short-term rentals still allowed source: sec.state.ma.us
  • State housing office may issue rules source: sec.state.ma.us

Who it affects most: Home buyers and builders, landowners, local planning boards.

Cost to the state: The Executive Office of Administration and Finance says new housing could bring towns new tax revenue, but the size is hard to predict; no clear material cost.

What your vote means

YES

Allows single-family homes on residential lots of 5,000 sq ft or more, with some local rules.

NO

No change to the law on building single-family homes.

Our one-line summary. The state's Information for Voters booklet has the official statements of what a YES and a NO vote would do.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with more YES than NO votes, and YES votes from at least 30% of all voters who cast a ballot.

  • On May 4, 2026, the Special Joint Committee on Initiative Petitions recommended no action, citing unclear building-size rules and no guidance on what counts as adequate infrastructure. source: sec.state.ma.us

Why supporters want it

  • Official argument by Wesley Lima, Legalize Starter Homes: young adults struggle to buy homes and costs have about doubled since 2014; seniors cannot find places to downsize; large-lot rules raise prices; smaller lots where there is infrastructure would mean more affordable homes. source: sec.state.ma.us

Why opponents object

  • Official argument by Adam Chapdelaine, Massachusetts Municipal Association: the state needs more housing but a statewide mandate is the wrong way; it overrides local planning regardless of neighborhood, infrastructure, or environment; one rule ignores the needs of 351 cities and towns. source: sec.state.ma.us

Question 7: Law Proposed by Initiative Petition. Single Family Homes

Do you approve of a law summarized below, on which no vote was taken by the Senate or the House of Representatives before May 6, 2026?
YES
NO

Official summary by the Attorney General (tap to open)
This proposed law would allow single-family homes to be built in a residentially zoned area as long as the land on which it is to be constructed is at least 5,000 square feet, has at least 50 feet of land bordering the street, road, or public way that it faces, and has access to public sewer and water services.
The proposed law would allow cities and towns to reasonably regulate certain aspects of those single-family homes, including their height, distance from neighboring buildings, open space, parking requirements, and whether they can be rented out on a short-term basis. The proposed law would also allow the Executive Office of Housing and Livable Communities to issue guidance or regulations to administer the proposed law.
(sec.state.ma.us ↗)
