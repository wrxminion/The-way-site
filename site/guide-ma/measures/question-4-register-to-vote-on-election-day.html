Question 4Register to vote on Election Day

Statewide ballot question: Law proposed by initiative petition: Election Day registration, placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Law proposed by initiative petition: Election Day registration). Passes with more YES than NO votes, and YES votes from at least 30% of all voters who cast a ballot.

Read the full text of the measure: Information for Voters page for this question ↗

In plain words

Lets eligible people register to vote, or update their address, at their polling place on Election Day. (sec.state.ma.us ↗)

Before and after

TodayIf YES wins
What the rule isVoters must register at least 10 days before an election.People could register or update their address at the polls on Election Day. They would show proof of where they live (photo ID or a document such as a utility bill, lease, bank statement, or paycheck) and sign an oath. Lying is a felony (up to 5 years and $10,000). Without proof, a person casts a provisional ballot that counts only if they return with proof in time. Party could not be changed on Election Day. Starts January 1, 2028.

Example

Illustration only: a renter moves across town in October and misses the deadline. Under a YES result (starting 2028), the renter could register at the new polling place with a lease and vote that day. (sec.state.ma.us ↗)

Biggest change

  • Registration or address update at the polls on Election Day source: sec.state.ma.us
  • Proof of residence and signed oath required source: sec.state.ma.us
  • False statements a felony: up to 5 years and $10,000 source: sec.state.ma.us
  • Provisional ballot if no proof source: sec.state.ma.us
  • No party change on Election Day source: sec.state.ma.us
  • Effective January 1, 2028 source: sec.state.ma.us

Who it affects most: People who move often, such as renters and students; new voters; local election workers.

Cost to the state: The Executive Office of Administration and Finance says the law has no clear material cost to state or local budgets.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with more YES than NO votes, and YES votes from at least 30% of all voters who cast a ballot.

  • On May 4, 2026, a majority of the Special Joint Committee on Initiative Petitions recommended no action, citing staffing and technology needs. source: sec.state.ma.us

Why supporters want it

  • Official argument by Norma B. Shulman, YES on Election Day Registration: makes elections more representative, secure, and modern; matches other New England states; helps people who move often; requires proof of residence and an oath with felony penalties; seeks "maximum voter access with rigorous security safeguards." source: sec.state.ma.us

Why opponents object

  • Official argument by Amy Carnevale, Massachusetts Republican Party: removes the advance period to check identity, residence, and duplicate registrations; checks would happen at the polls under time pressure; errors may be found only after ballots are mixed in; "Election security depends upon meaningful verification before voting." source: sec.state.ma.us

Who backs each side

  • YES on Election Day Registration (Norma B. Shulman) source: sec.state.ma.us

Question 4: Law Proposed by Initiative Petition. Election Day Registration

Do you approve of a law summarized below, on which no vote was taken by the Senate or the House of Representatives before May 6, 2026?
YES
NO

Official summary by the Attorney General (tap to open)
This proposed law would permit eligible individuals to register to vote or update their voter registration address on Election Day.
An individual who is eligible to vote could register to vote on Election Day by going to the polling place in the precinct where they live during voting hours and presenting proof of residency and signing a written oath. Proof of residency could be a valid photo identification, or documentation showing the individual's name and the address where the individual resides, such as a current utility bill, bank statement, government check, residential lease, wireless telephone statement, paycheck, current student fee statement or other document from a post-secondary school, or another government document or correspondence. The written oath would require the individual to certify that they are a citizen of the United States, are at least 18 years old, are not legally prohibited from voting, and have not and will not vote in the same election at another location. The oath would require the individual to acknowledge that providing false information is a felony punishable by not more than 5 years imprisonment or a fine of not more than $10,000, or both.
If an individual did not present proof of residency, they would be allowed to cast a provisional ballot, which would be counted only if the individual returned to provide the required information before the close of polls for a municipal election; within two days after a state primary; or within six days after a state election.
Individuals who register to vote on Election Day would be registered to vote in future elections as well as in the election taking place that day.
Individuals who are already registered to vote would not be able to change their political party affiliation on Election Day.
The proposed law would take effect on January 1, 2028.
(sec.state.ma.us ↗)
