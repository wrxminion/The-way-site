Proposition A: 2025 congressional map referendumReferendum on the 2025 congressional map (House Bill 1)

Citizen referendum (on a law passed by the Legislature): Referendum 2026-R004, placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Referendum petition 2026-R004 on House Bill No. 1 (2025 Second Extraordinary Session)). Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Secretary of State 2026 ballot measures page ↗

In plain words

In 2025 lawmakers passed a new map of Missouri's 8 U.S. House districts (House Bill 1). It splits Kansas City among three districts and makes the Kansas City-based 5th District lean Republican. Citizens gathered signatures to force a public vote, and courts put the new map on hold until voters decide. A yes vote approves the 2025 map; a no vote keeps the 2022 map. (kcur.org ↗)

Biggest change

  • A yes vote approves the 2025 map (House Bill 1). A no vote keeps the 2022 boundaries. source: sos.mo.gov (Missouri Secretary of State)
  • Under the 2025 map, the 5th District would stretch from Troost Avenue in Kansas City nearly 200 miles east, mixing city neighborhoods with rural areas. source: kcur.org

Who it affects most: Voters in the Kansas City area, whose U.S. House district would change most under the 2025 map.

Cost to the state: State and local governments estimate no costs or savings. No effect on taxes.

What your vote means

YES

The 2025 congressional map is approved and used in future elections, splitting Kansas City among three districts.

NO

The 2025 map is rejected and the 2022 congressional map stays in place.

Our one-line summary. Missouri does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Republican lawmakers passed the map in a 2025 special session at President Donald Trump's urging. People Not Politicians turned in about 305,000 signatures in December 2025 to force a referendum; Secretary of State Denny Hoskins rejected the petition. source: kcur.org
  • The 2025 map was used in the Aug. 4, 2026 primary. On Sept. 3, 2026 the Missouri Supreme Court ruled redistricting laws can be put to a referendum and that the map cannot take effect until voters decide. source: kcur.org
  • On Sept. 25, 2026 the U.S. Supreme Court refused to let Missouri use the 2025 map, saying "At this late date, ordering reversion to the 2025 map in the midst of an ongoing election would usher in electoral chaos." source: scrippsnews.com

Conflicts with another measure

  • KCTV5 reports that if Amendment 6 also passes, changing the map voters choose here would take 80% of both chambers of the Legislature. source: kctv5.com

Why supporters want it

  • Gov. Mike Kehoe supports it, calling it "the map that represents what a constitutional congressional map should look like the best." source: kctv5.com
  • U.S. Rep. Mark Alford: "I support the Missouri First map." The Missouri Republican State Committee passed a resolution backing a yes vote. source: kcur.org
  • The official ballot title says the new boundaries "keep more counties intact." source: sos.mo.gov (Missouri Secretary of State)

Why opponents object

  • People Not Politicians, which ran the referendum petition, is urging voters who oppose the map to vote no. Director Richard von Glahn says the heavy spending by the map's supporters suggests they expect voters to reject it. source: kcur.org
  • Von Glahn on the court rulings: "The law is the law, the Missouri constitution is clear as was the Missouri Supreme Court." source: ajc.com

Who backs each side

  • For (yes): Put Missouri First, the committee formed to fight the referendum, had spent $2.9 million overall (mostly on the petition fight and court cases) and reported less than $20,000 in donations since June 30, 2026; the Missouri Republican Party. source: kcur.org
  • Against (no): People Not Politicians, which had bought almost $800,000 in TV ads through Election Day. source: kcur.org
