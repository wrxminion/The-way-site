Question · every Maryland voter

Question 1: Arbitration for state workers' contractsState budget must pay for state worker contracts, including ones set by an arbitrator

Legislative referral (constitutional amendment): Chapter 155 of 2026 (House Bill 604), placed on the ballot by the Legislature as House Bill 604 (Chapter 155 of 2026) ↗. Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: State Board of Elections file (PDF) ↗

In plain words

Many state workers have unions that bargain with the state over pay and benefits. Today, if talks get stuck, an outside "fact finder" only makes suggestions, and the governor decides whether the budget pays for the deal. This amendment makes the governor's budget include the money for each signed or arbitrated contract. A law passed with it adds binding arbitration: a neutral person (an "arbitrator") picks one side's final offer, and both sides must accept it. Changes that need new money or a new law still need the General Assembly's approval. (mgaleg.maryland.gov ↗)

Example

Illustration only: the state's budget analysts assumed an arbitrator costs $600 an hour. A 40-hour case would cost $24,000, and the bill splits that fee equally: $12,000 from the state and $12,000 from the union. (mgaleg.maryland.gov ↗)

Biggest change

  • The governor's budget must include the money needed to carry out the terms of each state worker contract, whether agreed to or set through arbitration. source: elections.maryland.gov
  • Each budget must also show changes in pay, hours and benefits under those contracts for the next year. source: elections.maryland.gov
  • A state law passed with the amendment (and that only takes effect if voters approve it) adds a neutral arbitrator to contract talks and binding arbitration if talks reach an impasse, with a final award due by December 15. source: mgaleg.maryland.gov

Who it affects most: State employees covered by union contracts (outside public colleges for the new arbitration steps), and the state budget that pays them.

Cost to the state: The Department of Legislative Services estimates at least $150,000 a year more starting in fiscal 2028 for arbitration and staff. Costs for pay and benefits could be "significant" starting in fiscal 2029, depending on what arbitrators award; the exact amount is unknown.

What your vote means

YES

The governor's budget must fund state worker contracts, and stuck contract talks go to binding arbitration.

NO

The governor keeps deciding whether to fund state worker contracts, and stuck talks still end with only a fact finder's suggestions.

Our one-line summary. Maryland does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

The Legislature put this on the ballot. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • Maryland state workers can bargain over pay but have no binding arbitration (except BWI airport firefighters and MTA transit workers). Today a fact finder's recommendations do not bind the governor or the General Assembly. source: mgaleg.maryland.gov
  • The General Assembly passed the bill in April 2026 largely along party lines (House 93-35, Senate 35-11). Gov. Wes Moore signed it on April 28, 2026. Sponsors were Del. Jared Solomon and Sen. Cory McCray, both Democrats. source: mgaleg.maryland.gov

Why supporters want it

  • Donna S. Edwards, president of the Maryland State and DC AFL-CIO: "Binding arbitration is mutually beneficial to our state government, our state employees, and taxpayers." source: news.ballotpedia.org

Why opponents object

  • The University System of Maryland opposed the bill. Sherri Roxas, its senior director of labor relations, warned of "serious fiscal consequences for the USM, particularly its smaller institutions" and said giving one outside person the final say conflicts with collective bargaining. source: news.ballotpedia.org

Who backs each side

  • For: the Democratic majority in the General Assembly that passed it, and the Maryland State and DC AFL-CIO. Against: the University System of Maryland (during the legislative debate). No public record found of a campaign committee or money for either side. source: news.ballotpedia.org

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the State Board of Elections' official Kent County ballot (PDF) ↗.

Question 1
Constitutional Amendment
(Ch. 155 of the 2026 Legislative Session)
Arbitration Reform for State Employees Act of 2026

Providing that each budget submitted by the Governor shall include proposed expenditures necessary to implement wages, hours, fringe benefits, health benefits, and other terms and conditions of employment in each collective bargaining memorandum of understanding with State employees, whether the State and State employees reached those terms and conditions by mutual agreement or through a neutral decider's order.

A vote FOR this amendment means that the Governor has to include money in the proposed State budget to pay for agreements between the State and its employees. Agreements might include changes to wages, hours, fringe benefits, health benefits, and other terms and conditions of employment. The Governor must put enough money in the State budget to cover the agreed upon pay and benefits for employees, whether those terms were negotiated or decided by a neutral decision-maker.

A vote AGAINST the amendment means that the law about funding agreements between the State and State employees stays the same.

(Amending Article III of the Maryland Constitution)
