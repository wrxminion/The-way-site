Judges on the ballot

In Maryland, judges of the two top courts are appointed by the governor, approved by the state Senate, and then face a yes-or-no vote on whether to keep them ("continuance in office") for a 10-year term. No one runs against them. Circuit court judges run in contested elections on each county's page.

Judges on your ballot

  • Supreme Court of Maryland, Appellate Circuit 4
    Peter Killough source: elections.maryland.gov source: Governor's press release, July 25, 2024    On the ballot in Appellate Circuit 4 only. Gov. Wes Moore announced his appointment to the Supreme Court of Maryland on July 25, 2024. Before that he was an associate judge on the Prince George's County Circuit Court from 2018. The governor's release lists nearly 10 years in the Maryland Attorney General's office (insurance consumer counsel, then director of the Medicaid Fraud Unit), earlier work as a senior attorney at Ford Motor Company and a corporate lawyer at Carter, Ledyard and Milburn, three years of active Army duty, a B.A. from New York University and a J.D. from the University of Virginia. Voters decide yes or no.
  • Appellate Court of Maryland, at large
    Dan Friedman source: elections.maryland.gov source: Maryland Manual (State Archives)    On the ballot in every county and Baltimore City. The Maryland Manual lists him as an at-large judge of the Appellate Court of Maryland since September 23, 2014 (the court was called the Court of Special Appeals until December 14, 2022). Earlier: assistant attorney general and counsel to the General Assembly, 2008 to 2014, a litigator in private practice, and clerk to two Maryland judges. He has been an adjunct professor at the University of Maryland School of Law since 1998. Voters decide yes or no.

Both judges come from the State Board of Elections' official candidate list ↗. In a continuance vote you vote Yes or No on keeping the judge. Background is from the governor's release and the Maryland Manual linked on each entry; we have not found a bar association rating or a published campaign for or against either judge.
