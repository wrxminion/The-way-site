Local measure · Baltimore County

Baltimore County Question ACharter Amendment – Farmland Preservation (Urban-Rural Demarcation Line Vote)

What your vote means

YES

A YES vote requires 6 of the 9 County Council members, not 5, to approve any change to the Urban-Rural Demarcation Line.

NO

A NO vote keeps the rule that a simple majority of 5 council members can approve changes to the line.

Our one-line summary of the official wording.

In plain words

Our summary of the official materials and local reporting.

  • The Urban-Rural Demarcation Line (URDL) separates the county's urban area from its rural area. Created in 1967, it limits development in the rural zone, which covers about two-thirds of the county and about 10% of residents. source: thebanner.com
  • Other planning and zoning decisions would still need only a simple majority. source: elections.maryland.gov
  • The County Council voted 5-2 in April 2025 to send the question to voters; Councilmen Julian Jones and Pat Young voted no. source: thebanner.com
  • The bill as first introduced would have also let the council member for the affected district block a change; that part was removed by amendment and is not on the ballot. source: wypr.org

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

  • Bill 19-25, the Farmland Preservation Act, was introduced March 3, 2025 by Councilmembers Marks, Kach, Ertel, Patoka and Crandell. source: mgaleg.maryland.gov
  • Councilman David Marks (R) was lead sponsor. The charter change goes to voters Nov. 3, 2026. source: wypr.org

Why supporters want it

  • Council Chair Mike Ertel (D): 'It is about our water supply'; the aim is 'to make it just a little bit harder' to move the line. source: thebanner.com
  • Councilman David Marks (R), lead sponsor, hopes the issue will be debated publicly before the vote. source: wypr.org
  • Dozens of residents, including farmers, environmentalists, planners and community leaders, testified in favor at the council. source: thebanner.com

Why opponents object

  • Councilman Julian Jones (D) argued the line was working and the county should 'let sleeping dogs lie.' He also said 'where the people are, they generate the taxes.' source: thebanner.com
  • David Plymyer, a retired Anne Arundel County Attorney, wrote in a Baltimore Brew op-ed that the bill would lock the line into the charter and the council had not studied its effects. His op-ed targeted the earlier version with the district veto. source: baltimorebrew.com
  • Sharonda Huffman, a 2026 council candidate, urged planners to study the line in light of the housing shortage. source: thebanner.com

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

Word for word from the State Board of Elections' official Baltimore ballot: elections.maryland.gov ↗.

Charter Amendment Baltimore County Farmland Preservation
Act (URDL) (Bill 19-25) Currently, the Planning Board changes the Urban-Rural Demarcation Line (URDL), which separates urban areas from rural areas in Baltimore County, largely in response to zoning changes or water/sewer designation changes enacted by the County Council. The Baltimore County Charter requires a simple majority of the County Council to approve changes by the Planning Board to the Urban-Rural Demarcation Line (URDL). This amendment to the Baltimore County Charter would require a vote of six (6) of the nine (9) members of the County Council rather than a simple majority of five (5) votes to approve changes to the Urban-Rural Demarcation Line (URDL) recommended by the Planning Board. All other planning and zoning matters would continue to be approved by a simple majority vote of the County Council. This would amend Section 522.1 of the Baltimore County Charter and add Section 1015 to the Baltimore County Charter. A vote FOR the amendment requires six (6) of the nine (9) members of the County Council to approve changes to the Urban-Rural Demarcation Line (URDL). A vote AGAINST the amendment keeps the current simple majority vote of five (5) members of the County Council on changes to the Urban-Rural Demarcation Line (URDL).
