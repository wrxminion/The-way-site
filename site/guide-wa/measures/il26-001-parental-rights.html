Initiative IL26-001: Parents' rights in public schoolsBring back the 2024 parents' rights law in public schools

Initiative to the Legislature (citizen-proposed law the Legislature did not pass): Initiative Measure No. IL26-001, placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Initiative Measure No. IL26-001). Passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

Read the full text of the measure: Secretary of State's 2026 state Voters' Pamphlet (PDF) ↗

In plain words

In 2024 the Legislature passed Initiative 2081, a list of parents' rights in public schools. In 2025 lawmakers changed that list. This measure undoes the 2025 changes and puts back the 2024 version, with some differences: faster records answers, notice of medical services, wider opt-outs from surveys and lessons, and some 2025 protections removed. (sos.wa.gov (Secretary of State) ↗)

Biggest change

  • Repeals the 2025 amendments to the parents' rights law (RCW 28A.605.005) and restores the list as enacted in 2024 by Initiative 2081. source: snohomishcountywa.gov
  • Replaces the law's non-discrimination language with an assurance about the sincerely held religious beliefs of the child's family. source: snohomishcountywa.gov
  • Supporters say schools would not have to release mental health counseling records when a parent is the subject of a child abuse or neglect investigation. source: sos.wa.gov (Secretary of State)

Who it affects most: Parents and guardians of public school students, students, and school districts that must update their rules.

Cost to the state: No effect on state money. School districts may have small costs to update policies; the amount is unknown. It would take effect December 3, 2026.

What your vote means

YES

The 2025 changes are undone: parents get records in 10 business days, notice of medical services, and wider opt-outs, and some 2025 protections are removed.

NO

The parents' rights law stays as lawmakers changed it in 2025.

Our one-line summary. Washington does not publish an official "what your vote means" statement; the ballot title above is the official description.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes a state law, which lawmakers can later change. It passes with a simple majority of the votes cast on it.

  • The measure began as an initiative to the Legislature. The Legislature's majority Democrats did not take it up, so it goes to voters. It revives a 2024 Let's Go Washington proposal that lawmakers changed in 2025; a judge upheld the 2025 law in a court challenge. source: opb.org

Why supporters want it

  • The official voters' pamphlet argument for, written by teachers Travis Reep and Tony Harris, Alesha Perkins, La Center School District Superintendent Peter Rosenkranz, Amy Sousa and Jess Grant (Democrats for an Informed Approach to Gender): "Deliberately excluding parents treats them as inherently unsafe without an individualized assessment." source: sos.wa.gov (Secretary of State)
  • Their rebuttal says the measure "contains zero mandates placing students in danger". source: sos.wa.gov (Secretary of State)
  • Brian Heywood of Let's Go Washington says the campaign stands for parents being cut out of decisions about their children. source: opb.org

Why opponents object

  • The official voters' pamphlet argument against, written by State Sen. John Lovick (D), Roshelle Cleland (Washington Coalition Against Domestic Violence and Sexual Assault), Justin Gill (Washington State Nurses Association), Karen Crowley (League of Women Voters of Washington), Courtney Normand (Planned Parenthood Alliance Advocates) and school counselor Jaime Fineman-McKinney: it "ends commonsense protections for child abuse victims whose parents are accused of child abuse and rape". source: sos.wa.gov (Secretary of State)
  • The same argument says it "forces schools to knowingly put LGBTQ+ kids in increased danger of violence and homelessness", and the rebuttal says it "is so poorly written it would eliminate key existing parental rights". source: sos.wa.gov (Secretary of State)

Who backs each side

  • For: Let's Go Washington (Brian Heywood), which reported raising over $1.9 million this year as of June 2026 across its campaigns. Against: No Hate in WA State, which had raised $736,000 in 2026 and more than $400,000 in 2025, with large gifts from the National Education Association and its Washington branch, the ACLU, Gender Justice League and SEIU 775. source: opb.org
