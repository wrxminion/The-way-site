Voting in Georgia

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

Many people are registered automatically when they deal with the DMV or certain state agencies. You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
Photo ID required

Without photo ID, vote a provisional ballot and present ID at the county registrar within 3 days after the election. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Anyone can vote by mail

Return deadline: received by 7 p.m. Nov 3 (close of polls).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Oct 12 - Oct 30 (begins 4th Monday before; ends Friday before); 'three weeks including two Saturdays'

After a conviction
Voting rights return after the whole sentence, including parole or probation

People convicted of felonies 'involving moral turpitude' lose the vote until the full sentence, including parole/probation, is complete, then it is restored automatically.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Georgia.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Monday, October 5). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Without an accepted ID, your ballot is provisional. It only counts if you make a second trip to an election office with ID, within a few days and during office hours.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • After a felony conviction: People convicted of felonies 'involving moral turpitude' lose the vote until the full sentence, including parole/probation, is complete, then it is restored automatically.
  • The Sentencing Project estimated in 2024 that 249,699 people in Georgia (3.25% of the voting-eligible population, and 5.22% of Black voting-eligible residents) could not vote because of a felony conviction.Source: The Sentencing Project, Locked Out 2024
  • After Georgia's 2021 election law (SB 202) moved drop boxes indoors to early-voting sites during business hours, the four core metro Atlanta counties went from 107 drop boxes in 2020 to 25 in 2022.Source: NPR / WABE / Georgia Public Broadcasting
  • A 2019 Leadership Conference Education Fund study of polling place closures since 2012 found Georgia had 214 fewer polling places, with some counties closing more than 80% of their sites.Source: Leadership Conference Education Fund, Democracy Diverted
