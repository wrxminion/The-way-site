Voting in Arkansas

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
By mail or in person

You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
Photo ID required

Without photo ID, vote a provisional ballot and show ID to the county clerk or election board by noon the Monday after the election. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Mail voting needs a reason

Excuses: absent from county on Election Day, illness/disability, religious belief or practice; also long-term care residents, military/overseas Return deadline: by mail: received by 7:30 p.m. Nov 3; hand delivery by the Friday before (Oct 30) per NCSL.

Early in-person voting
Yes

Oct 19 - Nov 2 (15 days before through 5 p.m. Monday before); no excuse needed

After a conviction
Voting rights return after the whole sentence, including parole or probation

Rights restored automatically after completing the full sentence including parole/probation (proof of discharge and payment of fines/costs may be needed to register).

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Arkansas.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Monday, October 5). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • There's no online registration. Signing up means a paper form by mail or a trip to an office.
  • Without an accepted ID, your ballot is provisional. It only counts if you make a second trip to an election office with ID, within a few days and during office hours.
  • You need an accepted reason to vote by mail. Excuses: absent from county on Election Day, illness/disability, religious belief or practice; also long-term care residents, military/overseas. Everyone else votes in person.
  • A mail ballot envelope needs a witness or notary signature, an extra step that is harder for people who live alone or are homebound.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • After a felony conviction: Rights restored automatically after completing the full sentence including parole/probation (proof of discharge and payment of fines/costs may be needed to register).
  • The Sentencing Project estimated in 2024 that 84,099 people in Arkansas (3.77% of the voting-eligible population, and 7.6% of Black voting-eligible residents) could not vote because of a felony conviction.Source: The Sentencing Project, Locked Out 2024
