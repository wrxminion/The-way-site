Voting in Colorado

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

Live in Colorado? Find your districts and see what's on your ballot.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

Many people are registered automatically when they deal with the DMV or certain state agencies. You can register and vote on the same day, including Election Day.

ID at the polls
ID asked for

Show one of many photo or non-photo IDs (e.g. utility bill, bank statement); without it, vote a provisional ballot that counts once eligibility is verified. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Every voter gets a ballot by mail

Ballot mailed automatically to every registered voter; no request needed Return deadline: received by 7 p.m. Nov 3 (Election Day).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Voter service and polling centers open Oct 19 - Nov 3 (15 days before)

After a conviction
Voting rights return when you leave prison

Only people serving a felony sentence in prison lose the vote; rights return automatically on release, including on parole.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Colorado.

  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
