Voting in Kansas

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
Photo ID required

Without photo ID, vote a provisional ballot and provide ID to the county election officer before the county canvass. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Anyone can vote by mail

Return deadline: postmarked by Election Day and received by Nov 6 (3 days after) -- under court injunction; SB 4 (2025) Election-Day receipt deadline blocked.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Kansas.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Tuesday, October 13). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • Without an accepted ID, your ballot is provisional. It only counts if you make a second trip to an election office with ID, within a few days and during office hours.
  • After a felony conviction: Rights restored automatically after completing the sentence including parole/probation.
  • A federal appeals court found in 2020 that Kansas's documentary proof-of-citizenship rule had denied registration to 31,089 applicants and upheld an injunction blocking it; the rule is no longer enforced, but it shows how many eligible people a paperwork rule can stop.Source: U.S. Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit, Fish v. Schwab (2020)
