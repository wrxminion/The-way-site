Voting in North Dakota

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
No registration

North Dakota has no voter registration. You show ID with your current address at the polls.

ID at the polls
ID required

Show a ND ID with residential address or tribal ID (supplemental documents allowed); without it, mark a set-aside ballot that counts if you show valid ID before the canvassing board meets. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Anyone can vote by mail

Return deadline: received before close of polls Nov 3 (HB 1165, 2025; postmarks no longer accepted).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Up to 15 days before (Oct 19) through Nov 2; county option

After a conviction
Voting rights return when you leave prison

Only people in prison lose the vote; restored on release.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in North Dakota.

  • Without an accepted ID, your ballot is provisional. It only counts if you make a second trip to an election office with ID, within a few days and during office hours.
  • North Dakota's voter ID law required a residential street address, which many Native Americans on reservations lack; a 2020 settlement made the state responsible for assigning or verifying addresses for tribal voters.Source: Associated Press via MPR News
