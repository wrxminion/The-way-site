Voting in Arizona

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
ID required

Show one photo ID or two non-photo documents with name and address; without them, vote a provisional ballot and show ID by 5 p.m. on the 5th day after a federal general election (2nd business day for other elections). No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Anyone can vote by mail

Return deadline: received by 7 p.m. Nov 3 (Election Day).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Oct 7 - Oct 30 (27 days before through 7 p.m. Friday before)

After a conviction
Some people lose the right to vote for good unless it's restored

First-time felony offenders regain rights automatically after completing their sentence and paying restitution; people with two or more felonies must apply to the court to restore rights.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Arizona.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Monday, October 5). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • Without an accepted ID, your ballot is provisional. It only counts if you make a second trip to an election office with ID, within a few days and during office hours.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • After a felony conviction: First-time felony offenders regain rights automatically after completing their sentence and paying restitution; people with two or more felonies must apply to the court to restore rights.
  • The Sentencing Project estimated in 2024 that 214,791 people in Arizona (4.2% of the voting-eligible population, and 10.54% of Black voting-eligible residents) could not vote because of a felony conviction.Source: The Sentencing Project, Locked Out 2024
  • A 2019 Leadership Conference Education Fund study found Arizona had the most widespread drop in polling places of the states studied (320 fewer), including 171 fewer in Maricopa County since 2012.Source: Leadership Conference Education Fund, Democracy Diverted
  • Arizona requires documentary proof of citizenship to vote in state and local races; in 2024 a records error affected about 218,000 registered voters, whom the Arizona Supreme Court allowed to cast full ballots.Source: Votebeat
