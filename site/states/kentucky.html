Voting in Kentucky

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
Photo ID asked for

Without photo ID, a voter known to the poll worker or showing an alternative document can sign a voter affirmation form stating why they lack ID; otherwise vote provisionally. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Mail voting needs a reason

Excuses: age/disability/illness, absent from county (incl. students, temporary residence), military/overseas, ACP participant, incarcerated but eligible, election officials, pregnancy (3rd trimester), work schedule Return deadline: received by 6 p.m. Nov 3 (close of polls).

Early in-person voting
Yes

No-excuse early voting Oct 29 - Oct 31 (Thu-Sat before); excuse-based in-person absentee earlier

After a conviction
Some people lose the right to vote for good unless it's restored

Felony convictions remove the vote for life unless restored; Gov. Beshear's 2019 executive order automatically restores rights to people with most non-violent felonies after completing their sentence, while others must apply to the governor.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Kentucky.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Monday, October 5). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • You need an accepted reason to vote by mail. Excuses: age/disability/illness, absent from county (incl. students, temporary residence), military/overseas, ACP participant, incarcerated but eligible, election officials, pregnancy (3rd trimester), work schedule. Everyone else votes in person.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • After a felony conviction: Felony convictions remove the vote for life unless restored; Gov. Beshear's 2019 executive order automatically restores rights to people with most non-violent felonies after completing their sentence, while others must apply to the governor.
  • The Sentencing Project estimated in 2024 that 158,381 people in Kentucky (4.66% of the voting-eligible population, and 11.67% of Black voting-eligible residents) could not vote because of a felony conviction.Source: The Sentencing Project, Locked Out 2024
