Voting in District of Columbia

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

Many people are registered automatically when they deal with the DMV or certain state agencies. You can register and vote on the same day, including Election Day.

ID at the polls
No ID for most voters

No ID is generally required at the polls; voters state their name (and often address) and sign the poll book. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Every voter gets a ballot by mail

Ballot mailed automatically to every registered voter; no request needed Return deadline: postmarked by Election Day and received by Nov 13 (10 days after).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Early vote centers Oct 22 - Oct 31 per NCSL rule (12 days before through Saturday before)

After a conviction
You never lose the right to vote, even in prison

DC residents never lose the right to vote for a felony; since 2020 they can vote while incarcerated (absentee ballots are mailed to them).

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in District of Columbia.

  • No extra hurdles beyond the basics stood out in our check. Register early anyway, and confirm your registration before Election Day.
