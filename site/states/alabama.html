Voting in Alabama

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

You must register before the deadline below.

ID at the polls
Photo ID asked for

Without photo ID, vote a regular ballot if two poll officials sign an affidavit identifying you, otherwise a provisional ballot that counts if you show ID by 5 p.m. the Friday after Election Day. No ID? Ask your election office whether it offers a free one for voting. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Mail voting needs a reason

Excuses: absent from county, illness/disability, physically incapacitated (65+ or disabled), living outside county (e.g., student), election officer/poll watcher elsewhere, work shift of 10+ hours during polling hours, caregiver of homebound family member, incarcerated but eligible Return deadline: by mail: received by noon Nov 3 (Election Day); hand delivery by 5 p.m. Nov 2.

Early in-person voting
No

No early in-person voting; excuse-qualified voters may vote absentee in person at the Absentee Election Manager's office until Oct 29

After a conviction
Some people lose the right to vote for good unless it's restored

Only felonies 'involving moral turpitude' (a statutory list of about 46 crimes) remove the vote; most can apply for a Certificate of Eligibility to Register to Vote after finishing their sentence, but some (e.g. murder, rape, treason) require a pardon.

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in Alabama.

  • Registration closes before Election Day (in person: Monday, October 19). Miss it and you can't vote in this election. If you've moved, your registration may need updating by then too.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
  • You need an accepted reason to vote by mail. Excuses: absent from county, illness/disability, physically incapacitated (65+ or disabled), living outside county (e.g., student), election officer/poll watcher elsewhere, work shift of 10+ hours during polling hours, caregiver of homebound family member, incarcerated but eligible. Everyone else votes in person.
  • A mail ballot envelope needs a witness or notary signature, an extra step that is harder for people who live alone or are homebound.
  • Mail ballots have to arrive by Election Day; a postmark isn't enough. A ballot mailed in the last days can arrive too late and won't count, so return it in person if it's close.
  • There's no early in-person voting open to everyone, so most people who vote in person have to do it on a Tuesday, a workday.
  • After a felony conviction: Only felonies 'involving moral turpitude' (a statutory list of about 46 crimes) remove the vote; most can apply for a Certificate of Eligibility to Register to Vote after finishing their sentence, but some (e.g. murder, rape, treason) require a pardon.
  • The Sentencing Project estimated in 2024 that 227,437 people in Alabama (5.95% of the voting-eligible population, and 8.28% of Black voting-eligible residents) could not vote because of a felony conviction.Source: The Sentencing Project, Locked Out 2024
  • In 2015 Alabama announced plans to close 31 part-time driver's license offices, including those in all six counties where Black residents exceed 70% of the population, while the state requires photo ID to vote.Source: Brennan Center for Justice
