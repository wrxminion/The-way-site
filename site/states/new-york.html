Voting in New York

General election: Tuesday, November 3, 2026.

At a glance

Register
Online, by mail or in person

You can register and vote on the same day during early voting, but not on Election Day.

ID at the polls
No ID for most voters

No ID is generally required at the polls; voters state their name (and often address) and sign the poll book. First-time voters who registered by mail may need to show ID under federal law.

Mail voting
Anyone can vote by mail

Return deadline: postmarked by Election Day and received by Nov 10 (7 days after).

Early in-person voting
Yes

Oct 24 - Nov 1

After a conviction
Voting rights return when you leave prison

Only people in prison for a felony lose the vote; restored on release (2021 law).

Where the road gets harder

Every rule is a cost, and it doesn't fall on everyone the same. A deadline, a document or a trip to an office is easy for some people and a real obstacle for others: people who work hourly jobs, move often, live far from an office, don't drive, or have a disability. Here is what that looks like in New York.

  • You can register and vote at the same time during early voting, but not on Election Day itself. Someone who only learns about the election late, or can only get free on a Tuesday, is out of luck.
  • Registration isn't automatic. Each person has to sign up on their own, which leaves out people who don't know the rules or move often.
