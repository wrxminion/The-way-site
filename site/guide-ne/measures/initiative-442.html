Measure 442Puts sex-separate school sports teams in the state constitution

Citizen initiative (constitutional amendment): Initiative Measure 442, placed on the ballot by voters who signed petitions (Fairness for Girls constitutional amendment petition). Passes with a majority of the votes cast on it, and the yes votes must be at least 35% of all votes cast in the election.

Read the full text of the measure: Secretary of State ballot measures pamphlet (PDF) ↗

In plain words

A 2025 state law already requires school and college teams to be labeled male, female or coed, and keeps male students off girls' and women's teams. Measure 442 would write this into the state constitution, so lawmakers could not change it on their own; only another statewide vote could. (newsfromthestates.com ↗)

Biggest change

  • Teams for "females, women, or girls" would "not be open to students of the male sex." There is no matching rule keeping female students off male teams. source: nebraskapublicmedia.org
  • The measure itself does not define "sex"; the 2025 law sponsored by Sen. Kathleen Kauth does. source: newsfromthestates.com

Who it affects most: Student athletes, especially transgender students, and school and college athletic programs.

Cost to the state: Unknown. No cost estimate found.

What your vote means

YES

Keeping male students off girls' and women's school teams would be in the constitution, not just state law.

NO

The rule would stay only in state law, which lawmakers could change.

Why people want it, and why others object

Why this is on the ballot

Voters put this on the ballot by signing a petition. It changes the state constitution, so only another statewide vote can change it back. It passes with a majority of the votes cast on it, and the yes votes must be at least 35% of all votes cast in the election.

  • Organizers turned in about 211,000 signatures in late June 2026; about 126,000 valid ones were needed. source: nebraskapublicmedia.org

Why supporters want it

  • Supporters contend (official pamphlet): Initiative 442, Fairness for Girls, protects fairness, opportunities, and safety for Nebraska girls in sports. It establishes constitutional protections for sex-separated athletics in Nebraska grade schools, high schools, and colleges. Fairness for Girls ensures female athletes are not forced to compete against biological males. Allowing biological males to compete against girls poses safety risks because of physical differences in strength, bone density, and size, increasing the likelihood of injury. The initiative protects female athletes from losing scholarships, medals, or tournament advancement after being displaced in rankings by biological males. Fairness for Girls protects every girl’s opportunity to succeed on a fair playing field. source: sos.nebraska.gov
  • Sen. Kathleen Kauth, who sponsored the 2025 law: "One of the things we always worry about when we pass a law is that it can be unpassed." source: nebraskapublicmedia.org

Why opponents object

  • Opponents contend (official pamphlet): Initiative 442 adds unnecessary limits on participation in girls’ sports into the Nebraska State Constitution. Initiative 442 raises significant concerns about fairness and privacy by making gender verification constitutionally required for girls and young women who want to play sports at their public school, college or university, but would not place a similar requirement on boys and young men. It would also empower bad actors to unfairly cast doubt on young athletes who don’t fit within traditional gender expectations. Proponents say that this measure is about promoting fairness and safety by ensuring transgender girls cannot participate, but it would actually subject all girls to greater barriers and risk of harm and create a constitutional right to bully kids about their appearance. Vote AGAINST 442. source: sos.nebraska.gov
  • OutNebraska, an LGBTQ advocacy group, said the measure is "designed to permanently embed" the policy in the constitution, and that "Transgender students are not a threat to girls' sports." source: nebraskapublicmedia.org

Who backs each side

  • For: Fairness for Girls (led by former Sen. Julie Slama). Restore the Good Life Inc., a conservative PAC, gave at least $3 million; the Nebraska Family Alliance also gave. Fairness for Girls paid $3 million to Vanguard Field Strategies for petition gathering. source: nebraskapublicmedia.org
  • Against: OutNebraska. source: nebraskapublicmedia.org

What you will see on your ballot

Official ballot wording, word for word (tap to open)

The official ballot title, word for word from the Secretary of State's official ballot measures pamphlet ↗.

A vote “FOR” will amend the Nebraska Constitution to provide: (1) Schools must expressly designate each athletic team or sport as one of the following based on biological sex: (a) Males, men, or boys; (b) females, women, or girls; or (c) coed or mixed; and (2) Athletic teams or sports designated for females, women, or girls shall not be open to students of the male sex. The amendment would apply to all athletic teams or sports sponsored by public schools or postsecondary educational institutions, and private schools or educational institutions whose athletic teams or sports compete against public schools or institutions.
A vote “AGAINST” means the Nebraska Constitution will not be amended in this manner.

Shall the Nebraska Constitution be amended to provide: (1) Schools must expressly designate each athletic team or sport as one of the following based on biological sex: (a) Males, men, or boys; (b) females, women, or girls; or (c) coed or mixed; and (2) Athletic teams or sports designated for females, women, or girls shall not be open to students of the male sex?
