Judges on the ballot

In Nebraska, judges are appointed, then voters decide whether to keep them, with a yes or no vote. There are no opponents. The Secretary of State certified 48 judges for the 2026 retention ballot.

Judges on your ballot

The Nebraska Judicial Branch lists these 48 judges as having filed for retention source: NE Judicial Branch retention list. Which ones you see depends on your county: voters see at least two judges and up to 15, and Douglas County has the most. The Nebraska Examiner reports that the Nebraska State Bar Association polled attorneys who appear before the judges and that attorneys recommended retaining all but one of the 48, that no Supreme Court justice is up this year, and that the last time a Nebraska judge was not retained was 2008 source: Nebraska Examiner via WOWT, Sept. 16, 2026. The Examiner does not name the one judge in this summary, so we do not either.

County, district and local judges

Coverage. Nebraska judges are appointed by the governor and then face a yes or no retention vote, with no opponents. This section groups the same 46 trial and appeals judges from the Nebraska Judicial Branch's 2026 retention list by judicial district, using the district and court shown on each judge's Judicial Branch profile, read November 4, 2026. It covers every district, county, separate juvenile and Court of Appeals judge on that list. Two Workers' Compensation Court judges (Dirk V. Block, Thomas E. Stine) are also on the list and not grouped here. Not covered: Judicial District 7 has no judge on the list; judges not up for retention in 2026 are not shown. Which names you see depends on where you live.
