Judges up for retention

Arizona voters decide yes or no on whether to retain judges. The Secretary of State publicity pamphlet lists 82 judges and justices on the retention ballot: one Arizona Supreme Court justice, 14 Court of Appeals judges and Superior Court judges in Coconino, Pinal, Pima and Maricopa counties. The question on the official unofficial-results page reads, for example, "Shall Judge Michael Catlett, Judge of the Court of Appeals Division I, be retained in office?" The pamphlet prints "meets judicial standards" for every one. Updated November 4, 2026 against the official county and state results pages; see the coverage note below for what is confirmed and what is not.

  • The Judicial Performance Review Commission considers and votes on whether a judge "MEETS" or "DOES NOT MEET" Judicial Performance Standards. The pamphlet prints "MEETS JUDICIAL STANDARDS" for every judge below and lists no one under "DO NOT MEET". source: apps.azsos.gov (Arizona Secretary of State)
Checked November 4, 2026. We read the unofficial results pages of the Arizona Secretary of State (statewide retention), Maricopa County, Pima County and Pinal County. Results are unofficial until canvassed. Confirmed there: the Supreme Court justice and all 14 Court of Appeals judges, all Pima Superior Court names, and the Maricopa and Pinal names noted below. Not confirmed: Coconino County Superior Court (we did not reach its results page). Two pamphlet names did not appear on county results pages and are marked on their entries. source: AZ SOS unofficial results

Judges on your ballot

The Commission on Judicial Performance Review voted on April 24, 2026. Its news release says it evaluated 84 judges from across the state plus one Arizona Supreme Court justice, using more than 10,000 surveys, written comments and a public hearing, and that all judges eligible for retention met the performance standards source: Commission news release, April 24, 2026. The pamphlet list on this page has 82 names; the release does not say why its count is higher. Per-judge reports are on azjudges.info ↗.
