Judges on the ballot

In Illinois, judges on the retention ballot get a yes or no vote with no opponent. This page lists the judges we could confirm for Cook County and three Appellate Court seats. It is not a full list for the state.

INCOMPLETE. This page covers only what we could confirm from an official list: the Cook County Clerk's candidate list for Cook County, plus three Illinois Appellate Court retention seats (the 1st District seat is on the Clerk's list; the 4th and 5th District seats are from Ballotpedia, not from an official list). We have not checked the other 101 counties' circuit court retention and vacancy races or any Illinois Supreme Court seat. Your county clerk's sample ballot is the final word.

How retention works

  • Yes or no, no opponent. A judge on the retention ballot is kept if enough voters say yes. Illinois Policy reports a judge needs a yes from 60 percent of voters. source: Illinois Policy, Cook retention

County, district and local judges

Coverage. This section covers Cook County only (the Circuit Court of Cook County), as of November 4, 2026. It combines (1) the 59 retention judges listed above, from the Cook County Clerk's candidate list, and (2) the 28 partisan vacancy races for Circuit Court seats, five countywide and the rest in subcircuits, from Ballotpedia, which the Clerk's list backs up for each seat we checked: a Democratic nominee is the only name on it. Not covered: the other 101 counties. The State Board of Elections lists downstate circuit and subcircuit vacancies as well (circuits 2, 3, 4, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, 17 and 18 appear on its July 2025 list), plus retention judges in each circuit. We could not open the Board's candidate listing, so we have no names for those.

Illinois circuit judges are elected in partisan races to fill a vacancy. After that they face a yes or no retention vote and need three-fifths yes to stay. Every race below shows one candidate (a Democrat) in the sources we read.
