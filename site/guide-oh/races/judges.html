Judges on the ballot

Ohio votes on two Ohio Supreme Court seats this year. Both are contested and carry party labels. For the term starting January 1, 2027: Republican Daniel Hawkins and Democrat Marilyn Zayas. For the term starting January 2, 2027: Democrat Jennifer Brunner and Republican Colleen O'Donnell. Below that, we list county and district judges for four counties.

The Ohio Secretary of State's own site returned an error to us, so we checked the Supreme Court pairings against four county boards of elections (Cuyahoga, Franklin, Hamilton and Lake). All four list the same four names and the same two start dates. Cuyahoga's list is dated October 15, 2026 and Franklin's was revised October 16, 2026. An earlier version of this page had the two start dates swapped; that is fixed. Check your sample ballot.

Ohio Supreme Court

  • Term starting 1-1-2027: Daniel R. Hawkins (Republican) and Marilyn Zayas (Democrat) source: wysu.org (unreviewed)All four county lists show these two candidates for this seat. Reporting says Hawkins is the incumbent justice and Zayas is a judge on the First District Court of Appeals. That detail is not independently reviewed.
  • Term starting 1-2-2027: Jennifer Brunner (Democrat) and Colleen O’Donnell (Republican) source: wysu.org (unreviewed)All four county lists show these two candidates for this seat. Reporting calls Brunner the incumbent and the only Democrat on the seven-member court, and calls O’Donnell a former Franklin County Common Pleas judge and former federal immigration judge. That reporting is not independently reviewed.

County, district and local judges

Coverage: this section covers judges of the Court of Appeals, Court of Common Pleas, and related common pleas divisions in four counties: Cuyahoga, Franklin, Hamilton and Lake. Each comes from that county board of elections candidate list as of the date noted. It does NOT cover the other 84 counties, municipal courts, county courts, or the Courts of Appeals districts outside Cuyahoga (8th), Franklin (10th), Hamilton (1st) and Lake (11th, partly). We did not reach the Secretary of State list or any other county.

How Ohio fills these seats: judges of the Courts of Appeals and Common Pleas are elected, with six-year terms. A governor appointment can fill a vacancy until the next election. All seats below are contested elections or unopposed seats, not retention votes. Where only one candidate is listed, that candidate is unopposed on that list.

Party labels: the Cuyahoga list marks Common Pleas candidates Nonpartisan. The Hamilton list shows a party in its party column for the same kind of seat. We show the party only where a county list prints it, and do not know whether it appears on the ballot. Check your sample ballot.
